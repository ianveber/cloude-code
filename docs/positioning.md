# Agency Positioning

## The thesis in one sentence

Service businesses in high-stakes verticals have repeatable, expensive workflows and no engineering capacity — we replace those workflows with agent systems built specifically for their industry, delivered as a complete operating layer.

---

## What we are

**An AI-native vertical agent agency.**

- We ship complete, production-ready agent systems
- Scoped to a specific functional cluster inside a specific vertical
- Delivered with integrations, documentation, and an operating handoff
- Priced on the value of the cluster replaced, not hours spent

We are practitioners. We know the workflows we're automating before we talk to the client. When we walk into a specialty dental group, we already know their new-patient intake process, their insurance pre-auth bottleneck, their treatment plan follow-up leak, and their recall dropout rate. We don't do discovery to learn the industry. We do discovery to learn their specific instance of it.

---

## What we are not

**We are not an AI consulting firm.**
We don't sell strategy, roadmaps, education, or "AI readiness assessments." We build things that run.

**We are not a software company.**
We don't sell seats, licenses, or monthly subscriptions to a platform. Clients own what we build for them. The revenue model is build fees plus an optional retainer for operation, improvement, and expansion.

**We are not a digital marketing agency.**
We do not run paid ads. We do not manage social accounts. We do not write blog posts as a service. The Acquirer Agent handles our own organic distribution — it is not a service we sell.

**We are not a staffing layer.**
We don't replace staff by giving clients access to Claude. We replace workflows by building systems that handle specific functional clusters end-to-end. The distinction matters: a staffing layer creates dependence; a workflow system creates leverage.

**We are not horizontal.**
"AI for any business" is not a position. We are vertical-specific by design. Depth in a vertical compounds: every engagement makes the next playbook sharper, the discovery faster, the scoping more accurate.

## The pitch in plain language

> "Your practice runs on about a dozen high-stakes workflows that repeat every day. Right now, those workflows run on a combination of your staff's time, a handful of software tools that don't talk to each other, and manual coordination that falls through the cracks. We replace a specific cluster of those workflows — intake, scheduling, follow-up, whatever the highest-leverage cluster is for your practice — with a system that runs automatically, surfaces exceptions to your team, and improves over time. We build it in 6–10 weeks, integrate it with what you already use, and hand it to you with documentation so your team can operate and modify it. You own it. You don't pay us monthly to keep the lights on unless you want us to keep improving it."

Adjust the cluster, the timeline, and the vertical language by client. The structure is the same.

## Differentiation proof points (as we build them)

These need to be earned, not claimed. Track them as we complete engagements:

- Number of completed agent systems by vertical
- Measured workflow metrics before/after (time saved, error rate, revenue recovered)
- Named client references willing to speak
- Published case studies (sanitized where needed)
- Vertical-specific content demonstrating practitioner depth

Until we have real case studies, the differentiation is in how we sell: discovery conversations where we describe their workflow to them before they explain it, and proposals that name the specific agents, integrations, and handoff protocol before they sign anything.

## Market validation

This is not a speculative bet on where AI is going. The category of vertical AI agents is being independently validated by the highest-signal source in the ecosystem: YC.

**YC Bet #02 (Summer 2025):** "Vertical AI Agents Over Horizontal Tools." YC explicitly names vertical agents — systems that replace entire functional workflows inside specific industries — as the highest-conviction investment category. Named examples: Taiga (AI medical billing, healthcare vertical) and Andco (agentic legal case workups, legal vertical). Both are direct analogues to what Veta builds.

**Scale benchmark:** AI-native companies are hitting $100M ARR with 30 employees. The unit economics work because the system does the work, not the headcount. This is the structural advantage of building agent systems rather than services.

**Adoption gap:** 88% of organizations are experimenting with AI. 81% report no measurable bottom-line gains from that experimentation. The gap between "we're experimenting" and "we have a running system" is the exact problem Veta solves. 88% of YC's Summer 2025 batch is AI-native — the infrastructure bets are settled; the application layer is where value is being built now.

**Ecosystem signal:** Anthropic is the #1 API choice among YC founders building in this space. The tooling is mature. The constraint is vertical knowledge and operational delivery — not access to capable models.

**What this means for positioning:** The question practitioners ask is no longer "should we look at AI?" — it's "who can actually build something that works in our specific workflow?" That's the positioning gap Veta occupies. Not AI strategy. Not generic AI tools. Complete, operational agent systems built by someone who already knows the vertical.
