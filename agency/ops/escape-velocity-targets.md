## The escape-velocity thesis

A vertical agent agency reaches "escape velocity" when:

- Revenue per cofounder approaches €500K annualized
- Operate retainer revenue exceeds operating cost (true recurring profitability)
- Acquisition motion is self-sustaining (Acquirer Agent + dog-food case study + partnerships drive enough qualified pipeline that cofounders don't have to manually prospect)
- New engagements can be onboarded without disrupting existing operate-phase work
- First non-cofounder hire is supported by margins (orchestrator hire pays back inside 12 months)

At escape velocity, the agency compounds. Below it, cofounders are doing artisanal work that doesn't scale.

We target escape velocity by Q4 2027. Everything before that is the runway.

## Hiring milestones

When to hire (in priority order):

### Hire 1 — Orchestrator (Q2 2027)
**Trigger:** cofounder utilization sustained >50% delivery, operate-phase engagements ≥4
**Role:** manage 4–8 operate-phase engagements at low touch (~4 hours/week each); handle weekly digests, monthly reviews, escalation triage; free cofounder time for build + strategy + acquisition
**Profile:** senior project manager or junior consultant; AI-literate but not technical
**Compensation:** salary + small equity (if equity-eligible per founders' agreement)
**Expected impact:** frees ~16 cofounder hours/week; supports 6 additional active engagements

### Hire 2 — Second orchestrator (Q1 2028)
**Trigger:** operate-phase engagement count ≥10; first orchestrator at capacity
**Role:** mirror of first orchestrator
**Profile:** similar to first
**Expected impact:** frees another ~16 cofounder hours/week; supports another 6 active engagements

### Hire 3 — Operations / admin support (mid-2028)
**Trigger:** cofounder time on admin (legal, finance, ops) >10% sustained
**Role:** invoicing, vendor management, basic legal coordination, scheduling, miscellaneous ops
**Profile:** experienced operations specialist
**Expected impact:** frees ~5 cofounder hours/week each; clears admin debt

### Hire 4 — Senior engineer (2028–2029)
**Trigger:** Builder workflow needs deeper engineering; Ian over-capacity on technical work
**Role:** Builder workflow improvements, integration infrastructure, multi-model abstraction
**Profile:** senior AI/ML engineer
**Expected impact:** scales technical layer beyond Ian-bottleneck

### Hire 5+ — Cofounder addition (late 2028 or 2029, depending on growth trajectory)
**Trigger:** revenue >€3M annualized AND strategic gap that none of the three founders fills well AND mutual cofounder consensus
**Role:** specific to gap (could be growth/sales, could be vertical expansion, could be technical depth)
**Compensation:** equity-based (per founders' agreement provisions for new cofounders)
**Expected impact:** strategic; not just operational

### What we don't hire

- Junior employees (we don't have onboarding capacity for juniors at this stage)
- Sales reps as separate function (cofounders lead sales; acquisition system supports)
- Marketing managers (Acquirer Agent + cofounder time covers)
- Account managers (orchestrator role subsumes)

## Pricing review schedule

Annual pricing review during Q1 strategic offsite. At each review:

- Per-vertical actual margins vs target margins
- Per-vertical win rates and pricing pressure observations
- Inflation adjustment (modest, ~3–5% annually if applicable)
- Tier band adjustments based on accumulated engagement experience
- Imputed cofounder rate review

Outside of annual review, no pricing changes without all-cofounder consensus and decisions-log entry.

## Vertical expansion decisions

Each year, during strategic offsite:

- Review current vertical performance
- Decide: deepen existing verticals or add new?
- If adding: which vertical (per `verticals/_selection-framework.md`)?
- If deepening: which sub-segments or features to invest in?

Realistic vertical count:
- 2026: 3 verticals (current)
- 2027: 3 verticals (deepen, don't add)
- 2028: 4 verticals (add one)
- 2029+: 4–6 verticals (selective additions)

Beyond 6 verticals, the operating model strains. Each vertical needs cofounder fluency + Acquirer Agent attention + partner network. Vertical sprawl is a known failure mode.

## When the trajectory diverges

If actual progress diverges materially from these targets:

### Below trajectory (slower than planned)
- Diagnose: which dimension is slow? Acquisition? Capacity? Quality?
- Strategic adjustment options: pause hiring, slow pipeline, narrow vertical focus, raise prices
- Annual offsite re-baselines targets for following year
- Don't panic — escape velocity is 6 quarters from formation; slippage by 1–2 quarters is recoverable

### Above trajectory (faster than planned)
- Diagnose: is this sustainable, or are we taking on margin-eroding engagements?
- Strategic adjustment options: accelerate hiring (carefully), tighten qualification, raise prices
- Annual offsite re-baselines targets upward if sustainable
- Don't panic — extra revenue is a manageable problem

### Far above trajectory (>2× expected)
- Strong signal of product-market fit
- Strategic discussion: is this the right time to raise external capital? (default: no — agency model doesn't usually justify capital, but worth examining)
- Discipline: don't dilute quality to absorb growth

### Far below trajectory (<50% expected)
- Strong signal something structural is wrong
- All-cofounder reset: is the thesis right? Is the vertical mix right? Is the cofounder team right?
- Pricing review out of cycle
- Possibly: pivot to a different model or wind down deliberately rather than persist with bad fit

## What we won't do regardless of trajectory

- Take on engagements that violate our principles (per `docs/principles.md`)
- Compromise margin to chase revenue
- Add verticals that fail the selection framework
- Hire to fill capacity gaps rather than to add specific capability

The trajectory is aspirational. The principles are non-negotiable.
