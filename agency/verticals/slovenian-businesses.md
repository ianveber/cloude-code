# Slovenian Businesses — Geography Moat

AIS deploys agent stacks for Slovenian businesses where language, local trust networks, and owner-operator concentration give us a structural advantage no international competitor can credibly attack.

---

## Why we picked this vertical

**Function-bounded:** Variable — depends on which function we own per engagement. We constrain scope per engagement to bounded functions (ops automation, intake, retention) inside a Slovenian SMB context. Generic "Slovenian business marketing" fails the bound test; specific functions inside specific Slovenian SMBs pass.

**Buyer-authoritative:** Slovenian SMBs are overwhelmingly owner-operated. The owner signs everything. Decision cycles are days-to-weeks, not months. ~80% of EU-25 Slovenian businesses have a single ultimate decision-maker reachable in one meeting.

**Work-encoded:** True for most ops, intake, content, and reporting functions we'd deploy against. Embodied parts (in-person service, on-site work) stay with the client team — we only own the encoded layer.

**Margin-viable:** Slovenian SMB price ceiling is lower than international (~€18K–€50K build vs €40K–€100K international), but cofounder time per engagement also lower (cofounders speak the language, know the market, decisions happen faster). Net contribution margin matches or exceeds international engagements when sized correctly.

The bigger structural pass: language and local trust networks compound. Two well-executed engagements in any Slovenian sub-sector produce inbound from the rest of the sub-sector within 6 months. International competitors can't replicate this because they don't speak the language or sit in the trust networks.

## ICP signals

### Firmographic

- **Location:** Slovenia primarily. Slovenian-speaking Carinthia (Austria) and Slovenian-Italian border region acceptable.
- **Revenue band:** €1M–€20M annual revenue. Sweet spot €2M–€10M.
- **Employee count:** 5–80 employees. Sweet spot 10–50.
- **Ownership:** Privately-held. Owner-operated or close-family-operated. No private equity (PE-owned Slovenian SMBs introduce committee dynamics that break the model).
- **Maturity:** 5+ years operating. We avoid pre-PMF businesses — they can't pay the price band and they pivot too fast for engagements to land.
- **Sub-sectors of interest** (not exhaustive — we go where the inbound shows):
  - Aesthetic medicine and dental clinics (high revenue per client, function-bounded acquisition + retention)
  - Accounting and bookkeeping firms (function-bounded ops + reporting)
  - Real estate agencies (acquisition + ops + transaction admin)
  - Construction and contractors (intake + scheduling + estimate generation)
  - Hospitality and spa businesses (acquisition + retention)
  - B2B services firms (consulting, engineering, marketing agencies under €5M)
  - Specialty manufacturing (intake, quote-to-cash automation)

### Role

The buyer is the owner-operator (lastnik), generally between 35 and 60, in the business 10+ years. Often the founder, sometimes the second-generation family member. Signs €25K decisions without consultation. Reads contracts personally, sometimes with a personal accountant (računovodja) review.

Operates the business hands-on. Knows the names of all employees. Often does sales personally. Is the primary or only decision-maker on technology investments. Has been pitched "AI consulting" before — usually by Slovenian marketing freelancers or international SaaS vendors — and is either skeptical (because nothing shipped) or curious-but-cautious (because they want it but don't trust the providers).

### Function-shape

Varies by engagement. The unifying pattern: the bought function is currently held together by the owner-operator's time, plus 1–3 long-tenured employees, plus a patchwork of tools (often including Excel, paper, and personal email).

Typical bottlenecks we see:
- Owner is the single bottleneck on customer-facing decisions
- Long-tenured employees own institutional knowledge that's never been documented
- Tools are partially integrated at best — most data lives in spreadsheets or email
- Reporting happens in the owner's head, monthly, on a weekend

## Pain map

Five common pains across Slovenian SMB engagements (the specific 2–3 per engagement depend on which function we own):

### Pain 1 — Owner-operator is the bottleneck on every customer touch

The owner is on every important call, every new-client conversation, every quote. Capacity caps growth. They can't take a vacation. They can't scale beyond what they personally service. Most have hit the ceiling and are looking for a way through.

### Pain 2 — Institutional knowledge lives in 2 people's heads

A long-tenured employee or two carry the operational know-how. The owner relies on them. The business has no documented SOPs. If that employee leaves, the function collapses. Most owners haven't documented this knowledge because they "haven't had time."

### Pain 3 — Cross-tool data fragmentation

CRM (if any) doesn't talk to email. Email doesn't talk to accounting. Accounting doesn't talk to scheduling. Reports are assembled monthly by hand. Decisions are made on gut feel because the data is impossible to assemble.

### Pain 4 — Slovenian-language gap with international tools

Most modern SaaS doesn't speak Slovenian well. Customer-facing AI tools generate awkward Slovenian. Internal team members who don't speak English struggle with English-only tooling. This is a tax most international vendors don't pay attention to.

### Pain 5 — Marketing depends on owner posting on social and word of mouth

No systematic content. No outbound. No partnership program. New clients come from referrals or from the owner personally networking. Growth is linear with owner energy.

## Agent stack

The standard deployment for a Slovenian SMB engagement uses two to four agents from the canonical roster. The specific configuration depends on the bought function. Default starting stack:

### Operator Agent (always deployed)

- Owns the day-to-day execution of the bought function
- Vertical-specific configuration (intake for clinics, reporting for accounting firms, scheduling for contractors, etc.)
- Slovenian-language by default for all internal-facing work
- Wired to client's existing tools via available APIs (often Outlook/Google Workspace + a Slovenian-market CRM or accounting tool)
- AIS-side owner during build: Ian Veber (with Anej supporting on architecture)
- Client-side owner during operate: the long-tenured employee whose function the agent is augmenting (critical — operator buy-in is the survival factor here)

### Knowledge Agent (almost always deployed)

- Ingests the long-tenured employees' institutional knowledge through structured interviews + SOP capture (build-phase weeks 1–3)
- Serves the captured knowledge to the Operator Agent
- Becomes the firm's documented operational manual
- AIS-side owner: Ian Veber
- Client-side owner: owner-operator (because the knowledge belongs to the business, not the employee)

### Acquirer Agent (when acquisition is the bought function)

- Slovenian-language content (GEO/AEO + local SEO + sub-sector-specific blog content)
- Partnership outreach to referral sources in the local network (other professionals, complementary services)
- Qualified outbound to Slovenian companies showing buying signals (only when targeting B2B sub-sectors)
- Often tied to a single-page Slovenian website refresh as part of build phase
- AIS-side owner: Anej Vučič
- Client-side owner: owner-operator (no one else has marketing authority)

### Retention Agent (when retention is the bought function)

- Post-engagement / post-purchase outreach
- Birthday / anniversary / re-engagement triggers (Slovenian register, locally calibrated)
- Review collection (Google + sub-sector-specific platforms like Doktor.si or Zlate Strani)
- AIS-side owner: Anej Vučič
- Client-side owner: owner-operator or front-desk staff

**Operate retainer:** €3K–€7K/month
- €3K–€4K: Compact
- €4K–€5.5K: Standard
- €5.5K–€7K: Comprehensive

**Rationale:** Slovenian SMB price ceiling sits below international equivalents because Slovenian market won't bear €50K+ build fees from an unknown agency. The compensating efficiency: cofounder time per engagement is lower (Slovenian-native conversation, faster decisions, less cultural translation), and operate-phase costs are lower (Slovenian-language inference is well-served by current Claude models without expensive specialty tuning).

Margin target: 60%+ (slightly below the 65% baseline because price ceiling forces it, but acceptable given engagement velocity and referral compounding).

## Discovery script

Run in Slovenian unless the prospect prefers English. Most prefer Slovenian.

### Must-pass questions

1. **"Kdo v podjetju odloča o investiciji v razponu €20K–€50K? Si to ti, ali moraš nekoga drugega vključiti?"** (Who in the company decides on a €20K–€50K investment? Is it you, or do you need to involve someone else?)
   - Pass: prospect alone, or prospect + one family member already aligned.
   - Fail: anything involving multiple partners requiring consensus.

2. **"Po implementaciji — kdo na strani podjetja bo dnevno delal s tem sistemom? Mora biti konkretna oseba, ne 'bomo videli'."** (After implementation, who on the company side will work with this system daily? It has to be a specific person, not "we'll see.")
   - Pass: specific named person.
   - Fail: hand-waving or "the owner will."

3. **"Smo OK s 30-dnevnim postopnim uvajanjem, kjer prvi teden agent samo bere, drugi teden sodeluje s človeško kontrolo, in tako naprej?"** (Are we OK with a 30-day phased onboarding where the agent first only reads, then drafts under human control, etc.?)
   - Pass: yes.
   - Fail: any "we need it live immediately."

### Sizing questions

4. **"Koliko strank/projektov/intakov letno?"** (How many clients/projects/intakes per year?)
5. **"Kateri orodja trenutno uporabljate? (CRM, e-pošta, koledar, računovodstvo?)"** (What tools do you currently use?)
6. **"Kje je največji bottleneck — pridobivanje novih strank, ali izvedba, ali zadrževanje?"** (Where is the biggest bottleneck — acquisition, delivery, or retention?)
7. **"Koliko ur na teden ti osebno vzame ta funkcija?"** (How many hours per week does this function personally take you?)

### Risk-detection questions

8. **"Kdo od zaposlenih bi se počutil ogroženo zaradi tega sistema?"** (Which employees might feel threatened by this system?)
9. **"Kako daleč je tvoja interna SOP dokumentacija?"** (How developed is your internal SOP documentation?)
10. **"Si že kdaj poskusil AI orodje za podobno funkcijo? Kaj se je zgodilo?"** (Have you tried an AI tool for a similar function before? What happened?)

If 1–3 fail, decline. If 4–7 reveal mismatch with our price band or capacity, scope accordingly or decline. If 8–10 reveal red flags (open hostility, no SOPs at all, prior bad AI experience), proceed with extra caution and explicit risk-mitigation in the engagement charter.

## Proposal anchor

The anchor varies by sub-sector and function. Pattern:

> **"Free 8–15 hours of owner-operator time per week, redirect to higher-value work, while improving [function-specific metric] by [X%] in 90 days."**

Examples:

- **Aesthetic clinic:** Free 12 hours/week of owner time from intake and retention messaging. Improve client return rate by 18%. Build fee €28K + €4K/mo operate retainer. Payback inside 9 months from owner time alone.
- **Accounting firm:** Free 8 hours/week of partner time from client intake and monthly reporting. Improve client onboarding speed from 3 weeks to 5 days. Build fee €32K + €5K/mo. Payback inside 12 months.
- **Construction contractor:** Free 15 hours/week of owner time from estimate generation and scheduling. Improve estimate close rate by 22%. Build fee €25K + €4K/mo. Payback inside 7 months.

The math is always shown in the proposal. Hide-the-math proposals don't convert in this market.

## Failure modes

### Failure 1 — Long-tenured employee sabotages adoption

The employee whose function the agent augments feels replaced. Doesn't participate in onboarding. Withholds institutional knowledge. Reports problems back to owner instead of to AIS. Agent never reaches operate phase.

*Early warning:* In week 1, the named operator avoids the kickoff call or shows up unprepared. Escalate directly to owner — explicit conversation about role evolution and the employee's continued importance.

### Failure 2 — Owner signs but doesn't allocate time

Common Slovenian SMB pattern: owner is so over-committed that even after signing the build engagement, they can't carve out the time for onboarding-phase decisions. Build phase drifts from 10 weeks to 16 weeks. Margin collapses.

*Early warning:* Owner reschedules week-2 review more than once. Tighten the engagement charter — explicit hours required from owner-side, calendar blocked at engagement start.

### Failure 3 — Tooling integration impossible

The client's primary tool (often a Slovenian-market vertical-specific tool with no public API, or an old version of Microsoft Outlook running on-prem) can't be wired into. We discover this in week 3. Build phase pauses while we scope a workaround.

*Early warning:* During scoping, the tool isn't named specifically, or named only as "our CRM" without vendor identification. Push: ask for screenshot, vendor name, current version, whether they have API credentials.

### Failure 4 — Wrong sub-sector inbound from a referral

A happy client refers us into a sub-sector that fails our four-axis test (e.g. into pure-relationship-based work, or a tiny-revenue firm that can't afford us). We feel social pressure to take the engagement. It loses money.

*Early warning:* "Marko referred me to you, he said you can help with [thing we don't do]." Respond promptly and honestly: "Marko gave you our name, but the specific function you're describing isn't what we deploy. Here's who I'd refer you to instead."

### Failure 5 — Voice locking fails on Slovenian register

Slovenian has formal/informal registers (vikanje/tikanje), regional variations, and sector-specific vocabulary (the way an accountant writes vs the way a hairdresser writes). Generic Claude output sounds "off" to Slovenian readers in ways harder to articulate than in English. If voice samples aren't tightly curated, outputs read as foreign.

*Early warning:* In week 2 draft mode, the owner-operator describes the output as "tujevsko" (foreign-sounding) or "ne zveni kot mi" (doesn't sound like us). Collect more samples and re-tune. Don't deploy externally until this is resolved.

## Kill criteria

Walk away from a Slovenian SMB prospect when:

- The buyer is a multi-partner firm requiring consensus (committee buyer = decline)
- The annual revenue is below €1M (price band doesn't fit)
- The owner doesn't speak conversational English well enough for cofounder-internal handoff, AND none of the cofounders has bandwidth for full-Slovenian engagement (this is rare — Anej and Ian both speak Slovenian — but flag it)
- The bought function depends on a tool with no API access and no workaround
- The prospect treats us as an "AI marketing agency" expecting us to also run their paid ads (we don't; if this expectation can't be reset, decline)
- The owner has already had a bad AI vendor experience and starts the conversation hostile-defensive — sometimes recoverable, sometimes not; if not, decline

## Acquirer Agent specifics

### Where buyers hang out

- **Mladi Podjetnik** (mladipodjetnik.si) — Slovenian SMB community + content site
- **Finance.si** — business news, owner-operator readership
- **Delo** business section — broader audience but reaches the older SMB owner
- **Sub-sector LinkedIn groups** — small, but high-quality reach within Slovenian sub-sectors
- **Sub-sector conferences** — small and regional, but cofounder presence at 2–3/year compounds
- **Coffee meetings** — yes, still. "Kava sestanek" is part of the sales motion. Build it into the Acquirer Agent's outreach cadence (offer in-person coffee, not Zoom).
- **Personal LinkedIn from the cofounders** — Anej and Ian's Slovenian network is more reachable than paid distribution

### Cited content shapes

- Long-form Slovenian-language case studies (anonymized OK)
- Specific-sub-sector content (e.g. "Kako uspešne estetske klinike v Sloveniji avtomatizirajo intake leta 2026" — How successful aesthetic clinics in Slovenia automate intake in 2026)
- Comparison articles (manual vs agent-driven, Slovenian-language version)
- Owner-operator interview content (we interview, they get the publicity, we get the case study material)

### Partnerships worth building

- **Local accountants and lawyers** — strong referral sources for SMBs. Coffee with 1–2 per month builds the network.
- **Sub-sector consultants** — e.g. clinic operations consultants for aesthetic medicine, restaurant operations consultants for hospitality. Refer back-and-forth.
- **Mladi Podjetnik / SMB community organizers** — speaker placement, content collaboration
- **Banks** (NLB, NKBM business banking) — referrals from business banking advisors who see SMBs that need ops automation but don't know who to call

### Avoid

- Generic Slovenian marketing agencies (they push paid social and SEO, which is our anti-pattern; awkward partnership shape)
- International "AI consulting" partners (they don't understand the Slovenian market depth and won't refer well)

## Case study angle

### Headline metric

> **"Slovenian [sub-sector] business freed [X] hours/week of owner time and grew [metric] by [Y%] in 6 months, using a Slovenian-language agent stack deployed by AIS."**

### Story structure

1. **Business context** — name (if consented) or anonymized, location, sub-sector, revenue band, what they do
2. **Owner's situation** — specific quote on the bottleneck, owner's words in Slovenian + English translation
3. **What we deployed** — agent stack, integrations, build-phase timeline
4. **Onboarding ladder in Slovenian context** — what was different from international engagements (more in-person time, more SOP capture, more voice-locking iteration)
5. **Outcomes** — quantitative (hours freed, metric improved) and qualitative (what the owner now spends time on)
6. **What didn't work** — be honest about a missed assumption, an integration that took longer, an early-stage tuning issue

## Vertical-specific knowledge requirements

### Must-know

- Slovenian business law basics (s.p. vs d.o.o. structure, VAT thresholds, common contract patterns)
- Slovenian market-specific tools (računovodski programi like e-računi.si, Pantheon; vertical CRMs like ICDC for healthcare)
- Slovenian-language register conventions (vikanje for B2B by default, tikanje only with explicit permission)
- Major Slovenian SMB communities and gathering points (Mladi Podjetnik, GZS, sub-sector associations)
- Cultural patterns around in-person trust-building (coffee meetings, lunch meetings, regional accents and what they signal)

### Nice-to-have

- Familiarity with Slovenian payment systems (UPN, SEPA, integration with Slovenian banks)
- Awareness of regional sub-sector dynamics (e.g. Štajerska vs Primorska business culture differences)
- Connections inside Slovenian podjetniške asociacije

### Partnership for

- Sub-sector-specific deep knowledge (we don't pretend to be aesthetic medicine experts; we partner with the client's existing aesthetic consultant if one exists, or refer to one)
- Slovenian legal review on engagement letters (Nejc handles this — original cofounder owns legal)
- Slovenian language proofreading on customer-facing outputs (native-Slovenian cofounder reviews; do not ship Slovenian content unreviewed by Anej or via a Slovenian editor)
