# Partnership System

Referral partners are a high-leverage acquisition channel. Each partnership well-maintained delivers 2–8 qualified inbound conversations per year at near-zero marginal cost. AIS targets 8–15 active partnerships at steady state.

This file specifies who to partner with per vertical, outreach cadence, terms, enablement, retention, and reciprocity tracking.

---

## Why partnerships matter for AIS

The math:

- 1 well-maintained partner: ~2–8 qualified inbound per year
- 8–15 active partners: ~25–80 qualified inbound per year, sustained
- Cost: ~4–8 cofounder hours per partner per year (quarterly check-ins + ad-hoc support + reciprocity work)
- Conversion: inbound from partner referrals converts at higher rate than cold outbound (typically 30–50% advance to scoping vs 15–25% for outbound), because the partner pre-qualified and warmed

At target steady state, partnerships are the highest-leverage acquisition channel. They take longer to build than outbound (most partnerships require 6–12 months before producing significant referrals), but compound from there.

## Who to partner with — per vertical

### Specialty legal

**Tier 1 — High-value partners**
- **Legal-tech consultants:** specialists in practice management software implementation, change management, legal IT. They get hired by firms to fix operational problems; some of those problems are agent-stack-shaped.
- **Practice management software vendors:** Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther — integration partner programs. Listed-partner status drives inbound from firms evaluating the PMS.
- **Specialty CLE providers:** continuing legal education programs. Cofounder speaker placements at relevant CLEs reach the partner-buyer directly.
- **Specialty bar association BD committees:** speaker placements, panel participation. Slower-build but high-credibility.

**Tier 2 — Adjacent partners**
- **Legal design / legal ops consultancies:** non-competitive, refer back-and-forth opportunities
- **Specialty legal recruiters:** they know which firms are in growth phases and which partners are looking for operational leverage

**Avoid**
- Generic legal-marketing agencies (they push paid search and content as a bundle; awkward partnership shape)
- Direct legal-AI vendors (CoCounsel, Harvey, Casetext) — different category but brand association gets muddied

### B2B SaaS demand-gen

**Tier 1**
- **Fractional CROs / VP Sales consultants** specializing in B2B SaaS: trusted advisors to founder CEOs; refer-rich relationship if cultivated
- **GTM tooling vendors:** Clay (integration partnership), Common Room (integration partnership), Apollo (selective)
- **SaaStr / Pavilion ecosystem:** speaker placements, partnership announcements
- **B2B SaaS-focused VCs:** they care about portfolio company efficiency; refer-into-portfolio is a high-leverage motion if a few VCs commit
- **Outbound coaches and consultants:** Outbound Squad, others; non-competitive, often refer-friendly

**Tier 2**
- **SaaS-focused agencies in adjacent functions** (e.g. brand agencies, design agencies, copywriting consultancies serving SaaS) — refer non-overlapping work to each other
- **B2B SaaS founder communities:** Pavilion, GTMfund, regional founder slacks

**Avoid**
- Generic B2B marketing agencies
- Direct competitors (Apollo's services arm, Outreach's services, Salesloft consultants)

### Slovenian businesses

**Tier 1**
- **Local accountants and lawyers:** strong referral sources for SMBs. Coffee with 1–2 per month builds the network. Slovenian business community is small; the same accountants serve dozens of our ICP prospects.
- **Sub-sector consultants:** clinic operations consultants for aesthetic medicine, restaurant operations consultants for hospitality, etc.
- **Mladi Podjetnik / SMB community organizers:** speaker placements, content collaboration, community presence
- **Banks (NLB, NKBM business banking):** referrals from business banking advisors who see SMBs that need ops automation but don't know who to call

**Tier 2**
- **Slovenian sub-sector associations** (regional chamber of commerce subgroups, professional associations)
- **Slovenian business media** (Finance.si business reporters, Mladi Podjetnik editors)
- **Slovenian SaaS vendors selling into SMBs** (e.g. e-računi.si, Pantheon — they have SMB customer bases that overlap with our ICP)

**Avoid**
- Generic Slovenian marketing agencies (overlap and conflict)
- Large international consulting firms operating in SI (different price tier; no fit)

## Partnership types and structures

### Type A — Referral exchange

Partner refers prospects; we refer back when we see prospects who need their service. No formal fee, just reciprocity tracking. Most common type.

**Structure:**
- Mutual understanding (often informal — handshake or one-page document)
- Quarterly check-in to discuss flow
- Track referrals each direction; aim for rough parity over 12 months
- Both parties can recommend each other publicly (case studies, testimonials, intros)

### Type B — Listed-partner programs

Partner has a formal "preferred partners" or "integration partners" listing on their site/in their tooling. We get inbound from prospects browsing the partner's ecosystem.

**Structure:**
- Application + onboarding to partner's program
- Often co-marketing collaboration (joint webinars, joint case studies, joint articles)
- Sometimes a referral fee structure (typically 5–15% of first-year revenue for partner-referred deals)
- Often requires us to support their customers using their tooling (we already do, but worth confirming)

### Type C — Strategic alliances

Deeper integration with a partner — sometimes including technical integration, joint product offering, or co-selling. Rare and high-effort.

**Structure:**
- Written partnership agreement
- Named relationship manager on both sides
- Coordinated GTM motion (target verticals, joint pitches, etc.)
- Revenue-share or joint-deliverable structure if revenue-share applies
- Quarterly business review at executive level

Reserved for ≤3 strategic alliances total. Each consumes cofounder time at ~4 hours/month.

### Type D — Speaker placements

Not a partnership in the formal sense, but a partnership-shaped acquisition activity. Cofounder speaks at partner's event, partner gets credibility, we get audience reach.

**Structure:**
- Per-event basis
- Cofounder prepares talk specific to partner's audience
- Recording shared by both
- Lead generation typically through followup CTA at end of talk

## Outreach cadence — new partners

Quarterly goal: 1 new partnership conversation per cofounder. So ~12 new partnership conversations per year total.

### Identification

Acquirer Agent maintains a partnership prospect backlog. Sourced from:
- Cofounder observations (who keeps appearing in adjacent contexts)
- Inbound prospect mentions ("we already work with [partner]")
- Industry research (who's serving our ICP without our service shape)

### Initial outreach

Cofounder-led. Not via Acquirer Agent's outbound infrastructure. Why: partnership outreach is relationship work, not transactional.

Pattern:
1. Cofounder identifies a specific partner candidate
2. Cofounder finds a warm path (mutual connection, prior interaction, shared context)
3. Personal outreach (not via platform, often via personal email or LinkedIn DM)
4. Initial conversation: low-pressure, learn about their work, share about ours
5. Identify potential overlap or non-overlap
6. Second conversation if first showed potential

### Conversion to active partner

From first conversation to "active partner status" typically takes 3–6 months. Includes:
- 2–4 conversations to align on fit
- Initial test referrals (each direction)
- Quarterly check-in pattern established
- Both parties publicly acknowledging the partnership (in some form)

Active partner = pattern of referrals flowing, both sides committed to maintenance.

## Partnership terms

### Default terms (Type A — Referral exchange)

- **No referral fee** by default. Reciprocity-based.
- **Mutual NDA** if either party shares sensitive information.
- **Public acknowledgment** allowed (logo placements, "trusted by" mentions, case study features) with mutual consent.
- **Termination:** 30 days' notice from either party.

### Terms with referral fees (Type B)

When a fee structure is appropriate:

- **5–10% of first-year build fee** for partner-referred deals that close
- **Paid quarterly** based on actual close-and-cash
- **Capped at €5K per deal** to prevent gaming
- **Disclosed to client** during scoping (transparency is required; partner referral is fine to disclose)
- **No fee on operate retainer** (would create perverse incentives over time)

### Terms with strategic alliances (Type C)

Negotiated case-by-case. Always written. Always reviewed by Nejc.

### What we don't do

- **No exclusivity** (we don't promise we'll only work with one partner in a category, nor do we demand exclusivity from partners — exclusivity creates rigidity and rarely benefits both parties)
- **No prepayment for partnership** (we don't pay to be listed unless it's a true ROI-positive sponsorship, which is rare in our motion)
- **No "MOU" theater** (we sign documents that mean something; we don't sign MOUs that don't lead to real activity)

## Partner enablement

For a partner to refer well, they need to know what to refer. Enablement is the work we do to make their job easier.

### Partner-facing materials

Per partnership, we provide:

- **One-pager:** what AIS does, who we're a good fit for, who we're not, how to refer
- **Per-vertical brief:** specific to verticals where the partner has overlap with our ICP
- **Refer-when-X-happens guide:** specific signals the partner might see that suggest a referral candidate
- **Direct contact route:** how the partner introduces a prospect to us (email template, intro language, who to CC)
- **Case study or proof asset:** something the partner can share with the prospect to vouch for us

### Periodic enablement

- **Quarterly check-in conversation:** state of the relationship, recent referrals, what's working, what isn't
- **Annual enablement refresh:** updated materials, new case studies, updated ICP if shifted
- **Ad-hoc support:** when partner has a specific scoping question about a possible referral, cofounder responds within 24h

## Partner retention

Partnerships decay if not maintained. Standard maintenance pattern:

### Per-quarter (per partner)

- **30-min check-in conversation:** state of partnership, recent referrals (both directions), any concerns
- **Refresh on AIS updates:** what's new, what's launching, what we should be sending their way
- **Refresh on partner updates:** what they're focused on, what they could use referrals for

### Per-month (across all partners)

- **Reciprocity tracking review:** are we sending as many referrals as we're getting? Where are we under-delivering?
- **Material updates:** any new case studies, articles, or materials worth pushing to partners

### Per-year (per partner)

- **Annual review:** is this partnership still valuable for both sides? Should it evolve (deeper, lighter, different)?
- **Public acknowledgment refresh:** updated case study, joint content, public mention

## Reciprocity tracking

Reciprocity is the soul of a healthy partner relationship. We track it explicitly.

### What gets tracked

Per partner, in `sales/partner-tracker.md` (live document):

- **Referrals received from partner:** count + outcomes (closed / open / lost) + value
- **Referrals sent to partner:** count + outcomes
- **Public support:** speaker placements, joint content, mutual mentions
- **Ad-hoc support:** when one party helped the other on something not strictly partnership work

### What healthy reciprocity looks like

Within any rolling 12-month window:
- Referrals roughly balanced (within 3–5 in either direction)
- At least 2 instances of public support each direction
- Both parties report the relationship as "active and valued" at annual review

### What unhealthy reciprocity looks like

- Partner sends 8 referrals over 12 months; we sent 0 (we're under-delivering; risk of partner reducing flow)
- Partner sends 0 referrals over 12 months; we sent 4 (partner isn't activating; needs enablement or relationship has cooled)
- Public support imbalanced (one side promoting heavily, the other silent)

### Corrective actions

- If we're under-delivering on referrals to partner: explicit cofounder time to find referral candidates. Don't wait for organic opportunities.
- If partner is under-delivering to us: enablement refresh + direct conversation about what they're seeing
- If both sides have decayed: annual-review-style conversation about whether to continue, evolve, or wind down

## When to refer out to partners

We refer out when:

- Prospect needs paid-media management (refer to paid-media partner)
- Prospect needs custom software dev outside agent stack scope (refer to dev partner)
- Prospect needs CRM implementation (refer to Salesforce / HubSpot implementation partner)
- Prospect needs brand identity / visual design (refer to design partner)
- Prospect's vertical isn't in our roster and won't be (refer to a generalist partner)
- Prospect's revenue is too small for us but big enough for a smaller-scope vendor (refer)
- Prospect's geography is outside our reach without partner support (refer to partner with local presence)

The discipline: refer out ≥10% of inbound prospects (those failing our fit but legitimate elsewhere). High refer-out rate signals we're qualifying honestly. Low refer-out rate signals we're stretching to take work we shouldn't.

## Failure modes

### Failure 1 — Partner pipeline goes cold

Existing partner stops referring. Either dissatisfied, distracted, or relationship cooled.

*Early warning:* 6 months without a referral from a previously-active partner.
*Mitigation:* immediate check-in conversation. Identify cause. If recoverable, refresh enablement. If not recoverable, document and de-prioritize.

### Failure 2 — Reciprocity imbalance damages relationship

We've taken referrals for 12 months without sending any back. Partner notices. Relationship strains.

*Early warning:* reciprocity tracker shows imbalance worsening.
*Mitigation:* cofounder time allocation to identifying referral candidates for the underserved partner. If we genuinely can't reciprocate (no referral candidates ever fit them), have an honest conversation and consider formal referral fee instead.

### Failure 3 — Partnership becomes vanity

We have 15 listed partners on the website but only 3 actually produce referrals. The other 12 are decorative.

*Mitigation:* annual audit. Decorative partnerships either get re-activated or de-listed. Honest representation matters.

### Failure 4 — Partner becomes competitive

Partner expands into our service area. The previously-complementary relationship becomes competitive. Both sides have to decide.

*Mitigation:* annual review catches this. Direct conversation about how to handle. Sometimes the answer is "partner in one segment, compete in another" — works if both sides are mature. Sometimes the answer is "wind down the partnership amicably."

### Failure 5 — Strategic alliance fails to produce expected ROI

A Type C alliance was set up with high hopes but doesn't generate the expected flow. High cofounder time investment, low return.

*Mitigation:* don't pretend it's working. Have the conversation. Either pivot the partnership structure or wind it down gracefully.
