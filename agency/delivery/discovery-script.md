# Discovery Call Script

30–45 minute first-contact call. Cofounder leads. Goal: qualify or disqualify the prospect against the four-axis test and size the engagement. Decide go/no-go to scoping by end of call.

This is not a sales script. It's a structured conversation. The aim is honest qualification, not persuasion. Most prospects who fail the test should fail it during this call — saving everyone time.

---

## Pre-call (10 min, cofounder)

Before the call, prepare:

- Prospect company background (revenue band, employees, geography, vertical match)
- Buyer's role and likely decision authority (LinkedIn lookup)
- Source of inquiry (Acquirer signal trigger, partnership referral, content inbound)
- Hypothesis on which vertical playbook applies and which engagement shape
- 3 specific questions to ask based on what the prospect's company actually does (not generic discovery questions)

Have the relevant vertical playbook open in another tab. Pricing tables open in another tab.

---

## Opening (3 min)

Skip the "tell me about yourself" warmup. The cofounder already knows what the prospect does.

> "Thanks for the time. I've looked at [specific thing about prospect's company — recent news, product launch, exec move, whatever's real]. Before I jump in: I want to be useful, so let me set the goal for this conversation. By the end, we'll know whether what AIS does fits what you're trying to solve. If yes, we'll schedule a deeper scoping conversation. If no, I'll either refer you to someone who fits better, or tell you honestly that we're not the right vendor. Sound OK?"

This anchors the call's frame: qualification, not persuasion. Most buyers respond well — they're tired of vendors pretending every conversation is a fit.

---

## Function exploration (10 min)

Start with the function, not the company. Specific questions:

> 1. "Walk me through the function you're thinking about. What's currently broken or unscalable?"
> 2. "Who currently does this work? How long have they been doing it?"
> 3. "What tools do they use? Where does the data live?"
> 4. "If you could fix one thing about how this function operates today, what would it be?"

Listen for:
- **Bounded vs diffuse function.** Does the answer describe a well-bounded function (intake-to-scoped-memo, demand-gen-from-signal-to-meeting) or a sprawling one (all of marketing, all of ops)?
- **Encoded vs embodied work.** Is the work described as documents/data/structured decisions, or as in-person presence/relationship-based?
- **Specific pain or vague.** A buyer who says "intake takes 9 days from inquiry to scoped memo" is different from one who says "things are slow."

If the function is diffuse, push back gently: "When you say 'marketing' — can we narrow to one bounded function inside marketing? Lead generation? Content production? Brand strategy? Which specifically?"

---

## Must-pass questions (10 min)

These are non-negotiable. Any failure → polite decline at the end of the call.

### Authority

> 5. "Who at the company signs off on engagements in the €[X]K–€[Y]K range?"

(Use the relevant vertical playbook's pricing band as X–Y.)

- **Pass:** prospect on the call, OR one other person reachable in a week who is already aligned
- **Fail:** management committee, RFP process, "we'll need to discuss it at the next partnership meeting," budget approval through CFO + procurement

If fail: note it, continue the call (useful learning), but the call ends in decline.

### Operator

> 6. "After deployment, who on your team would be the named operator working with this system day-to-day? I need a specific person, not 'we'll figure it out.'"

- **Pass:** specific named person, either on the call or named by name
- **Fail:** "we'll see," "the team will handle it collectively," "I would" (if the prospect is too senior to actually operate)

The operator is critical — per `docs/principles.md` rule 10, engagements without a named client-side human owner fail.

### Onboarding ladder

> 7. "We run a 30-day phased onboarding: week 1 the agent only reads, week 2 it drafts under human review, week 3 it operates internally, week 4 it deploys externally. Is that OK with your timeline?"

- **Pass:** yes
- **Fail:** "we need it live for our launch next month," "can we compress this to a week?"

The ladder is non-negotiable per `docs/principles.md` rule 7.

### Paid media

> 8. "Just to confirm: we don't run paid ads. No Google Ads, no Meta Ads, no programmatic. The acquisition we do is content-based, partnerships, and qualified outbound. Is paid media something you'd need from us?"

- **Pass:** "no, we already have that handled" or "no, we're trying to build a non-paid motion"
- **Fail:** "yes, that would be ideal" — refer out

## Sizing questions (8 min)

Get the data to size the engagement.

For specialty legal:
> 9. "Roughly how many intake inquiries per year does the firm process?"
> 10. "Average billable rate across the partnership?"
> 11. "Current bottleneck — getting more inquiries, qualifying faster, or scoping faster?"

For B2B SaaS demand-gen:
> 9. "What's your current monthly meeting volume from outbound? From inbound?"
> 10. "How many SDRs/BDRs do you have today? Average ACV?"
> 11. "Biggest constraint — meeting volume, meeting quality, or AE bandwidth?"

For Slovenian businesses:
> 9. "Approximately how many [function-specific units: clients/intakes/transactions/etc] per year?"
> 10. "How many hours per week does this function take you personally?"
> 11. "What tools do you use today — CRM, accounting, email, scheduling?"

The answers position the engagement against the playbook's pricing band (compact / standard / comprehensive).

---

## Risk-detection questions (5 min)

Probe for early-warning signs of high-failure-mode engagements.

> 12. "Who on staff would feel threatened by an agent-driven [function]?"
> 13. "Have you tried AI tooling for this function before? What happened?"
> 14. "What's your tolerance for being early on something — versus wanting boring, proven approaches?"

Listen for:
- **Operator buy-in risk.** "My SDR team would hate this" or "my paralegals are skeptical of AI" — flag for scoping.
- **Prior bad experience.** "We tried CoCounsel and it didn't work" — probe specifically. Could be relevant context, or could be a buyer who blames vendors for their own onboarding failures.
- **Bleeding-edge buyer.** Someone who wants AI for the brag, not for the function. Yellow flag — these buyers under-commit on operator buy-in.

---

## Close (5 min)

Three possible closes based on the call's signal:

### Close A — Advance to scoping

> "Based on what you've shared, this looks like a fit on our side. The next step is a deeper scoping conversation — 60–90 minutes, we'd go through the function in detail, integration inventory, named owners, success metrics. We typically follow up with a structured questionnaire to capture details we don't cover live. After scoping, we'd draft an SOW with the proposed scope, pricing, and timeline. Want to schedule scoping for [next week]?"

Schedule on the call if possible. Send calendar invite immediately after.

### Close B — Refer out

> "Honest read: this isn't a fit for AIS specifically. The reason is [specific axis failure — diffuse function / committee buyer / paid-media-needed / etc]. I think you'd be better served by [specific referral]. Want me to make a warm intro?"

Don't pretend it's a fit. Buyers respect honest disqualification — they'd rather not waste their time too.

### Close C — Disqualify cleanly

> "Based on what you've shared, I don't think we're the right vendor for what you're trying to do. [One-sentence reason — won't be specific enough to be useful but won't be vague enough to seem like an excuse.] I'd recommend looking at [generic category of vendor that does fit]. Thanks for the time."

Polite but final. No follow-up sequence, no "let's stay in touch."

## Patterns to watch for

### Prospect over-qualifies themselves

Some buyers know the script. They answer must-pass questions correctly because they've been pitched before and know what we want to hear. The signal: answers come too smoothly, with no specific detail.

Test: ask for the specific name when they answer "the operator will be [role]." If they hesitate or use a generic title without a name, the answer was rehearsed.

### Prospect under-qualifies themselves

The opposite — buyer answers in vague generalities when they actually have specific answers. Often a defensive posture from previous bad vendor experiences.

Test: ask the same question two ways, separated by other content. If the second answer is more specific, the first was guarded; lean in and build trust.

### Cofounder talks too much

The biggest failure mode of discovery calls is the cofounder selling instead of qualifying. The cofounder fills silence with explanations of what AIS does. The prospect responds politely but you've learned nothing about whether they fit.

Test: at end of call, count whose talk-time was higher. If cofounder talked more than 40%, the call wasn't a discovery — it was a pitch.

Cofounder discipline: ask question, shut up, take notes. Comfortable silences. The buyer fills them with useful information.

### Prospect wants to see proof first

"Can you send me case studies before we have this conversation?" Some buyers want to evaluate AIS before committing time to a call.

Response: "Sure — here's our most relevant case study [link]. After you've reviewed, if it's interesting, let's do the call. Note: the call is a qualification conversation, not a pitch. I'll be asking whether your situation fits what we do."

This re-anchors the call frame and filters for buyers who genuinely want to engage.

---

## What this script is not

- A close script. Don't try to close on the discovery call. Discovery's purpose is qualification, not closing.
- A pitch deck. Don't open with company background. The prospect can read the website.
- A demo. No demo on the discovery call. Demos belong in scoping or post-SOW, when context is established.
- An NDA conversation. Don't sign NDAs before discovery. If the prospect insists, the discovery is too premature — push it back to "after we mutually qualify."
