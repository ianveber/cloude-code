## 0. The cold-start reality check (read this first — it governs everything below)

Iman's playbook was written from the top of a mountain he's already climbed. Every framework silently assumes: **an existing audience of millions, 30+ feeder accounts, a paid clipper army, a sales team, and years of runway.** Ian has none of that. Ian has:

- **30 days** to MVP, **€0 ad budget**, **100% organic.**

So the operating principle is a **hard inversion of Iman's default**: Iman can afford to optimize for reach first and monetize later because he has scale to burn. **Ian must optimize for the warm list and depth first, and treat reach as a slow compounding by-product.** Wherever a framework below only works at Iman's scale, it's flagged and the **adjusted cold-start version** is given. This is not optional — a recommendation that ignores the cold start is a wrong recommendation.

### How attention flows — the interlock rule

- **Attention only flows personal → app → sale.** Ian's face and story pull strangers in; he *points* them at ATHLOS. ATHLOS never has to be charismatic — it only has to be **credible and easy to buy.** This is Iman's **Content Siloing** applied honestly: don't dilute the trust engine with product-brochure content, and don't dilute the conversion surface with random vlogging.
- **The bridge is always the same click-path:** IG/YT content → link in bio / pinned → **Beehiiv waitlist or app checkout.** Map this *before* making any piece (**Intentional Brand**). If a video has no path to that click, it's a nurture piece for the core, not a growth piece — that's fine, but know which one it is.

### 🚩 Cold-start adjustment (this is where Iman is wrong for Ian)

Iman runs **two fully separate brand universes** because he has the audience to feed both. **Ian does not have enough audience to feed two accounts from scratch in 30 days.** If he splits his effort 50/50 on day 1, both accounts starve.

**Adjusted architecture for the cold start:**
- **Ian's personal account is the priority (~80% of effort).** It's the trust engine and it's where a cold Slovenian audience will actually follow a *person*, not a logo. People follow founders; they don't follow B2C fitness apps they've never heard of.
- **The ATHLOS account launches lean (~20% of effort):** at first it's essentially a **proof-and-offer repository** — Tim's science, product demos, the early-bird terms, testimonials as they arrive, the waitlist link. It's the place Ian *sends* people, not a place he expects to grow organically yet.
- **Short-form is shot once, posted to both** (Iman keeps feeders strictly separate — Ian cannot afford that overhead solo; cross-post the same Reel/Short to both accounts and to YouTube Shorts). Revisit separation only *after* the personal account has real traction (post-launch).
- **The "core converting" YouTube video lives under IAN, not ATHLOS** — because the buyer needs to trust the *founder-athlete* before they trust the app.

## 2. The ICP — who exactly are we selling to

**(Niche-Down-Then-Expand)**

### The primary buyer (the €29–89 tiers)

**"Resen slovenski športnik / resen rekreativec, ki trenira kot da nekaj šteje."**

A portrait of the person who pays €29–89/month:

- **Age 18–34**, male-skewed but not exclusively. Lives in Slovenia (Ljubljana / Maribor / Kranj / Celje and the surrounding regions).
- **Trains 4–6× per week** and *already spends money on it* — gym membership, supplements, occasionally a personal trainer they can't really afford. **Willingness-to-pay is already proven** by their spend; ATHLOS just captures it better.
- Either **a competitive club athlete** (plays in a real league) **or a serious "gym rat / hybrid athlete"** who trains with a purpose (getting faster, stronger, more explosive), not just for aesthetics.
- **Technically comfortable** — lives on Instagram/YouTube, would install a PWA, trusts an AI tool if a credible human vouches for it.
- **Self-driven, slightly obsessive, a little lonely in it** — they take their training more seriously than the people around them, and there's *no one in Slovenia* giving them elite, personalized programming in Slovenian.

### Their pains (in their own words — use these as hooks)

1. *"Ne vem, ali sploh treniram pravilno."* — programming anxiety; they follow random Instagram splits and English PDFs and never know if it's right for *their* sport.
2. *"Osebni trener je predrag, generični program pa je smeti."* — priced out of a real coach, underserved by free/generic plans.
3. *"Nihče ne trenira specifično za moj šport."* — no Slovenian resource programs for *their* sport's actual demands (speed, COD, explosiveness), only generic bodybuilding.
4. *"Ne znam periodizirati okoli sezone / tekem."* — no idea how to peak, deload, or manage in-season load.
5. *"Poškodbe me ustavljajo."* — get hurt because nobody's managing their fatigue/volume.

### What they already follow

- International fitness YouTube (Athlean-X, Jeff Nippard, sport-specific speed guys like the Spellman-type accounts).
- Slovenian/Balkan fitness & športna IG accounts — but these are mostly **aesthetics/bodybuilding**, not **athletic performance**. **That gap is the wedge.**
- Their sport's national figures / club pages.

### Why they'd buy

Because for **€29–59/month** — *less than one session with a decent personal trainer* — they get a system that (a) knows their sport, (b) periodizes around their season, (c) adapts, and (d) is vouched for by a real Slovenian physiologist (Tim) and a founder who *visibly trains himself with it* (Ian). It removes the "am I doing this right?" anxiety that follows them into every session.

### 🎯 The ONE narrow segment to dominate FIRST — and the defense

**Niche-Down-Then-Expand is the single most important Iman principle for Ian.** Iman ran *exclusively* SMMA content for years before broadening — order matters. Ian's instinct will be to say "for all Slovenian athletes." **That is the mistake this section exists to kill.**

**Recommended beachhead: young Slovenian TEAM-SPORT athletes who need SPEED & EXPLOSIVENESS — concretely, football (nogomet) + basketball (košarka) players, ages 16–26, at club/academy level.**

**Why this exact segment:**
1. **Biggest addressable pool in Slovenia** — nogomet and košarka are the two deepest youth-athlete pools in the country. Niche ≠ tiny; it means *specific*, not *small*.
2. **The product is already strongest here.** Tim's brain is loaded with speed/COD/explosive protocols (Rugby 7s speed framework, Spellman top-speed and hill work, plyometrics). We lead with our best-built capability.
3. **Speed/explosiveness is a *visible, emotional, shareable* outcome** — "postal sem hitrejši" makes far better short-form than "izboljšal sem hipertrofijo." Better content = better organic reach = better cold start.
4. **Clear enemy to position against:** generic bodybuilding splits that make team-sport kids slow and bulky. Easy to be "one-of-one" here.
5. **It maps to real Slovenian identity** — nogomet/košarka are cultural. Content in this lane travels.

**Why NOT the alternatives (the disqualification of segments):**
- *"All athletes"* → owns nothing, sounds like everyone, cold start dies.
- *Pure aesthetics/bodybuilding gym crowd* → hyper-competitive, already served by every fitness influencer, no differentiation, and *not* where Tim's science is strongest.
- *Endurance (kolesarstvo/tek)* → strong in Slovenia but a different physiology, different brain, would dilute the beachhead. **Expansion target #2, not the start.**
- *B2B clubs* → explicitly off the table for launch (Ian's locked decision). It becomes the natural *expand* step once individual athletes inside clubs already love it (bottom-up land-and-expand).

**The expansion ladder (do NOT start here — earn the right):** nogomet/košarka speed athletes → other explosive team sports (rokomet, odbojka) → individual explosive sports (atletika sprint/skoki, borilne) → serious hybrid/gym athletes → endurance → B2B clubs. Each step timed to when the previous one is *won and talking about it.*

## 5. The offer at launch — MVP + early-bird (first 100)

**(Price anchoring + honest scarcity)**

### The mechanics

- **Anchor high, sell the early-bird.** Regular pricing is the anchor; early-bird is the deal. Always show both, crossed-out.
  - BASIC: ~~€49~~ → **€29/mes**
  - PRO: ~~€99~~ → **€59/mes**
  - ELITE: ~~€149~~ → **€89/mes** *(waitlist)*
- **The grandfather clause is the real hook:** the **first 100** lock their price **for life** (`za vedno`). This is honest scarcity — 100 is a real number, and the lifetime lock is a real, permanent benefit. Never fake the counter.
- **Lead with PRO** as the recommended tier (price-anchored between BASIC and ELITE — the classic middle-option pull), but let BASIC be the low-friction entry so nobody bounces on price.
- **The warm Beehiiv list gets first access** — they're the first crack at the 100 spots. This rewards the early believers and gives the launch its opening momentum (the no-leak backend point: the warm list is the launch engine, not an afterthought).

### 🚩 Cold-start adjustment on scarcity

Iman uses **giant** scarcity theater ($250K prize pools, McLarens). **Ian cannot and should not.** At his scale, credibility *is* the currency — one whiff of fake urgency in a small market where everyone knows everyone kills trust permanently. So: **scarcity must be real and modest.** "Prvih 100, cena zaklenjena za vedno" is true, verifiable, and enough. Show a *real* remaining count only if you'll keep it honest; otherwise use round milestones ("že 40 od 100 mest").
