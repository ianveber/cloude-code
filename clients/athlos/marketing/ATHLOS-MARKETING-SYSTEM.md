# 1 · The Model & The Ground-Up Setup

## 1.1 The model in plain terms

### How the Iman model actually works

Strip away the villa and the screenshots and Iman Gadzhi's marketing runs on four load-bearing ideas. We steal the mechanics, not the persona.

1. **A brand is a machine that turns attention into revenue.** Not a vibe, not a follower count — a *machine* with an input (a stranger's attention) and an output (a paying member). The **Intentional Brand** principle: you map the path from *someone watching* to *someone buying* **before** you make anything. If a piece of content has no path to the offer, you either know it's a nurture piece and post it on purpose, or you don't make it.

2. **Content is a compounding owned asset, not a treadmill.** You are not a content creator posting to survive the algorithm; you are a business owner building "digital real estate" that keeps selling while you sleep. A video filmed once and packaged well keeps working for years. That reframe is what makes the whole thing sustainable for a founder who also has a product to ship.

3. **Optimize for LEADS, not views.** This is the discipline that separates a business from an influencer. A 5,000-follower brand can out-earn a 5,000,000-follower one because it measures the right thing. The first question on every content idea is: *is the goal views, or leads?* View counts are dopamine; leads are money. We ban vanity metrics from our scoreboard (Chapter 1.4 and the metrics section make this concrete).

4. **The founder's face is the non-delegable engine.** People follow a *person*, not a logo they've never heard of. The trust that makes a stranger buy comes from a human being on camera — their story, their opinions, their face. You can delegate editing, scheduling, thumbnails, email setup, analytics, and DMs. You **cannot** delegate being the face. That is the one job that has to stay with the founder.

### How ATHLOS runs it the same way — with Ian as the face

Everything above transfers directly, with one hard adaptation: **we are a cold start** (near-zero audience, a founder who's also shipping the app, a warm list you can count on two hands, a ~2.1M-person Slovenian market, organic-only, ~30 days to MVP). So we invert Iman's default: he optimizes reach-first because he can monetize later at scale; **we optimize warm-list-and-depth first, and treat reach as the slow second engine.** (This blind-spot check runs through the whole manual — never assume Iman's scale.)

**The central mandate: Ian Veber is THE FACE.** Ian films the content, Ian is *in* the content, Ian presents on camera. He is the founder-athlete building the AI training system he wished existed — documenting the whole thing (THE JOURNEY) while training himself on it, in public, in Slovenian. That building-in-public is un-copyable because it's literally his life for the next 30+ days. The entire system in this manual is designed so **Ian can be the on-camera talent while the team builds and runs the machine around him.** Tim Drenovc (physiolog) is the science-credibility face — the proof, not the promoter — appearing lightly where the science needs a real authority behind it.

- **Ian's face → content.** Ian is the reason a stranger stops scrolling. Everything starts with him on camera. The content isn't made "for content's sake" — each piece is reverse-engineered from one call-to-action (the free Tim plan / the waitlist).
- **Content → lead.** The content points at one thing: the free lead magnet. A follower is rented from the algorithm; a **lead is an owned email on the Beehiiv list.** We convert followers into emails as fast as possible, because we own the email and only rent the follow.
- **Lead → nurture.** The Beehiiv list gets a Slovenian story sequence + the core video (VSL). This is where trust in Ian *and* Tim is built. At our scale the warm list is not a footnote — **it is the launch.**
- **Nurture → offer.** The first-100 early-bird (BASIC €29 / PRO ⭐€59 / ELITE €89, lock price for life) opens to the warm list first. Real, modest scarcity — never a fake counter.

**The one-sentence version:** *Ian's face pulls a stranger in → content routes them to a free Tim plan → that turns them into an owned email → 30 days of Slovenian nurture warms them → the first-100 offer converts them → onboarding and retention keep them paying.* No step depends on volume we don't have. Every step depends on catching leads a single human can still handle by hand.

## 1.2 The non-negotiable principles

These are the rules the whole team operates by. If a decision is unclear, it resolves in favor of these. Print them; they govern every chapter that follows.

1. **Ian is the face. Full stop.** No piece of top-of-funnel content ships without Ian on camera or in voice. We build the machine *around* the founder; we never try to replace him with a logo, a faceless page, or AI voiceover. This is the one non-delegable job. *(Iman: the founder's face is the engine.)*

2. **Leads, not views.** We measure confirmed Beehiiv leads and paying members — never follower count, never Reel views. Anyone who catches themselves celebrating a view count is off-mission. *(Iman: Optimize-for-leads-not-views.)*

3. **Every piece has a path to the offer — or we know it's nurture.** Before we make anything, we map the click-path: content → link-in-bio → free plan → list → offer. If a piece has no path, it's a deliberate nurture/relationship piece for the core, and we label it as such. No "just vibes" posts by accident. *(Iman: Intentional Brand.)*

4. **The warm list is the launch, not a footnote.** The Beehiiv list is the highest-priority asset in the business. We over-index on emailing it, we plug every leak in it, and we give it first crack at the first 100 spots. Reach is the slow second engine; the list is the launch. *(Iman inverted for cold start: No-Leak Backend + warm-list-first.)*

5. **One niche, owned completely, first.** Young Slovenian **football (nogomet) + basketball (košarka)** athletes, 16–26, speed & explosiveness. The enemy is generic bodybuilding splits. We do not say "for all athletes." We earn the right to expand by winning this segment first. *(Iman: Niche-Down-Then-Expand.)*

6. **Manual where it converts, automated where it plumbs.** At a few hundred leads, a personal DM out-converts any automated flow. We automate the plumbing (Beehiiv sequence, Stripe, abandonment, dunning) and we do the *closing* by hand. Ian talks to every lead — that's the unfair advantage a big operator has lost. *(Iman: adapted — he automates because of volume; we go manual because we can.)*

7. **Packaging is 80%.** The big idea + title + thumbnail + hook decide whether content is ever seen. We decide title and thumbnail *before* filming, not after. Thumbnails are engineering (CTR), not art. *(Iman: Packaging = 80%.)*

8. **Scarcity and claims stay honest.** Real, modest scarcity only — "prvih 100, cena zaklenjena za vedno" is true and enough. In a small market where everyone knows everyone, one whiff of a fake counter is permanent trust death. We never fake a number. *(Iman inverted: no scarcity theater.)*

9. **Disqualify openly.** We state clearly who ATHLOS is *not* for. It raises perceived value for free and filters the tire-kickers a solo founder can't afford to babysit in the DMs. *(Iman: Disqualification.)*

10. **Documentation over production.** Ian doesn't need studio gear or to be the world's best trainer (Tim covers the science). He needs to be one step ahead and radically transparent about building ATHLOS. Good audio + clean background + honesty beats a fancy edit. *(Iman: documentation-before-education.)*

## 1.4 The single source of truth — the content board

The whole system runs on **one board.** If it's not on the board, it doesn't exist. This is where an idea becomes a filmed asset becomes a measured result — the machine's dashboard. Build it in Notion (or a shared Trello/Airtable) before Week 1, and give the team edit access so Ian can film while the team moves cards.

### The stages (columns), idea → measured

```
 💡 IDEA ─▶ 📝 SCRIPTED ─▶ 🎥 FILMED ─▶ ✂️ EDITED ─▶ 📅 SCHEDULED ─▶ 🚀 POSTED ─▶ 📊 MEASURED
```

1. **💡 Idea** — a hook + the pillar + the ONE CTA it points to. If it has no CTA path, it's tagged `nurture` on purpose.
2. **📝 Scripted** — the Slovenian hook is written, and the **title + thumbnail concept are decided here** (packaging before filming — principle #7).
3. **🎥 Filmed** — raw footage in `01-raw-footage/`, named per convention. This is the only stage that *needs Ian.* Batched on one Content Day/week.
4. **✂️ Edited** — finished cut in `02-edits/`, caption drafted in Slovenian, thumbnail built. Team-owned, no Ian required.
5. **📅 Scheduled** — loaded into Metricool with date/time, cross-post targets ticked (Ian IG / @athlos IG / YT Short).
6. **🚀 Posted** — live. Link-in-bio and pinned comment both point to the opt-in. Archived to `06-published/`.
7. **📊 Measured** — the only metrics that matter get logged: **leads driven**, saves/shares, watch-through, replies. **Views are noted but never the verdict.** Winners get flagged for future rehashing.

### Card fields (every content card carries these)

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| **Pillar** | THE JOURNEY · Športna znanost (Tim) · Treniram kot športnik · Offer |
| **Account** | Ian · @athlos · both |
| **Format** | Reel/Short · YouTube long-form · Story · Carousel |
| **Goal** | `lead` (growth, points to offer) · `nurture` (relationship, core) |
| **CTA / path** | e.g. „link v biu → brezplačen načrt" |
| **Packaging** | title + thumbnail concept (locked before filming) |
| **Stage** | the 7 columns above |
| **Publish date** | native Slovenian format, e.g. 15. 7. 2026 |
| **Result** | leads driven · watch-through · replies (logged at 📊) |

**The board's non-negotiable rule:** every card must have a **Goal** (`lead` or `nurture`) filled in at the Idea stage. That single field enforces principles #2 and #3 — it forces the team to answer *"is this for leads or views?"* before any effort is spent. A card with no Goal doesn't move to Scripted.

Alongside the content board, keep the **lead log** (the Google Sheet: name / sport / source / pending vs confirmed / replied) and a simple **Hot / Warm / Cold** network sheet. Those two, plus the content board, are the three surfaces the whole system runs on.

## Blind-spot check — the cold start

Before the team executes any of this, internalize where Iman's model quietly assumes a scale we don't have — and what we do instead. **A setup that ignores the cold start is the wrong setup.**

| Iman assumes… | Our reality | So we… |
|---|---|---|
| Millions of views feed the top of the funnel | ~a dozen warm subs, near-zero cold audience | Treat the **warm Beehiiv list as the launch**; reach is the slow second engine, not launch fuel. |
| Enough audience to run many separate accounts + feeder army | One founder also shipping the app; no clipper budget | **Ian's account = ~80% of effort**; @athlos = lean proof repo; cross-post short-form to both from one Content Day. |
| A back-catalog exists to rehash 14×/week | Empty catalog on day 1 | **Rehashing is N/A at launch** — it becomes the #1 anti-burnout lever *after* month 1. Note it, don't lean on it yet. |
| A team of 30 runs the machine | A small team + Ian as the only face | Automate the plumbing, **close by hand**; the board lets the team run everything *except* being on camera. |
| A high-ticket price means a few thousand qualified views = big money | €29–89/mo B2C — **low ticket, needs volume** | We *do* need reach (unlike Iman's business channel), but it must stay **on-niche** (nogomet/košarka speed) and be judged by **leads, not views**. |
| Giant scarcity theater (prize pools, cars) sells | €0 budget, small honest market | **Real, modest scarcity only** — "prvih 100, za vedno." A fake counter here is permanent trust death. |

**The one blind spot to keep front of mind:** Iman can optimize reach-first because he monetizes later at scale. We have 30 days and a handful of believers who can *actually* seed the first 100. So the machine we build in this manual is warm-list-first, founder-face-led, and manual where it converts. Set the success bar to our scale — **5–20 founding members is a win**, not Iman's screenshots.

# 2 · What To Film — The Content System

Everything here obeys three rules inherited from the foundation docs and the Iman method:

1. **Ian is IN every piece.** No faceless logo content, no stock B-roll standing in for a founder. People follow a person building something, not a fitness app they've never heard of. If Ian isn't on camera or on the mic, it isn't ATHLOS founder content — it's a product asset (fine, but a different job).
2. **Reverse-engineer from the click.** Before anything gets filmed, we know the path: *this video → link in bio → free 7-day Tim plan (Beehiiv) → early-bird cart.* This is Iman's **Intentional Brand** — content is made backwards from the offer, never forwards from "what would get views."
3. **Cold-start honesty.** We are one founder-ish team also shipping the app, a warm list in the dozens, organic-only, ~30 days. So we shoot for **leads and depth**, not raw reach. Every format below is chosen because one person can actually film it this week with a phone.

## 2.1 The content pillars and the 3:1 ratio

### The spine: THE JOURNEY

Every pillar hangs off one spine — **THE JOURNEY: Ian building ATHLOS in public, training himself on it, racing to put it in the hands of the first 100 athletes.** The "dan X/30" countdown is the narrative thread that makes strangers care about a nobody's fitness app: it turns a product launch into a story with a clock on it. When in doubt what to film, film the journey.

### The three pillars (from `01-brand-foundation.md §3`)

| # | Pillar | What it is | What it's FOR | Growth or nurture |
|---|--------|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **1** | **THE JOURNEY — gradim ATHLOS v živo** | Behind-the-scenes of building the app, wins/fails, the "dan X/30" countdown, decisions, the founder's why. | Parasocial trust + waitlist signups. The spine every week returns to. This is what makes the audience feel like *early insiders*, which is exactly what converts them into founding-100 buyers. | **Nurture** (with growth spikes) |
| **2** | **ŠPORTNA ZNANOST z Timom — česar ti nihče ne pove** | Punchy, myth-busting, *sport-specific* performance science: speed, explosiveness, periodization, why generic splits ruin team-sport athletes. Tim is the credibility anchor. | Reach + proof of the science depth behind the app. Highest viral ceiling — "postal sem hitrejši" science travels. This pillar *recruits strangers* and *earns the right to sell*. | **Growth** |
| **3** | **TRENIRAM KOT ŠPORTNIK — dokaz na sebi** | Ian running ATHLOS's own generated programming on himself: the process, the numbers, the speed/explosiveness gains, the grind. | Living proof the product works, embodied. Turns "cool app" into "I want that." Relatability + demonstration in one. | **Growth + proof** |

### The 3:1 growth-to-nurture ratio (Iman's principle, cold-start-adjusted)

Iman's rule: **~3 parts growth, ~1 part nurture.** Growth content is broad, curiosity-driven, brings in strangers — *"makes you known."* Nurture content is lower-reach, higher-trust, deepens the core relationship — *"makes you money."* The universal mistake is stopping at growth and never converting attention into leads.

**For ATHLOS we run roughly 2:1, not 3:1** — and here's the honest reason (per `01 §3` and the funnel doc). At Iman's scale, nurture volume is small relative to a giant funnel, so he can afford 3:1. Ian's warm list is tiny, which makes **each nurture piece disproportionately valuable** — it's where the launch actually converts. So publicly we lean ~2 growth : 1 nurture, and privately we treat the **Beehiiv email list as the highest-priority nurture channel of all** (that lives in Chapter on the funnel — email carries the heaviest nurture load, freeing the feed to lean growth).

Practical weekly mix at ~9–12 pieces:
- **~6 growth** (Pillar 2 science + Pillar 3 proof + hot takes) — the reach engine.
- **~3 nurture** (Pillar 1 journey, founder story, day-in-the-life) — the trust/close engine.
- **Every single piece** ends in the same click-path. Growth pieces use a soft CTA ("link v biu, brezplačen načrt"); nurture pieces can go harder ("prvih 100, ne čakaj").

> **The tag that goes on everything:** before we film, we write two words on the shot list — the **pillar** (1/2/3) and the **job** (growth/nurture). If we can't name the job, we don't shoot it. That discipline *is* the Intentional Brand.

## 2.2 The video-type catalog — the menu Ian films from

This is the concrete format library. ### FORMAT 1 — Build-in-public "dan X/30"
- **What it is:** Raw phone update on where the build is today. One win, one struggle, one decision. Shot in ~60 seconds, no production.
- **Why it works:** Iman's **documentation-before-education** + the **Intentional Brand** — a countdown creates narrative tension and a reason to come back tomorrow. Strangers who watch "dan 6" feel obligated to see "dan 7."
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 1 · **nurture** (the spine).
- **Length / platform:** 30–60s · IG Reels + YT Shorts (cross-post), Stories.
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"Dan 9 od 30. Danes se mi je sesula cela baza vaj — pa sem vseeno bližje kot včeraj."*
  - *"30 dni, da spravim ATHLOS v roke prvih 100 športnikov. Poglej, kje sem danes."*

### FORMAT 2 — Myth-bust
- **What it is:** Name a belief every Slovenian gym kid holds, then demolish it with Tim's science. One myth per video.
- **Why it works:** **Problem Farming** (turns audience pain into an endless backlog) + a **clear enemy** (generic bodybuilding splits). Contrarian hooks earn watch-time; being *against* something is more shareable than being *for* something.
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 2 · **growth** (highest viral ceiling).
- **Length / platform:** 30–60s short + can expand to a 6–10 min YT long-form · IG + YT.
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"Zakaj te 'klasičen split' z Instagrama dela POČASNEJŠEGA (in kaj delati namesto tega)."*
  - *"Nogometaš, ki dela bench press ponedeljek–petek, dela največjo napako v karieri. Evo zakaj."*

### FORMAT 3 — Tim science cut-in
- **What it is:** Tim on camera for 20–40 seconds explaining ONE protocol or mechanism ("zakaj plyometrija", "kako periodizirati okoli tekem"). Ian frames it; Tim delivers it.
- **Why it works:** **Effortless front, best-in-class backend** — Tim's scarcity *is* his authority. He doesn't run an account or post daily; his rare appearances transfer credibility to Ian and the app. Trust flows Tim → app.
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 2 · **growth + proof**.
- **Length / platform:** 20–45s short (or a segment inside a long-form) · IG + YT.
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"Vprašal sem fiziologa Tima: zakaj večina športnikov nikoli ne postane hitrejših. Odgovor te bo presenetil."*
  - *"Tim razloži v 40 sekundah, kako pravilno narediti prvi korak eksploziven."*

### FORMAT 4 — Founder-trains-on-it proof
- **What it is:** Ian doing the actual ATHLOS-generated workout — sprint work, plyos, the session the app told him to do today — with the screen shown.
- **Why it works:** **Documentation** + embodied proof. The founder eating his own cooking is the least fakeable content there is. It answers "does this thing even work?" without a single claim.
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 3 · **growth + proof**.
- **Length / platform:** 30–60s short · IG + YT; also feeds long-form "teden X rezultati".
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"Aplikacija mi je danes napisala trening. Poglej, kako je videti, ko ga dejansko narediš."*
  - *"30 dni treniram samo po tem, kar generira moja lastna app. Teden 1: to se je zgodilo."*

### FORMAT 5 — Athlete transformation / results
- **What it is:** A real athlete's before/after — sprint time, vertical jump, a number that moved. Early on this is Ian himself; post-launch it's founding members and beta testers.
- **Why it works:** **Optimize for leads not views** — a visible, emotional, *measurable* outcome ("−0,2s na 20m") is the single most persuasive asset for a performance product. Speed is shareable in a way hypertrophy never is.
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 3 · **growth + proof** (converts hard).
- **Length / platform:** 30–60s short · IG + YT; testimonial version → sales page.
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"Pred 6 tedni je tekel 20m v 3,1s. Danes 2,9s. Nič čarobnega — samo pravi sistem."*
  - *"Ta košarkar je dvignil vertikal za 7cm v enem mesecu. Točno to je delal."*

### FORMAT 6 — "How to" tutorial
- **What it is:** One concrete, do-it-today technique — a drill, a warm-up, a cue. Pure value, screen-recordable or demonstrated.
- **Why it works:** **Minefield/search farming** — high-intent, durable content that compounds and ranks. Someone searching "kako hitreje sprintati" is a *qualified* lead, not a random scroller.
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 2 · **growth** (search-durable).
- **Length / platform:** 45–90s short + strong YT long-form candidate · IG + YT.
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"3 koraki do bolj eksplozivnega štarta — naredi jih pred vsakim treningom."*
  - *"Kako pravilno narediti drop jump (99% ga dela narobe)."*

### FORMAT 7 — Day-in-the-life
- **What it is:** A day of the founder-athlete: training, building the app, meeting Tim, the grind behind the launch.
- **Why it works:** Iman's classic **nurture** format ("a day in the life" = "makes you money"). Lower reach, high engagement, deep parasocial bond. This is the piece that converts a follower into a *fan*.
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 1 · **nurture**.
- **Length / platform:** 60–90s short or 5–8 min YT vlog · IG + YT.
- **SL hooks:**
  - *"En dan v življenju, ko gradiš športno app in jo treniraš na sebi hkrati."*
  - *"5:40 zjutraj. 12 dni do lansiranja. Poglej, kako izgleda moj dan."*

### FORMAT 8 — Hot take / opinion
- **What it is:** A strong, defensible opinion about Slovenian sport/fitness culture, said straight to camera. Take a side.
- **Why it works:** **Content trifecta** applied to *what you say* — opinion content is inherently high-watch-time because viewers stay to agree or argue. Establishes Ian's **FOR/AGAINST** positioning (against generic aesthetics culture).
- **Pillar / job:** Pillar 2 (culture) · **growth**.
- **Length / platform:** 30–50s short · IG + YT.
- **SL hooks:**

## 2.3 The core converting video — the Flywheel VSL

This is the **one long-form video** that argues a Slovenian athlete into wanting ATHLOS. Iman's **Flywheel VSL** principle: record ONE evergreen video, once; every other piece links back to it; it compounds into a 24/7 salesman.

### Iman's prerequisite — and Ian's shortcut

Iman says you **cannot** record the VSL cold: you build ~15–30 videos on the micro-channel first *to discover the right topic and angle.* **Ian's cold-start shortcut** (per `04 §5.1` and the `01` blind-spot table): the topic is already known — **it's the launch pitch itself.** We don't need 30 videos to find it. So we build it in Week 2–3, after ~10–15 short pieces have taught us which hooks land, and we fold those winning hooks straight into the VSL. We do **not** burn Week 1 on a VSL no one will see yet.

Right-sized: **aim 8–15 minutes**, not Iman's 38 — cold Slovenian channel, MVP offer, a founder-to-camera piece shot honestly, re-cut after launch.

### The title (SL)

> **"Zakaj generični treningi delajo slovenske športnike počasnejše — in kako to popravim (v živo, na sebi)."**

Alt / thumbnail-line variant: *"Zgradil sem strokovno ekipo, ki jo imajo samo veliki klubi — za ceno dveh treningov na mesec."*

### Beat-by-beat outline

1. **Hook (0:00–0:15) — the enemy, named.** *"Če treniraš nogomet ali košarko in slediš 'splitom' z Instagrama, te ta video reši ene največjih napak v karieri."* Promise + stakes in the first frame. (Packaging = 80%.)
2. **The problem (0:15–2:00) — Problem Farming.** The five ICP pains in their own words (`01 §2`): "ne vem, ali treniram prav", generic English PDFs, no periodization around the season, nobody programs for *your* sport, injuries from unmanaged load. Make the viewer feel *seen*. No pitch yet.
3. **Why it's not your fault (2:00–3:30).** The real cause: elite athletes have a *strokovna ekipa* — physiolog + trener + sezonski planer — that costs €200–500+/month in Slovenia. You've been fighting alone against people who have a team. Reframe: the problem is access, not effort.
4. **The idea (3:30–5:00) — the big reframe.** *"Kaj če bi vsak športnik imel to ekipo — kot AI sistem, ki pozna njegov šport, v slovenščini, za ceno dveh treningov?"* Introduce ATHLOS as the concept, Tim as the science behind it.
5. **Proof it's real (5:00–8:00) — Documentation + demo.** Screen-share the Coach generating a real sport-specific plan live. Cut to Ian doing the session. Cut to a Tim science soundbite. Cut to a number that moved. This is the "it works" block — show, don't claim.
6. **Who it's NOT for (8:00–9:00) — Disqualification.** State plainly: not for shortcut-seekers, aesthetics-only trainers, or people who want someone to train *for* them. This raises perceived value and makes the right-fit viewer feel chosen (`01 §4`).
7. **The offer + why now (9:00–11:00) — Anchoring + honest scarcity.** The €200–500 human-team anchor first, *then* the price: BASIC ~~€49~~ €29 / PRO ⭐ ~~€99~~ €59 / ELITE ~~€149~~ €89. The **first 100 lock the price for life.** Three converging "why now"s: AI is finally good enough, nobody's done this for Slovenian athletes, the door closes at 100.
8. **One CTA (11:00–end) — No-leak.** A single instruction: *"Povezava spodaj. Vpiši se na listo, dobiš brezplačen 7-dnevni Timov načrt — in prvo mesto med prvih 100."* One click-path, nothing else.

### How every other video links back to it

The VSL is the **gravitational center.** Iman's flywheel: every new piece feeds the VSL, the rising traffic gets the algorithm to amplify it, and it compounds ("the older it gets, the more it sells"). Concretely for ATHLOS:

- **Shorts** end with "cela zgodba v enem videu — link v biu / v opisu." The short is the hook; the VSL is the argument.
- **Email N4** ("poglej, kaj sem zgradil") links the VSL for the first time; **L1** (cart open) links it again.
- **Every bio and pinned comment** points to the VSL or the opt-in that follows it.
- **Post-launch**, when the channel has traffic, we run Iman's **content-rehashing** on it (new thumbnail + title only) to keep it climbing — but that's a month-2 lever (empty catalog now).

> **Blind-spot honesty (`04 §5.1`):** at launch the VSL's job is **not** to be found by strangers — it won't get millions of cold views. Its job is to be the trust-and-convert asset Ian links in *every email, every bio, every DM*. It converts the traffic Ian already routes to it. It becomes Iman's evergreen gravity center *later*, as the channel grows.

## 2.4 Short-form vs long-form — and the remix

### What belongs where

| | **Short-form (Reels / Shorts)** | **Long-form (YouTube)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Job** | Reach + top-of-funnel + daily presence. The hook that pulls a stranger in. | Depth + trust + the *convert*. The argument that closes. |
| **Formats** | 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14 — most of the menu. | The VSL (§2.3); occasional myth-bust or tutorial expanded to 6–10 min; day-in-the-life vlog. |
| **Cadence** | Daily-ish (~1/day, batch-filmed). | ~1 real video (the VSL) for launch; more post-launch. |
| **Platform** | IG Reels + YT Shorts (cross-post), TikTok optional. | YouTube, under **Ian's** channel (the buyer must trust the founder-athlete first). |
| **Cold-start note** | The reach engine, but a *slow trickle* from a cold start — seeds month 2+, doesn't carry launch. | One is enough for launch. Don't run a weekly YT machine yet. |

**Placement rule:** the founder-athlete's *face and daily presence* live in short-form; the *full argument* lives in the one long-form. Product-only assets (pure demos, testimonials) can also live on the @athlos repository account, but the person is always on the personal channel.

### How ONE long-form becomes ~8–10 shorts

Iman's **content-rehashing / repurposing** logic, right-sized. The VSL is shot as one continuous piece but *planned* so each beat is independently clippable. From the 8-beat VSL you extract:

1. The enemy hook (beat 1) → a myth-bust short.
2. Each of 2–3 ICP pains (beat 2) → 2–3 POV/relatable shorts.
3. The €200–500 "strokovna ekipa" reframe (beat 3) → one hot-take short.
4. The live Coach demo (beat 5) → a behind-the-app short.
5. Ian doing the session (beat 5) → a founder-proof short.
6. The Tim soundbite (beat 5) → a Tim science cut-in.
7. The disqualification block (beat 6) → a "ATHLOS ni za …" short.
8. The offer/why-now (beat 7) → a launch/scarcity short (held for launch week).

That's **~9 shorts from one shoot** — one Content Day yields a week-plus of feed.

> **Cold-start reality (from `00-START-HERE` §"Cut or defer"):** do **not** treat the 8–10-shorts math as a mandate this month — "nobody's watching enough yet." **Post the clips that are genuinely good, ignore the ones that aren't.** The remix engine is real but its full value arrives in month 2+ when there's an audience to catch the volume. For launch: one Content Day/week, cut 3 good shorts *if* it makes anything, and let the VSL do the heavy converting.

### The Content Day (Iman's batch principle)

Batch-film one day a week (Iman: "Content Day" — 2–5 pieces in one sitting). Good audio + a clean background beats fancy gear. One session: shoot the day's journey update, 2–3 myth-busts/tutorials, one Tim cut-in if Tim's there, one founder-training clip. Everything else that week is raw phone documentation (Format 1), which needs no setup.

# 3 · How To Film — The Production Playbook

This is the most hands-on chapter in the manual, and the most important one for the founder-as-face model. Everything upstream — positioning, the funnel, the offer — is worthless if we can't reliably put Ian on camera and produce content the team can turn into leads.

Read this as a build spec. By the end you should be able to walk into a session, shoot 5–10 pieces, and hand a clean folder of footage to editing without anyone improvising.

**The one rule that governs this whole chapter:** *Ian is the only one who can be on camera. Everyone else builds the machine around him.* We design every part of production so that Ian's job is small, repeatable, and low-friction — show up, be a person, talk — and the team absorbs all the complexity (kit, setup, shot lists, files, editing, packaging). If a decision makes Ian's on-camera job harder, it's the wrong decision. See §3.7.

**Iman lens:** Iman spends only 3–5 hours/month actually creating; his team does the rest ("effortless front, best-in-class backend"). We can't match that team yet — but we build toward it from day one by making Ian's part tiny and the pipeline around him as systematized as our size allows.

**Cold-start reality:** we are one founder also shipping the app, a tiny warm list, organic, ~30 days. We do NOT have a studio, a videographer, or a clipper army. This chapter is deliberately phone-first and batching-first, because a solo founder's scarcest resource is on-camera time. **Batching is the answer to that scarcity — it is the load-bearing idea of this chapter (§3.5).**

## 3.1 · The Kit — Phone-First Gear List

We shoot on a phone. Not "for now until we can afford a camera" — on purpose. A modern phone shoots better video than most people's cameras, it's always in Ian's pocket, and it removes every excuse to not film. Iman's own rule: *good audio + a clean background beats fancy gear.* Fancy gear is a way to procrastinate. We reject it.

The single biggest quality upgrade is not the camera — it's the **microphone**. Viewers forgive mediocre video; they will not forgive bad audio. They click away in the first two seconds if it sounds echoey or hollow. So the mic is the one place we spend money first (§3.2 explains why).

### The list (cheap vs better)

| Item | Cheap / start-today | Better (when there's budget) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Camera** | The phone Ian already owns (iPhone with 4K is ideal; any recent phone is fine) | Same phone — don't upgrade the camera before the mic | It's already good enough. Reject the camera-upgrade excuse. |
| **Microphone** | Wired lavalier (clip-on) into the phone (~€20–30), OR the phone's own mic in a small quiet room as a last resort | Wireless lav kit (DJI Mic Mini / Rode Wireless-type, ~€100–150) — clips on, no cable, moves with Ian | **The #1 upgrade.** Audio > video, always. A €25 lav beats a €1,000 camera with the built-in mic. |
| **Tripod / stability** | Cheap phone tripod + phone clamp (~€15), or lean the phone against books | Small tripod with a phone mount + a gimbal (DJI Osmo-type, ~€100) for walking/movement B-roll | Static talking-head = tripod. Movement/B-roll = gimbal. Shaky footage reads as amateur. |
| **Lighting** | A window (free — see §3.2). North-facing, soft daylight. | One softbox or a large ring/soft LED panel (~€40–80) for consistent indoor light regardless of weather | Consistent light = a recognizable, professional look across the batch. Window light is genuinely great — but it changes with the weather, which breaks batching consistency. |
| **Storage / files** | Phone's own storage, cleared before a session; back up to cloud (Google Drive / iCloud) after | A fast USB-C SSD or a paid cloud tier for 4K footage | 4K footage is huge. Running out of space mid-session kills a Content Day. Always start a session with space cleared. |

### "You can start today with just X"

**You can start today with: your phone, a €25 clip-on lav mic, a €15 tripod, and a window.** That's the entire minimum viable kit. Total under €50. Everything else on the list is an optimization we add later, not a prerequisite. If Ian is waiting on gear to start, he's procrastinating — the excuse is already dead.

## 3.2 · The Setup — How To Physically Shoot

This is the checklist the team sets up *before* Ian sits down. Ian should walk into a room where framing, background, light, and audio are already handled. His job is to talk; the setup is ours.

### Framing

- **Headroom:** leave a small, consistent gap above Ian's head — not too much (floating in space), not cropping the top of his head. A thumb's width at the top of frame.
- **Rule of thirds:** Ian's eyes sit on the **upper third** line, not dead-center. Turn on the phone's grid (Settings → Camera → Grid on iPhone) and place the eyes on that top horizontal line every time. This is the single fastest way to make a shot look intentional instead of like a webcam.
- **Eye-line:** Ian looks slightly into the lens, not down at it. The phone should be at roughly **eye height or a touch above** — never below (shooting up the nose is unflattering and reads amateur). Prop the tripod up to eye level.
- **Distance:** medium-close. Chest-up for talking-head. Close enough to feel personal and intimate, not a security-camera wide shot. For vertical (Reels/Shorts), frame a little tighter — head and shoulders — because phones are held close.

### Background — what reads well

We have three environments, each with a job:

- **Gym / training floor** *(the hero environment).* This is where ATHLOS content should live most of the time — it's on-brand, it's proof, it signals "athlete, not influencer at a desk." Racks, turf, a court in the background. Depth behind Ian (don't back him flat against a wall — a few meters of gym behind him gives professional depth-of-field). This is the default for pillars 2 (science with Tim) and 3 (Ian training).
- **Home / clean indoor** *(THE JOURNEY / build-in-public).* A tidy, simple background — a plain wall with one or two intentional objects (a plant, a ball, nothing cluttered). This is where the founder-story, "dan X/30," and build-in-public talking-heads live. Clean beats fancy. Kill visual clutter — a messy background is the fastest way to look unprofessional.
- **Outdoors** *(reach / atmosphere).* A track, a pitch, a court, a stadium exterior. Great for hooks and B-roll, high energy, but **only shoot outdoors when the weather cooperates** — wind ruins audio (a lav windscreen/foam cover helps) and changing light breaks consistency. Treat outdoors as a bonus environment, not the reliable one.

**Rule:** pick a background that says "athlete" and has depth, and keep it consistent within a batch so the pieces feel like a series.

### Lighting

- **Natural window light is the free hero.** Ian faces the window (light on his face), never with the window behind him (that silhouettes him — the phone exposes for the bright window and Ian goes dark). Soft, indirect daylight from a north-facing window is flattering and free.
- **Ring light or soft LED panel** for consistency when daylight isn't available or when batching across hours (the sun moves; a lamp doesn't). Position it in front of Ian, slightly above eye level, angled down — mimicking natural overhead light.
- **Avoid:** harsh overhead ceiling lights alone (they cast raccoon shadows under the eyes); mixed color temperatures (a warm lamp + cool daylight makes skin look sickly — pick one); backlighting that silhouettes; and any flickering light (cheap LEDs can strobe on camera).

### Audio — why audio beats video

If we get one thing right, it's this. **Bad audio makes people leave in the first two seconds; mediocre video does not.** The brain tolerates a soft image; it will not tolerate echo, hiss, or hollow room sound. Concretely:

- **Use the lav mic, always.** Clip it on Ian's shirt, roughly a hand's width below the chin, centered. Hide the cable if wired. Test-record 5 seconds and play it back before the real take — every session.
- **Shoot in a quiet, soft room.** Hard empty rooms echo. Rooms with soft furnishings (couch, curtains, rugs, clothes) sound better. Turn off fans, AC, the fridge hum, notifications. A gym can be echoey and loud — for talking-head *in* a gym, still rely on the lav close to the mouth, and shoot when the gym is quiet.
- **Never rely on the phone's built-in mic for talking-head** unless you're in a genuinely small, soft, silent room and have no lav. The lav is the whole difference between "amateur" and "clean."

### Phone settings (set these once, lock them per session)

- **Resolution / fps:** 4K at 30fps for the standard look. 4K gives editing room to crop and reframe (important for cutting one horizontal long-form into vertical shorts — §3.6). Use 60fps only when you specifically want slow-motion B-roll (training clips).
- **Lock exposure and focus:** on iPhone, tap-and-hold on Ian's face until "AE/AF LOCK" appears. This stops the shot from hunting — auto-exposure drifting brighter/darker mid-sentence, or focus breathing in and out, both scream amateur. Lock it on the face before every take.
- **Orientation — per platform:**
  - **Vertical (9:16)** for Reels, Shorts, TikTok — this is where most of our reach lives, so it's the default for short-form.
  - **Horizontal (16:9)** for the YouTube long-form / VSL — the core converting video (per `04-funnel-sales-system.md §5.1`) is shot horizontal.
  - **The pro move:** shoot the long-form horizontal in 4K, framed with enough room around Ian that editing can *crop it to vertical* for shorts. One shoot, both formats (§3.6).

## 3.3 · On-Camera Delivery — How Ian Should Present

This is the part only Ian can do, so it deserves the most care. The goal is not "be a polished presenter." The goal is **be a real, high-conviction person the viewer trusts.** In a small Slovenian market where authenticity is the whole moat (per `01-brand-foundation.md §3` — Ian is the founder-athlete, the person IS the moat), polish that reads as "influencer" actually *hurts* us. We want one-of-one, not one-of-many.

**Iman lens:** packaging and the first seconds are 80% of the game; and the founder's job is to be *known and trusted*, not slick. Ian doesn't need to perform — he needs to be *the guy building this, on camera.*

### Energy

- Bring energy **one notch above** how you feel in a normal conversation. The camera flattens energy — what feels like "a bit much" in the room reads as "normal and engaged" on screen. Flat, low-energy delivery is the #1 reason good content dies.
- Not fake-hyped, not a caffeine ad — just *lean in, mean it.* Ian genuinely cares about this (he's building it and training on it); let that conviction show.

### The first-3-seconds hook rule

The first three seconds decide whether anyone watches the rest. No slow intros, no "hey guys, welcome back," no logo animation. Open on the most interesting sentence. State the hook, on camera, immediately — or lead with a visual that stops the scroll.

Slovenian hook patterns Ian can open cold with (idiomatic, spoken, not written):

- *"Če treniraš nogomet in delaš klasičen 'split' iz Instagrama — delaš se počasnejšega. Razložim."*
- *"Nihče v Sloveniji ti ne pove tega o hitrosti. Zato sem zgradil aplikacijo, ki ti."*
- *"Dan 12 od 30. Danes se je skoraj vse sesulo."*
- *"To je največja napaka, ki jo dela vsak mlad košarkaš pri treningu eksplozivnosti."*
- *"Osebni trener + kineziolog + fizioterapevt = 400 evrov na mesec. Ali pa to."*
- *"Ne verjameš, da lahko AI naredi pravi trening za tvoj šport? Poglej."*

The pattern: name the enemy, or promise a specific payoff, or open mid-story — in the first sentence, before anything else.

### Pacing

- Talk a little faster than feels natural and **cut every dead moment in editing** (§3.6). Short-form has no room for "umm," pauses, or throat-clearing.
- One idea per breath. Deliver a point, breathe, next point. This gives editing clean cut points.
- Vary the rhythm — a fast run of sentences, then one slow, weighted line for emphasis. Monotone pacing loses people even if the content is good.

### Eye contact with the lens

- Look at **the lens itself**, not at your own face on the screen. Put a tiny sticker or arrow next to the lens as a reminder. Looking at the screen means looking slightly off — the viewer feels talked-past instead of talked-to.
- Direct lens eye contact is what creates the parasocial "he's talking to me" feeling that builds trust and, downstream, leads.

### Talk like a person, not a presenter

- Speak the way Ian would explain this to a training partner in the gym, not the way he'd narrate a corporate video. Contractions, real Slovenian, the odd bit of slang the athlete audience uses.
- **Read vs speak:** do NOT read a script word-for-word off the screen — it always looks read (the eyes track, the delivery flattens). Instead: know the 3 beats you want to hit (hook → point → CTA), then *talk* through them naturally. Bullet points on a note, not a paragraph to recite. The one exception is the tightly-scripted hook line — that's worth nailing verbatim.
- If a take comes out stiff, say the line out loud once as if venting to a friend, then roll — the second, looser version is almost always the keeper.

### Hands and gestures

- Use hands naturally — gesturing on camera reads as confidence and energy, and it's how Ian talks anyway. Frozen, hands-at-sides delivery looks nervous.
- Keep gestures within the frame (don't wave out of shot). For chest-up framing, gestures near the chest/face read best.

### Retakes

- **Retakes are free and expected.** Fluff a line? Don't stop, don't sigh, don't restart the whole thing — pause for one beat and say the line again. Editing keeps the good take and cuts the flub. This keeps energy up and avoids the death spiral of "let me start over" (attempt 8 is always worse than attempt 2).
- Cap it: if a piece won't come out right after ~3 tries, move on and come back. Grinding one clip kills the whole Content Day's momentum.

### Do / Don't

**DO:**
- Bring energy one notch up.
- Hook in the first 3 seconds.
- Look at the lens.
- Talk like you're in the gym, not on stage.
- Use your hands.
- Retake the line, not the whole clip.
- Be specific and opinionated — take a side (against generic splits, against aesthetics-only training).

**DON'T:**
- Don't open slow ("hey guys, so today…").
- Don't read a script off the screen.
- Don't go monotone or low-energy.
- Don't look at your own image instead of the lens.
- Don't try to be a polished influencer — be the real founder-athlete.
- Don't restart the whole take over one flubbed word.
- Don't be vague or "balanced" — vague content converts nobody.

## 3.4 · B-Roll & Visuals — Show, Don't Just Tell

Talking-head alone is fine, but the pieces that travel *show* something. B-roll (supplementary footage cut over the talking) does three jobs: it holds attention (visual change every few seconds), it hides edit cuts, and — most importantly for us — it delivers **product proof**. "Show, don't just tell" is not a style choice here; it's how we prove a cold audience that the app is real.

### What to grab (a standing shot list to collect whenever possible)

- **Training clips of Ian:** sprints, plyometrics, court/pitch work, lifts. Shoot these in 4K/60fps so editing can slow-mo the explosive moments. This is proof for pillar 3 ("treniram kot športnik") and endlessly reusable B-roll.
- **App screen-recordings (the highest-value B-roll we have):** record the phone screen showing the ATHLOS app *working* — the Coach generating a real plan, the daily report, biometrija, the season calendar. This is the product-proof that turns "cool idea" into "I want that." Capture clean screen-recordings deliberately, not as an afterthought.
- **Tim / science footage:** Tim explaining or demonstrating a protocol, whiteboard/diagram moments, anything that visually signals real expertise (pillar 2).
- **Sport footage / atmosphere:** the pitch, the court, the gym filling up, a ball, cleats, the environment. Establishes the world and covers cuts.
- **Hands / detail shots:** lacing boots, chalking up, tapping through the app on a phone. Detail shots make an edit feel produced.

### The product-proof rule

Every time we make a claim about the app ("it knows your sport," "it periodizes around your season," "it adapts"), we should have footage of the app **doing exactly that** to cut over the claim. Capturing product-proof screen-recordings is a standing task, not a one-off — build a small library of them so any editor can pull the right clip. A claim over a shot of the feature working is worth ten claims over a talking head.

## 3.5 · The Content Day / Batching SOP

This is the operational heart of the founder-as-face model. Ian's scarcest resource is on-camera time — he's also building the app. So we do **not** film daily. We batch: **one Content Day per week, produce 5–10 pieces in one session.** Set up once, dress once, get into camera-energy once, and walk out with a week-plus of content. This is the direct answer to the solo-founder time blind spot.

**Iman lens:** the Content Day is Iman's explicit beginner system — batch-film in one focused block, let the team handle the rest. We run it as a literal repeatable SOP so it survives Ian being busy.

### Step-by-step SOP

**PHASE A — Prep (team does this BEFORE the day; Ian just approves)**

1. **Build the shot list.** The team assembles a numbered list of the 5–10 pieces to film: each with its hook line (Slovenian, written out), the 3 beats, the pillar it serves (JOURNEY / science / training), the CTA, and the intended format (vertical short vs horizontal long). Ian reviews it the day before — his only prep job.
2. **Group by setup.** Reorder the shot list so all pieces sharing a background/outfit/location are filmed back-to-back. Filming order follows *setup*, not topic — never tear down and rebuild a setup mid-day.
3. **Plan outfits.** Ian brings 2–3 different tops (and swaps mid-day) so batch-filmed pieces don't all look identical when posted across a week — otherwise viewers notice it was all shot in one session. Keep each outfit consistent within its block.
4. **Confirm locations & timing.** Book the gym/court for a quiet window. If outdoors is on the list, check the weather and slot it when light is good.
5. **Charge and clear.** Phone charged, storage cleared (enough free space for 4K), mic batteries charged, backup power ready.

**PHASE B — Setup (team, ~15 min at the start of the session)**

6. **Rig the shot.** Tripod at eye height, framing per §3.2 (grid, thirds, headroom), background set with depth, light in front of Ian, mic clipped on.
7. **Lock settings.** 4K/30 (or /60 for training), grid on, exposure + focus locked on Ian's face.
8. **Audio + framing test.** Record 5 seconds, play it back — check the audio is clean and the framing is right. Do NOT skip this. Discovering bad audio *after* a 3-hour session is a catastrophe.

**PHASE C — Filming (Ian on, team runs it)**

9. **Warm up.** First one or two clips are throwaways to get Ian into camera-energy. Don't publish clip #1.
10. **Film in setup-blocks.** Work through the shot list in order. For each piece: team reads the number and hook, Ian delivers, team confirms it's usable (or calls a retake), then moves to the next. Ian never manages the list — the team does; Ian just performs.
11. **Grab the B-roll (§3.4)** while everything is already set up — training clips, app screen-recordings, detail shots. Never leave a session without B-roll.
12. **Slate lightly.** Team notes which take is the keeper for each numbered piece (a quick note, or Ian claps/says the number) so editing isn't hunting through 40 clips.

**PHASE D — File handling & handoff (team, immediately after)**

13. **Back up first, immediately.** Offload all footage from the phone to two places (cloud + a drive) *before* clearing the phone. Never delete originals until the backup is verified. Losing a Content Day to a full phone or a lost file is unacceptable.
14. **Organize into a folder** named by date, e.g. `2026-07-11-content-day/`, with subfolders `talking-head/`, `b-roll/`, `app-screens/`. Match clips to the shot-list numbers.
15. **Hand off to editing** with the shot list attached — each piece's number, hook, keeper-take note, format, caption direction, and CTA. Editing should never have to guess intent.
16. **Log it** on the content calendar: what was shot, what it becomes, when each piece publishes across the coming week(s).

**The output of one Content Day:** 5–10 talking-head pieces + a batch of B-roll + app-proof clips → enough for a week-plus of posting, all handed to editing in a clean folder. That is the entire founder-time cost of a week of content: one session.

## 3.6 · Editing & Packaging

Editing turns raw footage into scroll-stopping pieces; packaging decides whether anyone sees them at all. Per the Iman lens, **packaging is ~80% of the outcome** — so we treat the thumbnail and title as more important than the edit itself.

### The editing style (short-form, Slovenian)

- **Fast pace, no dead air.** Cut every pause, "umm," and breath. Short-form should feel a touch faster than comfortable. If a moment isn't earning its place, cut it.
- **Captions / subtitles are MANDATORY** for Slovenian short-form. Most people watch on mute; without burned-in Slovenian captions, the piece is dead on arrival. Auto-generate then **hand-correct** — auto-captions butcher Slovenian, and wrong subtitles look careless. Style them big, bold, high-contrast, one or two words highlighted as spoken.
- **Hook on screen in the first second.** Put the hook as text on screen immediately, backing up the spoken hook — it stops the scroll for muted viewers.
- **B-roll cutaways every few seconds** (§3.4) — cut to training, app screens, detail shots over the talking. Visual change holds attention and hides edit cuts.
- **Keep it tight.** For short-form, shorter usually wins — cut to the shortest version that still lands the point and the CTA.

### The tools

- **CapCut** for short-form — fast, mobile or desktop, good auto-captions (still hand-correct the Slovenian), easy for the team to run without a pro editor. This is the default.
- **Premiere / DaVinci Resolve** for the long-form / VSL — the core converting video deserves a proper edit. Optional; CapCut can carry the whole operation at cold-start scale.

### Packaging = 80%

For anything with a thumbnail (YouTube long-form, some Reels covers), the thumbnail and title do most of the work. Two disciplines:

**Thumbnail — the Rule of Three** (Iman: thumbnail is engineering, not art — optimize for click-through, ignore "pretty"). A strong thumbnail has exactly three elements:
1. **Ian's face with a genuine, readable emotion** (intense, surprised, determined).
2. **Big, readable text** — 3–5 words max, legible on a phone screen.
3. **One context visual** — the app on screen, a training moment, a "vs" comparison.

**Title discipline:** decide the title + thumbnail *before* filming (Iman's hard-won lesson — package first, shoot second), so the piece is built to deliver on the packaging. Specific and curiosity-driven, never generic.

**Three example SL title + thumbnail concepts:**

1. **Title:** *"Zakaj te 'klasičen split' dela POČASNEJŠEGA (nogomet)"*
   **Thumbnail:** Ian mid-sprint, intense face · text "POČASNEJŠI?" · small inset of a crossed-out Instagram workout split.
2. **Title:** *"Zgradil sem AI trenerja za slovenske športnike. Poglej, kako dela."*
   **Thumbnail:** Ian holding the phone toward camera, app plan visible on screen · text "AI TRENER" · gym behind him.
3. **Title:** *"30 dni treniram po svoji aplikaciji. Teden 1."*
   **Thumbnail:** Ian mid-plyo jump, determined · text "DAN 1 → 30" · the ATHLOS report visible as the context visual.

### One long-form → many shorts

The long-form (or VSL) is the mother asset. Because it was shot horizontal in 4K with room around Ian (§3.2), editing crops it to vertical and pulls out the self-contained moments — a strong hook, a punchy science point, a proof moment — as standalone shorts.

**Cold-start honesty:** per `00-START-HERE.md`, we do NOT run the full 10-shorts-from-one-video remix engine yet — nobody's watching enough for the math to matter, and it's editing time we don't have. We cut the **genuinely good** moments into a handful of shorts and ignore the quota. The remix engine is a month-2+ scaling lever once there's an audience and a back-catalog (Iman's "Content Rehashing" — noted, not used at launch).

## 3.7 · The Founder-as-Face Guardrail

This is the line that makes the whole system work. Draw it explicitly so nobody blurs it.

### ONLY Ian can do:

- **Be on camera** — his face, his presence, his energy. This is un-delegable. It's the entire trust engine (`01-brand-foundation.md §3`: the person IS the moat).
- **Be the voice** — the opinions, the takes, the founder story, THE JOURNEY. The point of view is Ian's; it can't be ghost-performed.
- **Show up to the Content Day and perform** — deliver the hooks, do the retakes, grab the training B-roll.
- **Approve the shot list** the day before (his one prep task) and, ultimately, own that the content sounds like *him*.

That's it. That is Ian's entire job in this pipeline.

### The team does EVERYTHING else:

- Builds and researches the shot list and hooks (§3.5 Phase A).
- Owns all the kit, setup, framing, lighting, audio rig, phone settings (§3.1–3.2).
- Runs the Content Day — reads hooks, calls retakes, manages the list, tracks keeper-takes (§3.5 Phase C).
- Handles all files: backup, organization, handoff (§3.5 Phase D).
- Does all editing, captioning, B-roll cutaways (§3.6).
- Does all packaging — thumbnails, titles, the Rule of Three (§3.6).
- Schedules, posts, and tracks everything on the content calendar.

**The design principle:** every task that *can* be lifted off Ian, *is* lifted off Ian — so his scarce on-camera time is the only thing he spends on marketing. The more the team absorbs, the more content Ian can be the face of without it eating the time he needs to ship the app.

### Blind-spot check — the solo-founder time trap

The honest risk: at cold-start we may not have a full team yet, and some of "the team does everything else" lands back on Ian by default. **Batching is the mitigation.** By compressing all of Ian's on-camera time into one weekly Content Day, we cap his marketing time-cost to a single predictable block — even if he's temporarily also doing some editing. As the team fills in (per `09-team-marketing-protocol.md`), each pipeline task moves off Ian in priority order: first the file-handling and scheduling, then editing/captions, then packaging, then shot-list research — until Ian's only job really is *show up on Content Day and be the face.* That is the target state, and this chapter is the spec for reaching it.

**One-sentence version:** *Ian films one focused Content Day a week and is the face and the voice; the team builds, shoots-around, edits, packages, and posts everything else — and batching is what keeps the founder's time-cost to that single weekly block.*

## 4.1 Platform roles — who does what, and the interlock

Every platform and every account has exactly ONE job. This is Iman's **Content Siloing** applied honestly at cold-start scale: we don't split effort evenly across a dozen surfaces, we assign each surface a single purpose so nothing is diluted and Ian always knows *why* he's posting a given thing.

### The account map

| Surface | Account | Single job | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram (Ian)** | personal — **the priority, ~80% of effort** | Trust engine. Daily founder presence, THE JOURNEY, the face people follow. | First person, human, opinionated — "jaz" |

TikTok's Slovenian discovery is real but thinner for this niche and the algorithm won't reward a cold account fast enough to justify dedicated production in 30 days — so it's a free cross-post, never a third machine.

### The personal ↔ app interlock (the hand-off moves)

This is the load-bearing part of the whole chapter. Attention that lands on Ian is worthless unless it moves to the app account and then to the waitlist. These are the **exact mechanical hand-offs** that move it. Use them deliberately — every one of them is a place a lead leaks if it's missing.

1. **The spoken outro (Ian → waitlist).** Every long-form and most Reels end with Ian saying it out loud, not just captioning it. Signature line, used verbatim every time so it compounds recognition (Iman's **signature style** motif):
   > *"Gradim ATHLOS — link je v biografiji. Prvih 100 zaklene ceno za vedno."*
   Spoken > written: people skip captions, they don't skip the last spoken line if the video earned the watch.

2. **The pinned comment (every post, both accounts).** The first comment is pinned by the poster and is *always* the same click-path — the free-plan opt-in, never a bare "check my app." On Ian's posts it reads as a gift; on @athlos it reads as the offer. See the first-comment formula in §4.3.

3. **Instagram Collab posts (Ian ⨯ @athlos).** When Ian posts a piece that features the product or Tim, publish it as an **IG Collab** so it appears natively on *both* grids and both audiences' feeds from one upload. This is the single cheapest way to seed the @athlos account without running it as a separate machine — Ian's reach carries the app account's early growth.

4. **Story chains (Ian → @athlos → link).** Ian's Story is the daily bridge. A chain runs: Story 1 = the human moment/hook → Story 2 = "poglej, kaj sem danes zgradil" pointing at @athlos → Story 3 = the **link sticker** to the opt-in. Ian **@-mentions @athlos** in the chain so a tap moves the viewer onto the app account's profile. Re-share @athlos's proof/testimonial posts to Ian's Story so the app account borrows Ian's audience.

5. **Link-in-bio (the one destination).** Both bios point to the **same** opt-in page (the free 7-day Tim plan → Beehiiv). Not a link tree with ten options — **one link, one decision.** Ian's bio: soft, "delam na tem." @athlos bio: hard, "prvih 100." Both buttons, same page. Every extra link on that page is a leak.

**The interlock in one line:** *Ian earns the attention → the spoken outro + Story chain + @-mention move it to @athlos → the pinned comment + link-in-bio move it to the waitlist.* If a piece of content has none of these hand-offs, it's a pure nurture piece for the core (fine — but know it, per Iman's **Intentional Brand**: map the click-path before you film, or don't film it).

## 4.3 The short-form engine — one Content Day → ~8–10 clips everywhere

This is the production heart. Iman's principle: **you are building assets, not running on a treadmill.** One filming session becomes a week of distribution. Here is the exact flow.

### From one Content Day to the feed

1. **Film once (Monday).** In one session Ian shoots one longer talk-to-camera piece (5–12 min — the "core" idea of the week) plus 3–5 standalone short setups (a hook + one point each). Raw phone footage is fine; the standard is *clarity and energy*, not production polish.
2. **Cut to ~8–10 vertical clips.** From the long piece, pull the 3–5 strongest self-contained moments (a myth busted, a number, a before/after, a hot take). Add the 3–5 standalone shorts. That's **~8–10 clips from one day.**
3. **Route the clips** (this is the honest cold-start version of Iman's remix engine — post the clips that are *genuinely good*, don't force the full 10× math when nobody's watching enough yet):
   - **Best 3–5 → Ian's IG Reels** across the week.
   - **Same clips → Ian's YouTube Shorts** (identical file, but a search-optimized title — see §4.4).
   - **1–2 product/Tim clips → @athlos as IG Collab** (appears on both grids from one upload).
   - **Overflow → TikTok** if it's a 30-second cross-post, skip otherwise.
   - **The long piece → YouTube long-form** only on VSL weeks or when it's genuinely a standalone build-in-public episode; otherwise it's just clip fuel.
4. **Every clip carries the three hand-off elements:** on-screen hook (first frame), spoken outro, pinned first-comment CTA.

**Do NOT re-edit per platform.** Same vertical clip, same subtitles, different *title/caption* per platform. Re-editing is the treadmill; re-titling is the asset move. (Post-launch, when a back-catalog exists, this is exactly where Iman's **Content Rehashing** kicks in — new title/thumbnail on an old clip to unlock latent reach at ~zero cost. Note it now; it becomes the #1 anti-burnout lever in month 2, not week 1.)

### The caption formula (Slovenian) — used on every clip

A caption is not a description, it's a **second hook + a reason to act.** Structure, top to bottom:

```
[HOOK LINE — one punchy sentence that restates the video's promise]

[1–3 SHORT VALUE LINES — the takeaway, so even a non-watcher learns something]

[CTA LINE — always the same click-path, pointing at the pinned comment/bio]

[3–5 niche hashtags — see §4.4]
```

**Drop-in example (a speed/explosiveness clip):**

> Tvoj "klasičen split" iz Instagrama te dela POČASNEJŠEGA. 🧵
>
> Nogomet in košarka ne potrebujeta bicepsov za pozo — potrebujeta eksplozivnost, hitrost spremembe smeri in moč, ki se prenese na igrišče. Generični program tega ne da.
>
> Napisal sem ti brezplačen 7-dnevni Timov načrt za tvoj šport → **link v prvem komentarju.** Prvih 100 zaklene ceno za vedno.
>
> #nogomet #košarka #hitrost #eksplozivnost #športnitrening

### On-screen hooks (the first frame)

The first frame decides watch-through, and watch-through decides reach (Iman: **packaging is 80%**). Rules:
- **Text on the very first frame**, big and readable on a phone, high contrast, safe from the UI overlay.
- It states a **specific, sport-named** promise or provokes, e.g. *"Zakaj si počasnejši, čeprav treniraš več"*, *"To dela vsak slovenski nogometaš narobe"*, *"Postal sem hitrejši v 3 tednih — tukaj je kako"*.
- Name the **enemy** where possible (generic bodybuilding splits) — it's the sharpest hook we have and it's ownable.
- **Never** a vague hook ("moji nasveti za trening"). Specific + sport-named beats clever every time in a small market.

### The first-comment CTA (pinned, every post)

Pin the first comment yourself the moment you post. It's the click-path that IG's link-suppression on captions can't kill. Formula:

> 👇 Brezplačen 7-dnevni načrt za tvoj šport (Tim Drenovc) — link v biografiji. Napiši mi, kateri šport treniraš, in ti povem, kje začeti.

That last line — *"napiši mi, kateri šport treniraš"* — is deliberate: it invites a comment (which boosts the post) **and** opens a DM-qualification thread (§4.5). The comment is a funnel entrance, not an afterthought.

## 4.4 Discovery / SEO — being found by searching athletes in a tiny market

We have no ad budget and a small audience, so **being findable** is disproportionately valuable — a searchable clip keeps working for years. Iman calls this **minefield / search farming**: durable, low-view, high-intent content that compounds. In a 2.1M market, a Short that ranks for "hitrejši v nogometu" doesn't need a million views — it needs the few hundred *right* Slovenian athletes who search that exact thing every year.

### YouTube (where search actually pays)

Treat YouTube as a search engine, not a feed. For **every Short and every long-form**:

- **Title = the exact query an athlete would type**, in Slovenian, front-loaded with the keyword:
  - Shorts: *"Kako postati hitrejši za nogomet (brez teka na dolge proge)"*, *"Košarka: skočnost v 6 tednih"*, *"Zakaj te split dela počasnega"*.
  - Long-form / VSL: *"ATHLOS — kako trenirati kot profesionalni športnik (slovenski sistem)"*.
- **Description = the search-farmed body.** First two lines repeat the keyword and the promise (that's what shows in search). Then a short paragraph in natural Slovenian using the athlete's own words (from the ICP pains: *"ne vem, ali sploh treniram pravilno"*, *"nihče ne trenira specifično za moj šport"*). End with the **link to the opt-in** and the offer line.
- **Tags:** `nogomet trening`, `košarka trening`, `hitrost`, `eksplozivnost`, `skočnost`, `sprint`, `atletska priprava`, `športna priprava slovenija`, `kondicijski trening`, `Tim Drenovc`, `ATHLOS`.
- **Consistent titling convention** so the channel reads as *the* Slovenian athletic-performance library — the thing that shows up when a coach or athlete searches in Slovenian and finds nothing else serious.

### Instagram (keywords > hashtags now, but use both)

IG search increasingly reads **captions and on-screen text as keywords**, so:
- Put the real query **in the caption and on-screen text** in plain Slovenian ("hitrost", "eksplozivnost", "priprava na sezono", "nogomet", "košarka") — not just in hashtags.
- **Hashtags (3–5, niche not broad):** `#nogomet` `#košarka` `#slovenskišport` `#hitrost` `#eksplozivnost` `#športnitrening` `#kondicija` `#atletskapriprava`. Avoid mega-tags like `#fitness` (2 billion posts, we drown). A tag with tens of thousands of Slovenian posts is where our athlete actually browses.
- **Geo + club language:** name Slovenian places and the sport's own vocabulary (*"za slovenske nogometaše"*, *"pred sezono"*, *"na igrišču"*) so the algorithm and human search both file us under the right, small, high-intent bucket.

### The search-farming mindset (blind-spot honesty)

We are **not** chasing virality — the market is too small and the buyer too specific for a viral hit to convert well (a motivational video with 200K views from Serbia and Croatia sells zero €59 Slovenian subscriptions). We are planting **durable, sport-specific, Slovenian-language answers** to questions our exact buyer types into a search bar. That's the reach lever that survives a founder who can only post a few times a week: it keeps working while Ian builds the app.

## 4.5 Community & DMs — the daily ritual that IS the funnel

At our scale, **engagement is not "support the content" — engagement is the sales team.** Iman automates because he has volume; we do the opposite (per `04` §0): we go **manual where it converts.** At tens-to-low-hundreds of leads, a personal DM out-converts any automated flow. This is Ian's unfair advantage — he can talk to *every single lead* by hand. A big operator can't.

### The daily engagement ritual (30–45 min, every day, posted or not)

Run this every day regardless of whether content went out:

1. **Reply to every comment. Every one.** No exceptions while the audience is small. A reply is free reach, a warmth signal, and often a DM opener. Ask a question back where natural (turns a comment into a thread).
2. **Reply to every DM and every Story reply personally.** No autoresponders. Ian, as himself.
3. **Go outbound, 15 min:** engage genuinely on the posts of Slovenian nogomet/košarka athletes, club pages, and the accounts our ICP already follows (§4.6). Real comments, not "🔥". This is how a cold account gets discovered by the right people — you show up in their world first.
4. **Log warm threads.** Any athlete who replies, comments meaningfully, or opts in → into the Hot/Warm/Cold spreadsheet (from `00-START-HERE.md` lever #1). These are the humans Ian DMs at launch.

### The DM-qualification move (pull a new follower/commenter → waitlist)

When someone new comments, replies to a Story, or follows after a good post, Ian opens a **short, human, non-salesy** thread whose only goal is to hand them the free plan and get an email. Never a wall of text, never a pitch. The move:

**Opener (after they comment/follow):**
> Ej, hvala za komentar! 🙌 Kateri šport treniraš?

**They answer (e.g. "nogomet"):**
> Top. Ravno delam brezplačen 7-dnevni načrt za nogometaše — hitrost + eksplozivnost, napisal ga je Tim (fiziolog). Ti ga pošljem?

**They say yes:**
> Super — vpiši se tukaj [link], da ti ga pošljem takoj: [opt-in]. In povej, kje te trening zdaj najbolj ustavlja — mogoče ti lahko pomagam že prej.

**If they engage but don't opt in:** don't push. Give one piece of genuine free value in the DM (a single cue for their sport), leave the door open. A helped athlete opts in later; a pitched one blocks you. (This is Iman's **disqualification** instinct inverted for warmth — we filter tire-kickers by *not* chasing, and we make the serious ones feel personally coached.)

### Turning followers into fans (parasocial trust)

- **Consistency of face + signature line** (the spoken outro) builds recognition — same person, same promise, every time.
- **Use their words back to them.** When Ian names the exact pain an athlete DM'd him ("ne veš, ali treniraš pravilno") in the next Reel, that athlete becomes a fan — they feel *seen*.
- **Founding-member framing in every interaction:** the early people aren't customers-to-be, they're *the first 100*, the believers who were here before it was public. Say it. It converts followers into evangelists who bring their locker room (§4.6).

# 5 · The Funnels & The Backend Machine

This chapter turns everything upstream — Ian's face, the content, Tim's science — into money. That's not a weakness here — it's the unfair advantage: at 80–200 leads, Ian can personally touch *every single one*. The rule for this whole chapter is: **automate the plumbing, close by hand.**

> This chapter maps and connects. It does **not** rewrite the assets. The finished Slovenian opt-in copy, the 13 emails, the pricing copy, the dunning emails all already exist in `04-funnel-sales-system.md`, `07-lead-magnet-tim-7day.md`, and `08-launch-email-sequence.md`. ## 5.1 The full funnel map — where every lead leaks, and what catches it

Seven stages. A stranger becomes a viewer, a viewer becomes a follower, a follower becomes an owned lead, a lead gets nurtured, a nurtured lead buys, a buyer is retained, and a retained member refers. **At every arrow, someone leaks.** The job of the backend is one thing: make sure that at our tiny volume, *not one of the 5–20 buyers we can realistically get* falls through a gap nobody was watching.

```
  STAGE 0 · COLD VIEWER  ── IG Reel / YT Short / long-form. Stranger, no relation.
  (stranger)                MECHANISM: hook → value → ONE CTA ("link v biju: brezplačen načrt")
        │
        │   LEAK: watches, feels good, scrolls on, never clicks.
        │   CATCH: Intentional Brand — every post is reverse-engineered from ONE CTA
        │          (the free 7-day plan). No "just vibes" posts. Pinned comment + link-in-bio
        │          point to the SAME opt-in page. (§5.2)
        ▼
  STAGE 1 · FOLLOWER     ── Follows Ian (personal) + @athlos, or subs YT. Warm-ish, in orbit.
  (in orbit)                MECHANISM: 3:1 growth-to-nurture content keeps them in the feed.
        │
        │   LEAK: follows but never joins the list. You don't own the relationship —
        │         the algorithm does. One shadow-ban and they're gone forever.
        │   CATCH: No-Leak — convert follower → OWNED email fast. Every 4th–5th post +
        │          every Story block ends in the lead-magnet CTA. Story link sticker +
        │          a DM-keyword trigger ("napiši NAČRT").
```

## 5.2 Lead capture — the magnet, the opt-in page, and where the CTA lives

### The lead magnet is a slice of the product, not a freebie

A "waitlist" that only promises "get notified at launch" is a dead magnet — "get notified" is a chore, not a benefit. The opt-in must hand over something valuable the instant someone joins. Ours is already built (see `07-lead-magnet-tim-7day.md`):

> **"7-dnevni ATHLOS Starter"** — a real, usable 7-day speed/explosiveness micro-protocol authored by Tim Drenovc, delivered as a clean PDF, plus a 60-second self-assessment ("Kje si zdaj?") that tells the athlete which phase they're in.

Why this specific magnet works (Iman's **documentation-before-education** applied honestly): it's *one real week of the actual product*, not a generic PDF. It proves ATHLOS's core value — personalized, Tim-verified training — before anyone pays a cent. It's near-zero marginal cost (the Coach brain generates the sport-specific base). And the assessment writes a **segmentation tag** (per-sport, per-level) we reuse in nurture. Iman would build a 5-day livestream with a $250K prize pool here; the right-sized cold-start equivalent is one PDF one person can ship.

### The opt-in page + waitlist mechanics (Beehiiv double opt-in)

The finished Slovenian opt-in copy lives in `04 §2.5` — do not rewrite it. Mechanically, the page does exactly three jobs:
1. **Names the free thing as a product**, not a notification ("Dobi svoj 7-dnevni načrt. Brezplačno.").

3. **Teases the first-100** at the bottom ("Med prvih 100, ki vstopijo, zaklene ceno za vedno.") — this seeds §5.3 before the price is ever named.

The one thing everyone building this must understand about Beehiiv: **double opt-in is ON, which means the biggest silent leak in the entire funnel lives right here.** A sign-up that never clicks the confirmation email is stuck "pending" and receives *nothing* — not the plan, not the nurture, not the launch. That person raised their hand and we lost them to a checkbox. So:

- The Google Sheet logs `pending` vs `confirmed` — **someone watches that column daily** and manually DMs anyone Ian knows personally. At this scale, one recovered pending lead can be one buyer.

**Price anchoring — two anchors, never a bare price.** Every tier shows against its crossed-out regular price (`~€49~ €29/mes`). But the *stronger* anchor is the human-alternative one, and it should be the hero of the pricing section:

**Real scarcity, never fake.** Hard cap at 100. A counter that actually decrements. In a market this small, one faked count is permanent trust death — round milestones ("že 34 od 100") are fine, invented urgency is not. The lock-for-life is the honest reason to act *now*.

## 5.4 The email / nurture engine

Three sequences, **13 emails**, all in Beehiiv, all from "Ian iz ATHLOS-a" with reply-to a real inbox Ian reads — **replies are the goal**, they're the warmest close signal at this scale. The full Slovenian copy already exists (re-engagement + nurture in `04 §4a/§4b`; the 5 launch emails as drop-in broadcasts in `08`). This section is the **send logic**, not the copy.

```
  SEQUENCE A · RE-ENGAGEMENT (wake the existing ~12) ── Beehiiv AUTOMATION, week 1
     R0 → only to `pending`: confirm → get the plan          [fixes the #1 silent leak]
     R1 → "Hvala, ker si bil zraven od začetka" + the gift
     R2 → "Zakaj sem začel graditi ATHLOS" + a question that begs a reply

  SEQUENCE B · NURTURE / 30-DAY STORY (whole list) ── Beehiiv AUTOMATION, ~1 email / 4–5 days
     N1 → welcome, deliver the magnet (instant on confirm)
     N2 → the problem, named (Problem Farming) — pure value, no pitch
     N3 → the expert: who is Tim, why Ian trusts him (trust transfer)
     N4 → build-in-public: the app is real + FIRST LINK TO THE VSL
     N5 → the €200–500 alternative it replaces (plants the price anchor before price exists)
     N6 → first-100 heads-up + disqualification + "odgovori JAZ za opomnik" (builds the DM list)

  SEQUENCE C · LAUNCH (cart open → close) ── Beehiiv manual BROADCASTS, 5–7 day window
     L1 → CART OPEN (warm list first, first-access checkout)
     L2 → handle the #1 objection ("ali deluje za MOJ šport?")
     L3 → founder story / why now (the nurture spike, 3:1)
     L4 → scarcity, real remaining count
     L5 → FINAL CALL / cart closes (lock-for-life delta, hard close)
```

**The send logic that matters:**

- **A and B are automations; C is manual broadcasts.** Re-engagement and nurture fire on triggers (confirm, time-delay) and run themselves. The launch is 5 manual broadcasts because a launch needs *live control* — a real counter, a real close time you announce and honour.
- **The trigger boundary:** R0 is segmented to `pending` only. N1 fires the instant someone confirms. C is sent to the confirmed warm list first (they got first-access framing in N6), then public.
- **Never duplicate across sequences.** A confirmed early sub gets R1/R2 (welcome-back), *then* rejoins the N-flow — don't send them the "here's your first plan" welcome twice. Segment by `confirmed_date` so old subs and new subs don't collide.
- **Manual DMs run in parallel to every launch email** (§5.5). The email is the announcement; the DM is the close. At ~100–200 leads, 5 launch emails + personal DMs beat 12 automated emails. **Depth of touch > breadth of sequence** — this is the deliberate cold-start inversion of Iman's heavily-automated 10-email launches.
- **A/B in Beehiiv:** use the built-in subject-line test on each launch broadcast (Subject = A, alternate = B, keep preview text identical), send the winner by open rate.

### The checkout UX (no-leak = minimal friction)

`/api/checkout` + `src/lib/stripe.ts` are built and launch-gated (503 until `LAUNCH_MODE=live`). The rules:

- **One page, one decision.** Tiers and checkout in the same flow. Every extra click is a leak.
- **Stripe Checkout, not a custom form.** Fewer failure modes, Apple/Google Pay built in, mobile-first by default, minimal fields (email + card — nothing we don't need). This is a **Slovenian phone-first audience**; the checkout has to feel like two taps.

## 5.6 Retention & referral — where a subscription actually makes money

MVP is a subscription, so **month-1 retention decides whether the business is real.** A €29 buyer who churns after 30 days is a loss after Stripe fees. Iman's own framing: retention beats acquisition, and failed-payment recovery is the most under-worked profit lever there is.

### Activation is retention (the first 10 minutes)

The single strongest churn-prevention lever is: *did they use it in week 1?* So **onboarding must end in a generated AI plan in hand, not an empty dashboard** — activation = first workout plan, same session they paid. Then a **Day-2 check-in email** (`Kako gre s prvim tednom?`, reply invited) catches confusion before it becomes a cancel.

### The founding-member ritual (retention engineering, right-sized)

Iman time-gates unlocks (coaching calls unlock month 2, etc.) to build churn-prevention into the product. Ian's cold-start version:
- **Founding-100 identity + perks over time:** price locked for life (the core hook) + a "founding member" badge + "the next feature ships *for you first*." A reason to still be here in month 2.
- **A weekly Tim value email to paying members** (separate from the marketing list) — keeps perceived value high between app sessions. Cheap, compounding.
- **Ask founders for feedback and USE it visibly.** "Predlagal si X → naredil sem X" is the most powerful retention move a founder-led MVP has — and it's the exact mechanism that converts a member into a **referrer**.

### Failed-payment dunning (pure found money)

Involuntary churn (expired/declined cards) is silent and large. Iman's most quotable stat: **50% recovery vs 5% industry.** Set up on day 1 of billing:
- **Stripe Smart Retries** — auto-retries failed charges on an optimized schedule.
- **Dunning emails** (Slovenian, from `04 §6.3`): fail day 0 → `Plačilo ni šlo skozi (2 klika)` · retry day 3 → `Še vedno ne gre — dostop se čez X dni zapre` · final → `Zadnji poskus — obnovi, da ne izgubiš founding cene`.
- **The lock-for-life price is the retention weapon:** "če odideš, izgubiš €29 za vedno — nova cena je €49." Staying becomes rational, not emotional.
- **Card-updater / expiry reminders** before renewal to pre-empt the failure entirely.

### Turning the first members into a referral loop (Stage 6 → refills the top)

Team-sport athletes share inside a locker room by default — we just have to make referral the path of least resistance, not a favour to ask:
- **Beehiiv native referral on the magnet:** "Deli s soigralcem → oba dobita bonus protokol." The free tier spreads inside a team chat on its own.
- **The founding-member "bring your soigralec" ask:** once a member is activated and happy (post Day-2 check-in), the moment to ask for one referral — a founder who feels chosen brings a teammate.
- **Visible used-feedback as social proof:** "predlagal si X → naredil sem X" posted publicly turns one happy founder into evangelism the whole warm graph sees. This is the loop closing: a referrer sends a stranger back to Stage 0/2, and the funnel refills without new cold reach.

## Blind-spot check

- **This funnel is real, but the top is a trickle.** With ~12 warm subs and a cold audience, the launch does **not** live or die on new cold traffic in 30 days. It lives on (a) waking the warm list, (b) converting Ian's own network + DMs by hand, and (c) whatever content adds on top. If you build the beautiful funnel and forget to *manually message every human*, you built the wrong thing. Cold content seeds month 2–6; it does not carry launch day.
- **Automation is a trap at this scale — except for the plumbing.** Iman automates because volume justifies it. At a dozen-to-few-hundred leads, a personal DM outconverts any automated email. Automate the Beehiiv sequences, Stripe, and dunning; **close by hand.** The day this funnel gets big enough to *need* full automation is a good day — we are not there, and pretending we are would cost buyers.
- **Sober numbers.** Grow the warm list to a realistic 80–200 leads and a good warm launch converts 5–12% → **~5–20 paying founding members in month 1.** That is a *success* for a solo organic cold-start, not a failure. The real asset built here is a warm, segmented Slovenian athlete list + a watertight checkout/retention machine + the first 5–10 testimonials. Do not measure any of this against Iman's launch screenshots — his one public financial data point showed insolvency. Measure it against the counter and the confirmed-leads number.
- **The #1 silent leak is the double opt-in, and it's invisible.** A pending sub looks like nothing on a dashboard — no bounce, no complaint, just silence. If nobody watches the `pending` column daily, the funnel will quietly lose its best-intent leads and no one will know why the numbers are soft. This is the leak most likely to go unnoticed, so it gets the most explicit human check.
- **Retention beats acquisition, and it's the part a founder in launch-adrenaline forgets.** The temptation is to pour all energy into the cart open and none into Day-2 activation and dunning. But for a subscription, a churned €29 buyer is a *loss*. The founding-member ritual, the week-1 check-in, and the dunning emails are not "later" — they are Phase 2 and Phase 5 on purpose.

## 6.1 The founder-as-face principle (for the team)

Iman's core structural move — **"effortless front, best-in-class backend"** — is the spine of how we operate. The public sees Ian: relaxed, on camera, building ATHLOS in the open. What the public never sees is the team running the unglamorous machine that turns Ian's face into leads, leads into founding members, and members into retained revenue. The gap between how effortless it looks and how systematic it actually is — *that gap is the moat.*

For that to work, we protect Ian's time like it's the scarcest resource in the company. Because it is. Ian is simultaneously the on-camera talent **and** one of the two engineers shipping the app. If the team leaks work onto Ian that the team could have absorbed, the app slips or the content dies — and both are fatal.

### What only Ian can do (never delegate these)

1. **Be on camera.** Ian is THE FACE. He films THE JOURNEY, he presents the science with Tim, he trains himself on the product on camera, he records the VSL, he goes live on launch day. Nobody else can be the founder-athlete. This is his #1 job and the one input the whole machine depends on.
2. **Own the network + the DMs.** The launch lives on Ian's warm graph (his athletes, coaches, training partners) and on personal replies. At this scale a founder's DM outconverts any automation. Ian personally messages the people he knows and personally replies to every lead who raises a hand. The team can *tee up* DMs (draft, list, remind) but the send comes from Ian, in his voice.
3. **Hold the vision + the taste calls.** Positioning, the offer, what ATHLOS stands for and against, whether a piece of content is "us" or not, the disqualification line, the honesty of the scarcity counter. These are founder judgment calls. The team executes them; Ian sets them.
4. **The few founder-only decisions:** launch date, price, whether to go live, what promise we can honestly make ("za vedno"), and any call that touches trust in a small market where one wrong move is permanent.

### What the team absorbs (so Ian never touches it)

Everything else. Editing, packaging (titles/thumbnails/captions), scheduling, posting, the @athlos account, Beehiiv setup and sends, the Stripe plumbing, the dashboard, abandonment and dunning, the lead-magnet generator, checkout and onboarding, launch ops, the numbers. Ian should walk into a Content Day, film, walk out — and the finished, packaged, scheduled content, plus every backend system it feeds, appears without him lifting another finger.

**The test for every task:** *"Does this require Ian's face, Ian's network, or Ian's founder judgment?"* If **no**, the team owns it end-to-end and does not put it back on Ian. If **yes**, get Ian the minimum viable input (film this, approve this, send this) and absorb everything around it.

> **Blind-spot check:** the failure mode here is the team quietly becoming a "please approve this" queue that turns Ian into a bottleneck. If Ian is approving more than a short daily batch, the team has under-absorbed. Default to shipping within the locked brand rules and showing Ian the result, not asking permission for every piece. Approval is for the few founder-only calls, not for a caption.

## 6.4 The weekly rhythm (keep meetings minimal)

The whole point of a tight OS is that the team spends its hours *building the machine*, not *sitting in meetings about the machine.* Four rhythms, that's it.

### 1. Content Day — the one tentpole (½–1 day, weekly)
The single batch-film day. Creative lead arrives with the pre-approved packaging list (Stage 1 already done). Ian films 3–5 pieces; Tim films his cut-ins if it's a science week. This is the only recurring block that needs Ian's face and the whole team's coordination. **Protect it** — a missed Content Day = an empty content week = the funnel starves.

### 2. Monday numbers review — LEADS not views (15 min, weekly)
Short and disciplined. The Funnel/Data lead presents the ~6 numbers (6.5). One question drives it: **"Did we grow confirmed leads, and did nothing leak?"** Not "how did the Reel do." If the team ever spends this meeting celebrating view counts, it's off-mission — that's Iman's core discipline made into a standing rule. Decide the week's one focus, end the meeting.

### 3. Async daily check-in (2 min, text, no meeting)
A single message in the team channel: what shipped yesterday, what ships today, any blocker. No standup call. At this team size a live daily meeting is pure overhead. The only thing that must surface fast: **anything blocking the app ship date or a lead leaking** — those get escalated the moment they appear, not saved for Monday.

### 4. The Week-4 launch push (the exception to "minimal")
Launch week breaks the calm rhythm on purpose. During the 5–7 day launch window: a short daily sync (go-live checklist, counter, which email drops today, who's DMing whom), the live launch moment, and the Funnel lead watching the scoreboard in near-real-time. This is the one week the team runs hot. Everyone knows it's coming and clears the decks for it.

> **Blind-spot check:** the temptation is to add more meetings as launch nerves rise. Resist. More syncs ≠ more members. The launch is won by the manual DMs and the emails going out on time, not by talking about them. Keep even launch week to *one* short daily sync plus async.

## 6.5 The metrics & dashboard (the ~6 numbers)

Vanity metrics are **banned** from this team. Follower count, Reel views, YouTube subs — they feel like progress and correlate with nothing that pays. The dashboard is short on purpose (Iman: *optimize for leads, not views*). The Funnel/Data lead owns it, updates it, and brings it to the Monday review.

| # | Metric | Why it's on the board | Healthy signal (cold-start, 30d) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Confirmed leads** (double-opt-in) | The only top-of-funnel number that pays | 12 → 80–200 by launch |
| 2 | **List growth rate** (net new confirmed/week) | Is the machine actually filling the top? | Steady weekly climb, no flat weeks |
| 3 | **Checkout completion** (started → paid) | Catches the biggest launch-day leak | > 50% finish; the rest get DM'd |
| 4 | **Founding members / 100** | The launch scoreboard | 5–20 = a win for solo organic |
| 5 | **MRR** (recurring, after Stripe fees) | Proof-of-model — is this real revenue? | Any positive, growing number |
| 6 | **Content-health number** — *leads driven per week by content* (not views) | Does the pipeline actually feed the funnel? | Content adds real opt-ins, however few |

**Who updates it, and when.** The Funnel/Data lead updates the dashboard continuously (it's their standing job) and presents it every Monday in the 15-minute review. During launch week, numbers 3, 4, and 5 are watched daily. Ian sees the dashboard but does not maintain it — his relationship to the numbers is "am I on plan?", not "let me update the sheet."

> **Blind-spot check:** #6 (content-health) is the one people will try to replace with "views" because views are bigger and feel better. Hold the line — a Reel with 4,000 views that drove zero opt-ins is a *failure*; a Short with 300 views that drove 6 confirmed leads is a *win*. That's the whole team's north star in one comparison.

## 6.6 THE GROUND-UP BUILD ROADMAP (from zero → launch in ~30 days)

Here's how the team builds the entire system from nothing, phased, mapped to the 30-day window. Each phase has a clear "done" bar. **The floor is sacred:** wake the list + hand out the free plan + plug the leaks + one honest VSL + five emails + DM every human. Everything above the floor is upside.

### Phase 0 — SETUP (roughly Days 1–3)
*Stand up the stack and the brand kit so the machine has rails.*
- **Accounts:** both bios live (Ian personal = priority, @athlos = proof/offer repo), Beehiiv workspace, Stripe account, the shared team channel + dashboard skeleton.
- **Stack wiring (engineers):** Stripe early-bird price IDs stubbed, `LAUNCH_MODE` gate in place, Beehiiv double-opt-in confirm-nudge configured (this fixes the #1 silent leak on Day 1).
- **Brand kit (Creative lead):** the Slovenian voice, the Hellenic visual look (kept as free differentiation), thumbnail/caption templates, the pillar plan + a starter problem-farming backlog.
- **Locks (Ian):** confirm the beachhead niche against the list's real sport mix; get Tim's minutes/week in writing.
- ✅ **Done when:** a piece of content could be posted and a lead could opt in and land in a working (if empty) sequence — the rails exist end-to-end.

### Phase 1 — FIRST CONTENT DAY + LEAD MAGNET LIVE (roughly Days 4–10)
*Turn on the two engines: content and the free offer.*
- **Lead magnet (engineers + Tim):** the sport-specific 7-day PDFs + 60-second assessment generated and live behind the opt-in.
- **First Content Day (Ian + Creative lead):** the pipeline runs its first full loop (idea→film→edit→package→schedule→post). Daily "dan X/30" documentation begins.
- **Wake the list (Funnel lead + Ian):** re-engagement sequence R0/R1/R2 sent; Ian starts mining his network by hand (the biggest week-1 lever); Tim's one post/story goes out.
- ✅ **Done when:** the lead magnet is live and converting, THE JOURNEY is publicly running, the warm list is awake and getting the free plan, and net-new confirmed leads are climbing.

### Phase 2 — AUDIENCE + LIST GROWTH + THE VSL (roughly Days 11–24)
*Grow the warm list and build the one convert asset.*
- **List growth (whole team):** network DMs continue, Beehiiv referral loop live, daily content → link-in-bio, Slovenian sport-community value drops. Target trajectory toward 80–200 leads.
- **Nurture (Funnel lead):** N1–N6 running so leads arrive at cart-open warm and primed.
- **The VSL (Ian + Creative lead):** record ONE honest 8–15 min founder-to-camera core video (why generic training fails → the system → the app working → the offer). Ship it rough; it becomes the gravity center every email and DM links to.
- **Launch prep (Funnel lead):** Stripe early-bird IDs finalized, abandonment recovery + dunning wired, the "JAZ" heads-up reply list built.
- ✅ **Done when:** the list is meaningfully bigger and warm, the VSL exists and is linkable, and every backend leak (confirm, checkout, abandonment, dunning) is plugged and tested.

### Phase 3 — LAUNCH (roughly Days 25–30)
*Open the cart to the warm list first and catch every lead by hand.*
- Flip `LAUNCH_MODE=live`; open to the warm Beehiiv list first (first crack at the 100).
- Run L1–L5 over a tight 5–7 day window; the live launch moment (IG/YT Live or launch-day Story series).
- **Manual close:** Ian personally DMs every clicker-who-didn't-buy and every "JAZ." This is the unfair advantage — at this scale a human "je vse ok?" recovers more carts than any automation.
- Funnel lead watches the scoreboard (checkout completion, members/100, MRR) daily.
- ✅ **Done when:** cart has opened and closed on schedule, every lead was personally caught, and the team has its first founding members + first testimonials. → **Day 31+:** onboarding, retention, dunning, and turning the flywheel for month 2 (where the compounding actually lives).

> **Blind-spot check:** the roadmap will feel like too much for a solo-ish team also shipping the app — because it is, if you try to do all of it perfectly. So the rule is: **if a week slips, protect the floor and cut from the top.** Skip the second content operation, skip the remix math, skip the mythology posts, skip a polished VSL. Never skip: the app ship date, waking the list, the free plan, one rough VSL, five emails, and DMing every human.

## 6.7 The 3 rules the team never breaks (+ the honest expectation)

**Rule 1 — Protect the app ship date above all.** The app shipping is the #1 marketing dependency; no product = no launch. Content and marketing serve the ship date, never the reverse. If marketing work is threatening the ship date, marketing yields — cut from the top of the roadmap (6.6), never from engineering.

**Rule 2 — Leads, not views. Always.** Every decision, every Monday review, every content call is judged by *confirmed leads and members*, not impressions. The moment anyone on the team is optimizing for a view count or celebrating a viral Reel that drove zero opt-ins, they're off-mission. This is the discipline the whole OS is built to enforce.

**Rule 3 — Never fake it, never leak it.** In a small market where everyone knows everyone, one whiff of a fake scarcity counter or a dishonest claim is permanent trust death. The "prvih 100 / za vedno" scarcity is real or it isn't used. And every lead is worth catching by hand — no confirmed lead leaks out of the funnel unnoticed, because at this volume every single one is a countable buyer.

### The honest expectation (the team internalizes this, or morale dies)
Plan emotionally for **~5 founding members** in month 1; be thrilled at 15. Confirmed leads of 40–150 by launch, ~8% converting, month-1 recurring revenue that is a **proof-of-model, not a salary.** The real asset the team builds is not the member count — it's the *warm, segmented list of right-fit Slovenian athletes + a working, leak-free checkout/retention machine + the first 5–10 testimonials.* 

The failure mode is **not** "only got 8 members." The failure mode is the team *feeling* like 8 is a flop and losing momentum before month 2, where the compounding lives. Do not measure this team against Iman's highlight reel — those numbers are marketing, and his one public financial data point showed insolvency. Measure against the floor: *did the machine get built, did the list get warm, did nothing leak, and can it run again next month without Ian burning out?* If yes, the team won.
