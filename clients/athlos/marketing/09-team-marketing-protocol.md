# ATHLOS — Team Marketing Protocol
## A consulting session with Iman

> **Language:** English (this is an internal operating manual for Ian's working team). Content/copy is quoted in Slovenian where it must ship in Slovenian. **Can be translated to SL for the team on request.**
>
> **What this is:** Iman Gadzhi, sitting across the table from a 5-person team, doing a live org-design consult. Not a lecture — the operating manual you print, pin, and run the next 30 days by. Every seat knows exactly what it owns, what number it's judged on, and the one thing it protects when the week falls apart.
>
> **Inherits from:** `00-START-HERE.md` (the realistic plan, the 20% cut, the honest numbers), `01-brand-foundation.md` (positioning, ICP), `04-funnel-sales-system.md` (the no-leak funnel, the metrics). If anything here contradicts those, those win on facts; this wins on *who does what*.

## Iman, opening the room

"Alright. Sit down. I'm going to be blunt because that's the only version of me that's useful to you.

Most founders think marketing is a *content problem*. It's not. At your stage it's an **org-design problem**. You have five seats and one partner and 30 days, and if I don't tell you exactly who owns what, you will do the thing every small team does — everyone touches everything, nothing has a single owner, and the founder becomes the bottleneck that the whole machine waits on. Then the app ships late, the launch is soft, and everyone blames 'marketing.'

So here's the first principle, and it governs this entire document: **remove the founder as the bottleneck.** Ian, your job is to be the face and to make the three or four decisions only you can make. *Everything else must be owned by someone who is not you.* If a task only moves when Ian touches it, that task is designed wrong.

Second principle: **the founder's face and story cannot be outsourced. Everything else must be.** Your network, your DMs, your on-camera presence, your 'why I built this' — that's the one non-delegable asset. If you try to delegate *that*, the brand dies. If you *fail* to delegate everything else, you die. Both failures are fatal. This doc draws that line hard.

Third: **leads, not views.** I'm going to say it in every section until you're sick of it. Nobody on this team gets to celebrate a view count. The scoreboard is confirmed leads and paying founders. That's it.

And before you get excited — a reality check I'm baking into every seat. You are **not me.** You have a tiny team that also has to *ship the app*, a warm list of about a dozen people, zero ad budget, and 30 days. Every framework I give you is down-scaled to that. Where my normal playbook assumes a 30-person team and millions of views, I'll tell you the small-team version. Don't run my scale on your resources — that's how small teams burn out and ship nothing."

## 2. The OWNERSHIP MAP (RACI)

> **R** = Responsible (does the work) · **A** = Accountable (one person, owns the outcome, the neck) · **C** = Consulted (input before) · **I** = Informed (told after).
>
> *Iman's rule: exactly ONE 'A' per row. If two people are accountable, nobody is. If you can't name the single A, the system will fail there first.*

| System part | S1 Ian (Face) | S2 Eng | S3 Eng | S4 Creative | S5 Funnel/Ops | Tim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal content (Ian's brand, THE JOURNEY)** | **A / R** | I | I | R (edit/package) | C | I |
| **@athlos content (lean proof/offer repo)** | C | I | I | **A / R** | C | R (Pillar-2 clips) |
| **Lead magnet (Tim's 7-day PDF + assessment)** | C | R (generator from Coach brain) | C | R (design/packaging) | **A** (delivery + copy) | **R** (protocol author) |
| **Email / nurture (Beehiiv sequences)** | C (voice, "from Ian") | I | I | C | **A / R** | C |
| **Backend / checkout (Stripe, no-leak)** | I | R (checkout UX) | R (onboarding UX) | I | **A / R** (config, prices, dunning) | I |
| **DMs / community (the hand-mined network)** | **A / R** | I | I | C (surfaces DM-worthy replies) | R (respond to leads at scale) | C (Tim's own audience) |
| **Data / dashboard (single source of truth)** | I | I | I | I | **A / R** | I |
| **Launch event (cart open, war-room)** | **A** (final go/no-go) | R (system stability) | R (system stability) | R (live content) | **R** (ops, checkout, counter) | C (appears/vouches) |
| **The app ship (MVP live on time)** | **A** (final scope call) | **R** | **R** | I | I | I |

**How to read the load-bearing rows:**

- **Personal content — Ian is A and R.** *Iman: "This is the one row where the founder does the work himself and I don't let him delegate it. The editing? Delegate. The face and the words? Never. Seat 4 can cut it, package it, schedule it — but Ian is in the frame and Ian's voice is the script."*
- **Lead magnet has three R's on purpose.** Tim authors the science, Seat 2 builds the generator off the Coach brain, Seat 4 makes it look like a real product — but **Seat 5 is the single A** because the only thing that matters is *did the right athlete receive the right plan without leaking.* One neck for delivery.
- **Email is Seat 5's, not Ian's.** *Iman: "Ian writes 'as Ian' — the voice is his, per the funnel doc. But he is not the person who logs into Beehiiv, builds the automation, and hits send. That's a specialist-on-autopilot job. Ian consults on voice; Seat 5 owns the machine."*
- **Data is Seat 5, full stop.** One person owns the dashboard so there is never an argument about 'which number is right.' The dashboard is the referee. The referee can't be five people.
- **The app ship — Ian is A on *scope*, the engineers are R on *delivery*.** Ian decides what's in the MVP and what gets cut; the engineers decide how it gets built and they own hitting the date. Ian does not get to keep adding features. Scope creep from the founder is how ship dates die.

## 3. CADENCE — the meetings that actually run the machine

> *Iman: "Cadence is what stops a small team from drifting. Four rhythms. Keep them short — the point is to unblock people and read the scoreboard, not to have meetings for the feeling of having meetings. If a meeting doesn't change what someone does next, kill it."*

### 3.1 Daily async standup (every workday, written, ~5 min each)

Posted in one shared channel by a fixed time (say **09:00**). No live call. Three lines per person, plus the seat's number if it moved:

```
Yesterday: [the one thing I shipped]
Today: [the one thing I'm shipping]
Blocked: [what I need from whom — @tag them] / "nothing"
My number: [leads / pieces / checkout% / ship-status — only if it changed]
```

*Iman: "Async, because your engineers cannot lose 30 minutes a day to a call — every minute they're in a meeting is a minute the app isn't shipping. The 'Blocked' line is the whole point: it surfaces the moment Ian, or anyone, has become a bottleneck. If Ian shows up blocking three people two days running, that's the alarm — he's back in the machine and needs to get out."*

### 3.2 Weekly Monday marketing review (30 min, live, whole team)

The one live meeting. Agenda is fixed and it is **leads, not views:**

1. **Seat 5 reads the dashboard out loud** — the ~6 numbers (Section 6). This is the only source of truth. Nobody brings their own numbers.
2. **The one question:** did confirmed leads go up, and did any leak open? (Pending→confirmed rate, checkout completion.)
3. Each seat: last week's deliverable — shipped or not, and why.
4. This week's 1–2 deliverables per seat (from the 30-day overlay, Section 7).
5. **Ian's decisions:** anything needing offer/scope/date sign-off, decided *in the room*, not in a week of DMs.

*Iman: "Whoever opens their mouth to talk about Instagram followers or Reel views in this meeting gets ignored. Seat 5 reports leads. The moment the team optimizes for view counts, you're off-mission — I've watched it kill more launches than any competitor ever did."*

### 3.3 Weekly Content Day (one day, batch, Seat 4 + Ian + Tim window)

One day a week Ian and Seat 4 batch-film. Per the 20% cut: **one Content Day that makes 3 shorts *if it makes anything.*** Good audio + clean background beats fancy gear. Tim slots his locked minutes into this day so he's only pulled in once a week, not pinged constantly.

*Iman: "Batch it. A founder who also ships product cannot film daily — that's the treadmill that kills the app. Film once, Seat 4 cuts it into the week's pieces. And decide the packaging — the hook, the title — *before* you film, not after. Packaging is 80%. Deciding it after you shoot is the mistake I made for years."*

### 3.4 Week-4 launch war-room (daily, live, launch window only)

For the 5–7 day launch window only, the async standup **upgrades to a 15-min daily live check** at a fixed time:

- Seat 5: leads in, carts opened, carts completed, counter position (X/100), abandonment list.
- Ian: whose DMs to hit today (every clicker-who-didn't-buy, every "JAZ" reply).
- Seat 4: today's launch content — is it cut and scheduled?
- Seats 2+3: **is the system up?** Checkout, onboarding, first-plan-generation — all green? This is their war-room job: nothing breaks while carts are open.
- **Go/no-go and pace calls** made live by Ian.

*Iman: "During launch, response speed IS conversion. At your scale every lead is worth a personal message — Ian and Seat 5 are DMing humans by hand while Seat 4 keeps the content flowing and the engineers keep the lights on. This is the one week you're allowed to be intense. The other three, protect the ship date."*

## 4. If the week collapses — each seat's FLOOR (the 20% that survives)

> *Iman: "Some weeks blow up. Someone gets sick, the app breaks, life happens. When that week comes — and it will — each seat abandons everything except ONE thing. This is the 20% cut made personal: the single job per seat that, if it's the only thing that got done, the launch still lives. Memorize your floor."*

| Seat | If the week collapses, you protect ONLY this | Why it's the floor |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Ian** | **Mine the network + reply to every DM by hand.** *This IS the launch.* | The warm network is lever #1. A cold week of missed content costs almost nothing; a week of ignored DMs kills the launch. |
| **S2 — Engineer** | **The MVP ships on time.** Cut features, not the date. | The ship date is the #1 marketing dependency. Everything downstream waits on it. |
| **S3 — Engineer** | **Checkout + onboarding don't leak.** A buyer can pay and get their first plan. | A working front door that closes and activates beats every feature you didn't finish. |
| **S4 — Creative** | **Ship whatever is genuinely good, packaged, pointing at the magnet.** Ignore the volume math. | Per the cut: post the clips that are good, forget the 10-shorts remix. One packaged piece with a real CTA > ten unpackaged. |
| **S5 — Funnel/Ops** | **Beehiiv confirm-nudge fires + the dashboard is honest.** No unconfirmed leads rot; no blind flying. | Unconfirmed opt-ins are found money leaking; a wrong dashboard makes the whole team optimize the wrong thing. |
| **Tim** | **Deliver the locked minutes — the protocol authority behind the magnet + one Pillar-2 clip.** | 100% of the authority strategy depends on Tim existing in the content. Even a collapsed week needs his minimum. |

*Iman: "Read your floor again. When the panic hits, you don't triage in the moment — you already decided, right now, calmly, what you protect. That's the whole value of a floor: you make the hard call before the fire, not during it."*

## 5. DECISION RIGHTS — who gets to say yes

> *Iman: "Half of small-team paralysis is nobody knowing who decides. So we make it explicit and boring. When in doubt, the A on the RACI row decides. Here are the four that cause the most fights."*

| Decision | Who owns it | Who's consulted | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Approve content before it posts** | **Seat 4** owns the calendar; **Ian** has final veto on anything personal-brand or offer-related | Tim on science claims | *Iman: "Ian approves the SPINE (his face, the offer, the positioning). Seat 4 ships everything else without waiting on Ian — otherwise Ian is the bottleneck again. Don't route every clip through the founder."* |
| **Own the offer / pricing** | **Ian, sole authority** | Seat 5 (what's technically lockable in Stripe) | The €29/59/89, the first-100 lock-for-life, the tiers — Ian's call and only Ian's. Never negotiated ad hoc in a DM. |
| **Change the launch date** | **Ian, but only on the engineers' recommendation** | Seats 2+3 (can we ship?), Seat 5 (is the list ready?) | *Iman: "Ian can't move the date because he's nervous, and the engineers can't move it because they want more polish. It moves only when the engineers say the app genuinely isn't safe to ship AND Ian agrees. Protect the date; make moving it deliberately hard."* |
| **Escalation path** | Async standup → tag the blocker → if unresolved by next standup, → **Monday review** → if urgent, → **Ian decides same day** | — | Nothing sits blocked longer than one cycle. Speed of unblocking is a KPI in itself. |

**Science-claim guardrail:** any performance/physiology claim in content or the magnet must be **consulted with Tim before it ships.** In a small market, one wrong science claim vouched by "a physiologist" is permanent credibility damage. Tim is the C on every science row for exactly this reason.

## 6. THE DASHBOARD — the single source of truth

> *Iman: "One dashboard. Six numbers. Owned by one person (Seat 5). Reviewed at one meeting (Monday). If a number isn't on here, the team doesn't get to optimize for it. This is where 'leads not views' stops being a slogan and becomes an instrument."*

| # | Metric | Healthy signal (cold-start, 30 days) | Why it's on the board |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Confirmed leads** (double-opt-in) | 12 → 80–200 by launch | The only top-of-funnel number that pays. NOT followers, NOT views. |
| 2 | **Pending → confirmed rate** | > 60% confirming | Catches the #1 silent leak — opt-ins stuck unconfirmed, never emailed. |
| 3 | **Opt-in page conversion** (visitor → lead) | 20–40% | Tells you if the magnet + copy actually works. |
| 4 | **Email open + reply rate** | opens > 40%, replies > 3–5% | Reply rate is the real warmth signal at this scale — replies are warm close threads. |
| 5 | **Lead → buyer conversion** (launch) | 5–12% | The number that defines whether the launch worked. |
| 6 | **Founding members / 100** | **5–20 = a win** | The launch scoreboard. Plan for 5, thrilled at 15. |

*(During launch week, Seat 5 adds two live ops numbers to the war-room view: **checkout completion %** and **counter position (X/100)** — the leak-and-scarcity gauges.)*

- **Refreshed by:** Seat 5, updated **daily** (2 min from Beehiiv + Stripe + the Google Sheet).
- **Reviewed by:** the whole team, **every Monday**, read aloud by Seat 5 as item #1. Daily glance during launch week.

*Iman: "Deliberately banned from this board: IG follower count, Reel views, YouTube subs. They feel like progress and correlate with nothing that pays. The day someone screenshots a view count into the team chat as a 'win,' Seat 5's job is to point at this table."*

## 8. Iman's closing directive to the team

"Stand up. Here's what I want you to remember when this doc is buried in a drive folder and you're deep in week 3 and tired.

**The three rules you do not break:**

1. **The founder stays the face — and gets out of everything else.** Ian, your voice and your DMs are non-delegable; the machine around them is non-negotiable to delegate. The day you're the bottleneck on a task a specialist could own, you've broken rule one. Remove yourself.

2. **Protect the ship date above all.** The app shipping on time is the marketing plan. Cut features, never the date — and Ian doesn't get to add scope after the freeze. A launch with no product to sell is not a launch.

3. **Leads, not views. Every meeting, every seat.** The dashboard is the referee. Nobody celebrates a view count in this building. The moment you're optimizing for the dopamine number instead of the number that pays, you're lost — and I've watched better teams than yours die exactly there.

**And the honest expectation — say it out loud so nobody quits at the wrong moment:**

You are planning for **five founding members.** Five. If you get fifteen, you throw a party. The win is not a viral month — it's a warm, segmented list of real Slovenian athletes, a checkout machine that doesn't leak, and your first handful of testimonials. That's the asset. The failure mode is not 'only got eight' — it's *feeling* like eight is a flop and quitting before month two, where all the compounding actually lives.

Do not measure yourselves against my highlight reel. Those numbers are marketing. Build the machine, protect the ship date, catch every single lead by hand, and let month two do what month two does.

I'm watching from afar — and I'm rooting for you."
