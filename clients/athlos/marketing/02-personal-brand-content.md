# Ian Veber — Personal Brand Content System (IG + YouTube, Slovenian)

**Product:** ATHLOS — the Athlete Operating System (AI-personalized training app)
**Personal brand:** Ian Veber, the founder building it in public
**Market:** Slovenia first, individual athletes / serious fitness people (B2C, €29–89/mo)
**Channel:** 100% organic — Instagram + YouTube, short-form feeds both
**Lens:** Iman Gadzhi frameworks, adjusted for a COLD START (near-zero audience, solo/tiny team, small market, 30 days to MVP)

> **Read this first — the cold-start reframe.** Iman's system assumes 5M+ followers, 30+ feeder accounts doing 250M views/month, a rehashing team, and years of runway. Ian has ~a dozen waitlist subs, himself, and 30 days. So this document does two things at every step: (1) it names the real Iman framework, and (2) it gives the **adjusted cold-start version** that actually works at Ian's scale. Where a framework flatly does not apply yet, I say so and tell you what to do *instead* until it does. Do not run Iman's playbook literally — run this one.

> **The single most liberating principle for Ian (Playbook §3, "the deliberately small channel"):** You do NOT need to go viral to sell ATHLOS. A tiny channel of a few hundred *right* Slovenian athletes converts better than 100k random followers. Slovenia has maybe 3–5k serious trainable athletes in the sports ATHLOS serves. You are not building a media company. You are building a machine that turns the attention of *Slovenian athletes who already train hard* into €59/mo subscribers. Judge everything by **leads, not views** (Playbook §2).

## 1. Channel Roles — what IG does vs what YouTube does (Content Siloing)

**Iman framework: Content Siloing (§5) + Optimize for leads not views (§2).** Each platform gets ONE job and ONE audience. Never dilute a conversion surface with top-of-funnel noise.

### The division of labor

| | **Instagram (@ian.veber personal + @athlos.app)** | **YouTube (Ian Veber)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary job** | Reach + daily presence + trust drip | The deep, core-converting videos |
| **Content unit** | Reels (reach) + Stories (nurture) + carousels (save/authority) | Long-form (8–20 min) + the ONE Flywheel VSL |
| **Metric that matters** | Reach → profile visits → waitlist clicks / DMs | Watch time → description clicks → waitlist / app |
| **Frequency** | Daily-ish (high) | 1×/week long-form (low, deliberate) |
| **Role in funnel** | Top + middle — *makes you known* | Bottom — *makes you money* |
| **Audience it collects** | Broad Slovenian fitness/athlete audience | People warm enough to give you 12 minutes |

**IG = the street where you get seen. YouTube = the room where you close.** A Slovenian athlete discovers Ian through a Reel, follows because a Story made them trust him, then — when they're actually deciding whether to try ATHLOS — they watch the one long YouTube video that argues them into it. IG creates the *want*; YouTube converts the *want* into a waitlist signup.

### The two Instagram accounts — how they interlock (dual brand, locked decision)

- **@ian.veber (personal) = the trust engine.** This is where the *human* lives. Ian's own training, the build journey, opinions, face, voice, mistakes. People follow *people*, not apps. 70% of the reach comes from here. Personal account DRIVES.
- **@athlos.app (product) = the conversion surface.** Cleaner, more product-forward: athlete results, feature demos, Tim Drenovc's science, "the app just did X." Fewer posts, higher intent. The app account CONVERTS.
- **The interlock rule:** Ian's personal Reels casually feature the app ("evo, ATHLOS mi je danes zjutraj sestavil tole..."). The app account reposts/duets Ian's best personal content into its Story. Personal → follow → warmed → app account → waitlist. Never post the same content identically to both — personal is first-person and messy, app is polished and results-forward.

> **COLD-START ADJUSTMENT (blind spot):** Iman runs 30+ *separate* short-form feeder accounts. **Ian must NOT.** With one person and 30 days, spreading across many accounts guarantees all of them stay dead. **Run exactly TWO IG accounts + ONE YouTube channel.** The "feeder network" for Ian is not many accounts — it's *one long-form video remixed into ~10 shorts* (see §4) posted across his two IG accounts + YouTube Shorts + TikTok as a bonus surface. Same clips, three platforms, two accounts. That's the whole distribution engine. Add feeder accounts only after one account clears ~2,000 engaged followers — not before.

> **Second blind spot — the "small channel" caveat cuts both ways.** Iman keeps his channel *deliberately* small because he sells a $5,000 program to $250k/yr owners and needs zero broad reach. Ian sells a €59/mo consumer app — he needs *more* reach than Iman's high-ticket model, because the price is low and the Slovenian pool is small. So Ian is NOT running a deliberately-tiny channel. He wants maximum *qualified Slovenian* reach. The nuance: cap the *topic* narrow (Slovenian athlete training), not the reach. Go as broad as you can *within the niche*.

## 2. Content Pillars & the 3:1 Growth-to-Nurture Ratio

**Iman framework: The 3:1 growth-to-nurture ratio (§6) + Intentional Brand (§1) + Niche-down-then-expand (§4).** Two content jobs: Growth (~75%) brings strangers in and "makes you known"; Nurture (~25%) deepens the core relationship and "makes you money." Everything sits on the locked content spine: **THE JOURNEY** — the founder's build + training journey.

### Ian's 4 pillars (all hang off THE JOURNEY spine)

| # | Pillar | Slovenian name | Type | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Sport knowledge / training science** | *"Kako trenirati pravilno"* | GROWTH | Broad reach — VSL-style value. Athletes want to train better. |
| **P2** | **Build-in-public (technical)** | *"Gradim ATHLOS v živo"* | GROWTH+ | Reach + intrigue — the founder story people root for. |
| **P3** | **Founder journey / behind-the-scenes** | *"Dnevnik"* | NURTURE | Trust + parasocial bond — a day in the life, the why, the struggle. |
| **P4** | **Proof / results / demos** | *"ATHLOS v akciji"* | NURTURE (converts) | The bottom-funnel push — athletes' results, app demos, Tim's protocols. |

- **P1 + P2 = the ~3 (growth).** Curiosity-driven, broad, high-view. These are what a stranger scrolling past stops on. "Zakaj 90 % rekreativcev trenira nogo narobe." "Sam sem sestavil aplikacijo, ki pozna vsakega športnika."
- **P3 + P4 = the ~1 (nurture).** Lower reach, higher intent, deepen the relationship and convert. "Dan v mojem življenju kot solo founder." "Poglej, kaj je ATHLOS naredil za rokometaša iz 2. lige."

### The 3:1 split in practice — a real week of 4 pieces

Think in **blocks of 4 pieces = 3 growth + 1 nurture**:

1. **GROWTH (P1)** — *"3 vaje, ki jih dela vsak profi nogometaš (in ti verjetno nobene)."*
2. **GROWTH (P2)** — *"Dan 12/30: aplikacija zdaj sama prepozna, v kateri fazi sezone si."*
3. **GROWTH (P1)** — *"Zakaj tvoj napredek stoji — in ni do genetike."*
4. **NURTURE (P4)** — *"Poslušaj, kako je ATHLOS sestavil teden za košarkarja pred tekmo."* → CTA na waitlist.

> **COLD-START ADJUSTMENT (blind spot):** Iman can afford 3:1 because his growth content already reaches millions — his "1" nurture piece lands on a huge warm base. Ian's base is ~a dozen people. If Ian runs pure 3:1 from day one, the nurture pieces convert almost nobody because there's no audience to nurture *yet*. **Adjusted ratio for the first ~30 days: run 4:1 leaning growth (build the base), but make the ONE nurture piece per block explicitly point at the waitlist every single time.** You cannot convert an audience you don't have — front-load reach. Shift to a true 3:1 (more nurture, more selling) once the personal account clears ~1,000 engaged followers. Until then: reach is the job, but never let a nurture slot go by without a waitlist CTA, because your dozen warm subs are the ones who'll actually buy at launch.

> **Pillar sequencing (Niche-down-then-expand, §4):** Do NOT start broad. For the first 30 days, ~80% of P1 content should be about the *specific* sports ATHLOS serves best (nogomet, košarka, rokomet, atletika, borilne) — own "resen slovenski športnik" completely before you ever touch generic "fitnes." Broadening to general gym/fitness comes *after* launch, tied to a specific new goal (e.g. the BASIC tier). Ian's instinct to go broad early is the exact mistake §4 corrects.

## 3. The Flywheel VSL — the ONE core converting video

**Iman framework: The Flywheel VSL (§7).** Record ONE evergreen video that logically argues a viewer into wanting the product. Every other piece of content links back to it. It becomes a 24/7 salesperson that compounds — "the older it gets, the more it sells." No ads, no funnel, no sales team.

### Iman's prerequisite (and why Ian can't follow it literally)

Iman says: **you cannot make the VSL cold — build 15–30 videos on the channel first to *discover* the right topic** (the one with the longest watch time = your "growth topic," §10). This is correct at his scale and it is a **luxury Ian does not have.** Ian has 30 days to launch and near-zero content. Waiting for 15–30 videos of data means launching the VSL in ~4 months. That fails the brief.

> **COLD-START ADJUSTMENT — the shortcut:** Ian doesn't need to *discover* the topic empirically, because he already knows it. The whole ATHLOS thesis IS the VSL topic: *"every serious athlete deserves the pro-team support structure (physio + kinesiologist + trainer) — as an AI system, not a hired team you can't afford."* That's the argument. Ian's advantages that replace the 15–30-video data-gathering: (1) Tim Drenovc = a real expert whose credibility de-risks the claim; (2) the Coach chatbot already *demonstrably works* — Ian can show it building a real plan on screen; (3) the pain is universal and known (athletes plateau, generic programs, can't afford a strokovna ekipa). **So: build the VSL in week 1–2, not month 4.** Then treat every subsequent short/long-form video as a *live test* — whichever growth topic gets the longest watch time, you re-cut the VSL's HOOK to match it (packaging change only, §8). You get the compounding asset now AND the data-driven refinement later. Best of both.

### The VSL — full spec

**Format:** 12–16 min YouTube video (Iman's is 38 min of slideshow — too long and too cold for a Slovenian consumer app; a founder-to-camera + screen-share hybrid at 12–16 min is the right length for this market and price point). Ian to camera + screen-recordings of the app + Tim on-screen for credibility beats.

**Full title (Slovenian):**
> **"Zakaj slovenski športniki obtičijo — in kako sem zgradil sistem, ki pozna vsakega posebej"**

Alt titles to test (packaging is 80%, §9 — decide title before filming):
- *"Vsak profi klub ima strokovno ekipo. Naredil sem jo dostopno vsakemu športniku."*
- *"6 mesecev sem gradil aplikacijo, ki trenira namesto povprečnega trenerja. Evo dokaza."*

### Beat-by-beat outline

**BEAT 0 — Hook (0:00–0:30) — decide this before anything else.**
> "Če resno treniraš in tvoj napredek je zadnjih 6 mesecev stal na mestu — nisi len in ni do genetike. Problem je, da nimaš tega, kar ima vsak profi klub: strokovne ekipe, ki gleda samo tebe. Danes ti pokažem, kako sem to rešil."
State the promise, name the enemy (generic programs), tease the reveal. No intro, no logo animation.

**BEAT 1 — The problem, agitated (0:30–3:00). [Problem-frame]**
The gap between how pros train and how everyone else trains. Pros have a physio + kinesiologist + trainer watching one athlete, adjusting weekly to the season phase, recovery, load. Everyone else runs a generic PDF program bought online, the same block for a 17-year-old and a 30-year-old, no adjustment when they're tired or in-season. *"To ni tvoja krivda. To je krivda sistema, ki si ga imel na voljo."* Make the Slovenian athlete feel *seen*.

**BEAT 2 — The false solutions (3:00–5:00).**
Why the obvious fixes fail: hiring a real strokovna ekipa costs €500+/mo and isn't accessible; generic apps (Freeletics etc.) don't know your sport or your season; a personal trainer sees you 2×/week and doesn't program periodization. Disqualify the alternatives one by one. *(Disqualification positioning, Playbook §4 patterns — raising perceived value by naming what doesn't work.)*

**BEAT 3 — The reframe / the mechanism (5:00–8:00). [Solution-frame]**
Introduce the core idea: *"Kaj če bi lahko imel to strokovno ekipo — ampak kot sistem, ki te pozna, ne kot ljudi, ki jih ne moreš plačati?"* Introduce Tim Drenovc (the science is real, not vibes). Explain periodization simply: the app knows what phase of your season you're in and builds the week accordingly. This is the "aha."

**BEAT 4 — Proof / demo (8:00–12:00).**
Screen-record ATHLOS / the Coach building a real plan live. Pick a concrete Slovenian athlete case (a rokometaš 3 weeks out from a tournament). Show it choosing sport-specific exercises, adjusting for the phase, the table output with cues. *"Poglej — to ni naključen program. To je natančno to, kar bi ti dal Tim, samo ob 6h zjutraj in za desetino cene."* THIS beat is what actually converts — the working product on screen.

**BEAT 5 — The offer + close (12:00–15:00).**
Who it's for (resni slovenski športniki + resni rekreativci), the tiers (€29/€59/€89), the early-bird lock (prvih 100 za vedno). Then the CTA: *"Aplikacija gre v živo čez [X] dni. Prvih 100 ljudi zaklene ceno za vedno. Povezava je v opisu — vpiši se na čakalno listo zdaj, ker ko je 100 polnih, je konec."* Scarcity is real here (grandfather clause), so use it honestly.

**BEAT 6 — Signature outro (15:00+).**
An ownable, repeated closing line that compounds recognition (§10 signature style). Suggested: *"Treniraj pametno. Se vidimo v sistemu."* Same line every video, forever.

### How the flywheel actually spins for Ian

- Every Reel/Short ends with a soft pointer to "cel video na YouTube" and the waitlist link in bio/description.
- Every YouTube video (P1/P2/P3) has a pinned comment + end-screen linking to the VSL.
- The VSL description = the ONE link that matters: the ATHLOS waitlist.
- Quarterly (or when a new growth topic wins), re-cut ONLY the VSL hook + thumbnail. Never re-film the body. *(Content Rehashing, §8 — the compounding engine.)*

## 4. Short-Form Engine — the Reels/Shorts system

**Iman framework: Content Day batching (§10) + Documentation before education (§10) + one-to-many remixing (the feeder logic of §5, adapted).**

### The core mechanic — one long-form → ~10 shorts

This is the entire distribution engine for a solo founder. **Ian films ONE long-form YouTube video per week. That video is the quarry from which ~10 short-form pieces are mined.** Iman's 30 feeder accounts are replaced by *one video, cut ten ways, posted across two IG accounts + Shorts + TikTok.*

How one 12-min video becomes ~10 shorts:
1. The hook (first 30s) → its own standalone Reel.
2–5. Four self-contained "one idea" moments (a single tip, a single reframe, a single stat) → 4 Reels.
6–7. Two "controversial take" clips (a strong opinion that provokes a comment) → 2 Reels.
8. The best app-demo moment (screen-record) → 1 Reel (this one goes hardest on @athlos.app).
9. A "behind the scenes of making this video" clip → 1 nurture Reel (P3).
10. A "text-on-screen only, Ian's voiceover" cut of the single strongest line → 1 Reel.

Each cut gets its own native Slovenian hook (from the Hook Bank, §5) and its own thumbnail/cover. Same footage, ten packages. **This is the cold-start version of "content rehashing" — instead of a rehashing team changing thumbnails on a huge back-catalog, Ian changes the framing on the same fresh footage.**

### The "documentation vs education" mix (§10)

- **Documentation (~60% early on):** "gradim ATHLOS v živo," "dan 14/30," "danes sem se zjebal in program se je pokvaril." Low production burden, high trust, and it's the honest cold-start move — Ian isn't yet a famous expert, so he *documents the journey* rather than lecturing. People root for a builder.
- **Education (~40%, rising):** "kako pravilno periodizirati sezono," "3 vaje za eksplozivnost." As the audience grows and starts asking "kako to delaš?", education takes over. Iman: documentation *evolves into* education when the audience pulls it out of you.

> **COLD-START ADJUSTMENT:** Ian is "one step ahead," not an authority yet (§10). So early education must be *co-signed by Tim* or framed as "kar sem se naučil od Tima" — never "trust me, I'm the expert." Borrow Tim's credibility until Ian has his own. Documentation, by contrast, Ian owns 100% — nobody can dispute *his* journey. Lean documentation-heavy for the first month.

### Batching — Content Day (§10)

- **One Content Day per week.** Film the long-form video + record 3–4 extra to-camera hooks in the same session (good audio + clean background beats fancy gear).
- **One Editing Day per week** (or outsource): cut the ~10 shorts from the long-form.
- Schedule/queue the week's shorts so daily posting is a 5-minute publish action, not a daily creation grind.
- **The 10-Video Challenge as a warm-up (§10):** before going live, Ian films 10 to-camera videos he never posts, to kill camera fear and find his on-camera voice. Do this in the first 3 days. It decouples the *skill* from the *exposure*.

## 7. Packaging Rules — Thumbnail Rule of Three + title/hook discipline

**Iman framework: Packaging is 80% (§9).** The algorithm cares about two things — **CTR and watch time.** 80% of success is the big idea + packaging; 20% is execution. **Decide title + thumbnail BEFORE filming.** Thumbnail is engineering, not art.

### Thumbnail — the Rule of Three

Every YouTube thumbnail (and every Reel cover) has exactly three elements, nothing more:
1. **Ian's face with genuine emotion** — surprise, intensity, "poglej to." Real, not stock-smile. This is the trust engine; his face must be there.
2. **Clear, readable text** — 3–4 words MAX, huge, readable on a phone at thumbnail size. Slovenian.
3. **One context visual** — a barbell, the app screen, a stopwatch, an athlete mid-sprint. One thing that signals the topic.

Hellenic brand note: use the Aegean-blue / ink / gold palette and the marble texture as background treatment so thumbnails are *ownable* and instantly recognizable as Ian's — but never let the aesthetic reduce text legibility. CTR beats "lepo."

### Title / hook discipline

- Title decided FIRST, before filming (Iman's multi-year mistake was the reverse).
- Title = a promise + a curiosity gap. It must make a Slovenian athlete think *"to sem jaz / to moram vedeti."*

- One idea per piece. If you can't say the payoff in one breath, it's two videos.

## 8. Cadence — a realistic weekly rhythm for a solo founder

**Honest constraint:** Ian is building the MVP, managing the launch, AND doing content — solo, in 30 days. Iman spends 3–5 hrs/month on content because a team does the rest. Ian has no team. So the cadence below is built to be **survivable**, batched into ~1.5 days/week, and it explicitly refuses to promise a daily-original-content grind that will collapse in week two.

### The weekly rhythm (two anchored days + light daily)

| Day | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon — CONTENT DAY** | Film 1 long-form YouTube video (the week's quarry) + 3–4 extra to-camera hooks. Same setup, one sitting. | ~3 hrs |
| **Tue — EDIT DAY** | Cut the long-form + mine ~10 shorts from it. Write Slovenian hooks + covers. Queue the week. | ~3 hrs |
| **Wed–Sun — PUBLISH (5 min/day)** | Post 1 short/day across @ian.veber + @athlos.app + Shorts/TikTok. Post 1–2 Stories/day (P3 nurture — behind the scenes, polls, waitlist reminders). Reply to every comment/DM (this IS the lead work). | ~30 min/day |
| **1×/week** | Publish the long-form YouTube video (Wed or Thu). | included |
| **1×/week** | Re-cut the single best-performing thumbnail/hook (the cold-start rehash). | ~20 min |

**Weekly output from ~7 focused hours:** 1 long-form + ~7 shorts posted + daily Stories + full comment/DM engagement. That's a real, defensible content presence for a solo founder — not a fantasy.

### Rules that keep it survivable

- **Batch or die.** Never film day-of. The Content Day is non-negotiable; if Mon slips, the whole week's content slips — protect it like a client meeting.
- **Documentation over production (§10).** When time is tight, a raw "dan X/30" phone selfie-video beats a polished video that never ships. Consistency > polish for the first 30 days.
- **The VSL is the exception.** Build it once in week 1–2 with more care (it's the compounding asset). After that, never re-film it — only re-package.
- **Engagement is the actual funnel work.** For a cold start, replying to every comment and DM personally is worth more than one extra post. Iman's "leads not views" (§2) at Ian's scale = *conversations*. A dozen warm DMs > 10k cold views.

> **COLD-START ADJUSTMENT (the honest blind spot):** Iman's whole model assumes he can step back and the team keeps the flywheel spinning. Ian cannot step back — if he stops, everything stops. So the realistic promise is NOT "daily viral content." It is: **one Content Day + one Edit Day + light daily publishing/engagement, indefinitely sustainable.** If even that is too much during crunch weeks, the fallback floor is: **3 shorts/week + daily Stories + reply to everything.** Never let the floor drop below "reply to every DM" — that's where the launch-day buyers come from. Scale up (more shorts, feeder accounts, a hired editor) only *after* launch, when there's revenue to pay for leverage. Until then: batch, document, engage, protect the Content Day.
