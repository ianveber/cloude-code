# Run-Book

How Veta operates day to day. What gets done, when, by whom, and how decisions get made. The run-book reduces cognitive overhead on operational questions that shouldn't require fresh thinking every time.

This document covers the solo-founder stage. It will need revision when the first project lead is added.

---

## Daily (15 minutes)

**Morning sweep — every working day before 9:30 AM local time**

1. **Active engagement blockers**
   Open the engagement folder for each active project. Check: are there any blockers I created yesterday that I haven't yet communicated to the client? Any client responses I haven't acted on? Any integration or build issues that emerged since yesterday?
   Action: If yes — communicate or act within the hour, before starting any other build work.

2. **Post-launch exception queues**
   For any system in its 30-day support window or on a Tier 3 retainer: check the exception dashboard.
   Action: Any unreviewed exception older than 24 hours gets addressed before new build work begins.

3. **Overdue invoices**
   Check: any invoice past 15 days unpaid?
   Action: Past 15 days → email follow-up. Past 25 days → phone call. Past 30 days → flag as a blocker on the next milestone.

4. **Inbox scan (5 minutes)**
   Look for: client messages flagged urgent, prospect replies to proposals, new inbound inquiries.
   Action: Reply to urgent client messages immediately. Route prospect replies to pipeline tracking. Log new inbound inquiries with a scheduled discovery call date.

**That's it for daily.** Everything else runs on the weekly and monthly cadence. Do not let daily admin expand to fill the morning — it's a sweep, not a dive.

## Weekly (Fridays, 60–90 minutes)

**Friday review block — protect this time, don't reschedule it**

### 1. Engagement status (20 min)

For each active engagement, answer three questions:
- Is this engagement on track to hit the next milestone on the committed date?
- Is there anything the client needs to do that they haven't done? (pending spec reviews, overdue credential delivery, unanswered questions)
- Is there any technical risk I haven't surfaced yet?

Output: Send the weekly status update to each active client before 5 PM Friday. Format: 3–5 bullets. What was completed this week, what's up next week, any actions needed from the client. No narrative paragraphs. No filler.

### 2. Pipeline review (15 min)

Update the pipeline tracking document:
- Proposals sent this week?
- Proposals advanced (verbal acceptance, new info, follow-up)?
- Proposals stalled (no response in 7+ days since last follow-up)?
- Proposals lost?
- New discovery calls completed this week → add to pipeline
- Calculate current weighted pipeline value → compare to 2.5x coverage target

Action on stalled proposals: One follow-up at day 7, one at day 14. If no response after day 14 follow-up, mark the deal as dormant and move on. Do not chase indefinitely — if they're not responding to a proposal for a problem they said they wanted to solve, something changed. Find out what or find the next prospect.

### 3. Acquirer agent review (10 min)

Review content and outreach activity from the week (see `agents/acquirer/`):
- What content was published or distributed?
- Any inbound leads from content or organic sources?
- Any partnership or referral outreach that received a response?
- Is the content calendar on track for next week?

### 4. Financial snapshot (10 min)

- Invoices issued this week
- Payments received this week
- Projected milestone payments for the next 4 weeks (based on active engagement timelines)
- Any accounts receivable issues?

### 5. Blockers and decisions (10 min)

Is there anything I've been avoiding or delaying that needs a decision this week? Common categories:
- A prospect I haven't responded to
- A scope question I've been treating as ambiguous
- A build decision I've been deferring
- A client relationship issue I haven't addressed

Name it, decide it, act on it or schedule an action by next Friday.

## Monthly (last working day of the month, 90 minutes)

### 1. KPI review (30 min)

Pull the monthly KPI spreadsheet. Fill in the current month's values for every metric in the KPI framework. Then:
- What moved in the right direction?
- What moved in the wrong direction?
- What's a trend vs. a one-time event?
- Any metric outside its target range for two consecutive months? → That's a pattern, not noise.

Flag any metric that has been in the warning range for two consecutive months. Name a specific action to address it before the next monthly review.

### 2. Retainer client reviews (20 min)

For each Tier 3 retainer client:
- Review the previous month's improvement backlog: what was completed, what's in progress, what new items were added?
- Review agent metrics for the month (from the KPI framework Layer 3 metrics)
- Is the retainer delivering enough value to justify renewal? If not, what needs to change?
- Prepare the monthly performance summary to send to the client within 3 business days

### 3. Playbook update queue (15 min)

Check: any completed engagements in the last month that haven't had their vertical playbook updated?
Action: If yes, do the playbook update now or schedule it within 5 business days.

Check: any discovery calls this month where you encountered a new objection, a new integration complexity, or a new compliance wrinkle not in any playbook?
Action: Log it in the relevant playbook — even two sentences is better than losing the insight.

### 4. Content and acquisition review (15 min)

Review the Acquirer agent's monthly output:
- Content published and distribution metrics
- New inbound leads attributed to content or organic sources
- SEO/GEO ranking changes if tracked
- Referral and partnership pipeline

Is the current content strategy generating qualified discovery calls? If not, what's the hypothesis for why not, and what's changing next month?

### 5. Upcoming milestone calendar (10 min)

Map out all expected milestones for the next 6 weeks across all active engagements. Identify any week where more than 2 milestones are clustered — that's a risk week for timeline slippage. Adjust build plans if needed.

## Quarterly (first Monday of Q, 3 hours)

### 1. Trend analysis

Pull the trailing 3-month data for every KPI. Compute 3-month rolling averages. Compare to the prior quarter. Look for:
- Revenue trajectory: is the business growing, flat, or declining?
- Delivery quality trajectory: is on-time milestone rate improving as we compound playbook knowledge?
- Client outcome trajectory: are systems performing better or worse than earlier engagements?
- Pipeline health: is the pipeline growing in proportion to capacity?

### 2. Vertical expansion decision

Should we open a fourth vertical? Decision criteria:
- Have the first three verticals generated at least 3 completed engagements total?
- Is there inbound demand for a specific vertical we're not yet serving? (Track where unqualified leads are coming from)
- Do we have a practitioner-level knowledge base for the candidate vertical — or would we be starting from zero?
- Does the fourth vertical's playbook create leverage on existing integrations (i.e., tools we already know) or require entirely new integration work?

If the criteria aren't met, don't expand. Staying focused is a competitive advantage at this stage.

### 3. Pricing floor review

Should pricing floors change? Review against:
- Actual build cost of the last 4 completed Tier 1 engagements (hours × implied hourly)
- Are any engagements running below the floor after factoring in actual build time?
- Has the market signaled willingness to pay above current ceiling ranges?

Adjust floors upward if engagements are consistently taking more time than priced for. Do not adjust downward under any circumstances.

### 4. Capacity planning

Based on current engagement pipeline and MRR growth trajectory:
- Is the solo project lead model sustainable for another quarter?
- At what pipeline coverage level should a second project lead be added?
- What would a second project lead hire look like — full-time, contract, or vertical specialist?

The default answer for the first 2–3 quarters: focus on completing the first few engagements well before adding capacity. One botched engagement because of over-extension does more damage than slow growth.

### 5. Retrospective: what surprised us?

Three questions:
1. What happened this quarter that we didn't expect?
2. What decision do we wish we had made differently?
3. What's one thing we know now that we'd tell our 3-months-ago selves?

Write the answers down. They compound.

## Decision framework

Common decisions that come up. How to handle each without deliberating from scratch every time.

### "Should I take this engagement?" (qualification decision)

Use the discovery framework qualification criteria. If all "must have" boxes are checked: yes. If any "must have" is unchecked: no (or get the missing item before proceeding).

The only override: if you want to take a below-floor engagement for strategic reasons, explicitly name the reason in writing before signing. "Taking this at $15K because they're a prominent practice with high referral potential in the dental vertical, and I'm treating this as a first-vertical reference client." If you can't name a specific strategic reason in one sentence, you're rationalizing, not strategizing.

### "Should I agree to this scope change?" (change order decision)

Is it in the SOW? → Yes: do it, no discussion needed.
Is it clearly out of scope? → Write the change order, price it, get it signed. Don't do the work first.
Is it ambiguous? → Default to "out of scope, needs a change order." The cost of false ambiguity (doing unpaid work) is higher than the cost of having the change order conversation.

### "Should I agree to extend the timeline?" (timeline decision)

If the delay is Agency-caused: fix it, don't ask for permission to slip. Communicate what happened and when you'll be back on track.

If the delay is Client-caused: communicate it explicitly in writing within 24 hours of identifying it. Do not absorb client-caused delays silently — they create billing disputes and misaligned expectations at handoff.

If both parties want to extend for scope reasons: write a change order. A timeline extension without a change order is usually a scope change in disguise.

### "Should I offer the retainer?" (expansion decision)

Timing criteria (all must be true):
- Handoff complete and independent operation test passed
- No open bugs from the 30-day support window
- At least one metric from Layer 3 (client outcomes) is measurably positive
- Client has expressed satisfaction (post-handoff NPS ≥ 7)

If all criteria are met: offer at day 28 of the support window. Specific offer, not a vague "let's talk about ongoing engagement." Name the retainer tier, the scope, the monthly fee, and what improvement items are in the backlog.

If criteria are not met: don't offer. Fix the issues first.

### "Should I take a call from a prospect outside my active verticals?" (ICP decision)

Yes to the discovery call. No to the proposal if they're outside active verticals and:
- The workflow doesn't map cleanly to any of the three playbooks
- We'd be building vertical knowledge from scratch
- The engagement can't be scoped at Tier 1 minimum

The discovery call is free intelligence. Not every discovery call becomes a proposal.

## Communication standards

**Client-facing communications:**

- Status updates: sent by 5 PM Friday, every week of an active engagement — no exceptions
- Blocker notifications: within 24 hours of identifying the blocker, always in writing
- Spec deliveries: sent with a clear ask ("please review and respond by [date]") not "let me know what you think"
- Invoices: sent within 2 business days of a milestone being completed — not batched, not delayed

**Internal communications:**

- All engagement decisions that deviate from process (scope creep absorbed, floor broken, timeline extended without change order) are logged in the engagement folder with a one-sentence rationale. This creates accountability and teaches future-you.
- Playbook updates are not optional. They are deliverables of every engagement, due within 5 business days of handoff.

## What to do when things go wrong

### Client is unhappy with an output

First response: listen, don't defend. "Tell me specifically what you expected and what you got." Then:
- If it's a spec error (agent doing something the spec doesn't describe): fix it, no charge, no drama
- If it's a client expectation error (they expected something not in the spec): have the scope conversation, not a quality conversation
- If it's genuinely ambiguous: look at the spec together, agree on what was intended, fix accordingly

Do not let client dissatisfaction fester. Address it within 24 hours of hearing about it.

### An integration breaks in production

First: determine root cause. Is this a Veta build issue or a third-party change (API update, credential expiration, service outage)?

If Veta build issue: fix it, communicate timeline within 4 business hours, update the tool manifest with what broke and why.

If third-party issue: communicate within 4 business hours that the issue is a third-party change, what's happening, and what the fix timeline looks like. Do not bill for time spent fixing third-party breaks unless the investigation took more than 4 hours and is clearly out of the 30-day support window scope.

If it's a third-party break during the 30-day support window and will happen again: flag it in the engagement retrospective and price monitoring into any retainer for that integration.

### A project is running materially behind schedule

"Materially behind" = more than 2 weeks behind the committed milestone date.

Step 1: Write a frank assessment of what caused the delay and what's needed to recover. Be specific.
Step 2: Schedule a call with the client within 48 hours to communicate the delay directly — not via email, not buried in a status update.
Step 3: Propose a revised timeline with specific dates. Don't hedge with "hopefully" or "we're working on it."
Step 4: If the delay was caused by Agency, consider what (if anything) is appropriate to offer the client: a credit on the next milestone, expedited build on a specific feature, etc. Don't volunteer compensation reflexively — sometimes a frank conversation and a clear recovery plan is enough.
Step 5: Log the delay cause in the engagement folder. Update the relevant template (SOW, onboarding checklist, agent spec) if the root cause reveals a process gap.

### A client tries to terminate mid-engagement

Review the SOW termination clause. Confirm pro-rated payment is clearly defined.

Step 1: Get on a call within 24 hours to understand why. Sometimes a termination signal is actually an unhappiness signal that can be addressed.
Step 2: If the decision is final: confirm the termination in writing, confirm the pro-rated payment calculation, begin credential cleanup, deliver all completed work products.
Step 3: Do not fight it or withhold completed deliverables over a billing dispute. Disputes go to the invoice process, not a work stoppage.
Step 4: Debrief internally: what happened, what could have been caught earlier, what changes in the process.
