# Partnership and Referral Outreach

Partnerships are the highest-leverage organic acquisition channel at the stage Veta is at. A single relationship with a dental practice consultant who works with 30 practices per year is worth more than 18 months of SEO compounding. Partnerships also create warm inbound — a referral from a trusted advisor pre-qualifies the prospect and makes the discovery call easier.

This document covers the partnership categories for each active vertical, how to identify and approach specific targets, how to structure the relationship, and how to manage the pipeline.

No cold mass outreach. No purchased lists. No "spray and pray" email sequences. Every partnership is a person-to-person relationship built on a clear complementary value proposition.

---

## Partnership model

The core proposition to every partner category is the same:

> "You help practices with [strategy / operations / compliance / finances]. We build the technical systems that implement the operational changes you recommend. We're not competing with what you do — we're the implementation layer that makes your work stick. When a client of yours has a workflow automation need, we scope and build it. When a prospect of ours needs advisory help you provide, we refer them to you."

This works because most of the advisors and consultants in these verticals cannot build agent systems themselves, but they frequently see the operational problems that agent systems solve. The referral relationship is genuinely complementary — not "we need your leads."

## Partnership categories by vertical

### Specialty dental

**Category 1 — Practice management consultants**

Who they are: Independent consultants who help dental practices improve operations, hire staff, optimize scheduling, and grow revenue. Often former dental office managers or practice administrators. They typically work with 5–25 practices at a time on retainer.

Examples of firms to research: Dental Consulting Alliance, Scheduling Institute, Dental Intelligence consultants, Practice Café operations advisors, independent consultants who post on Dental Town and LinkedIn.

Why they refer: They identify the operational problems; they can't build the technical solution. A consultant who recommends "you need to automate your pre-auth process" needs to point the client somewhere. If they point to Veta, their recommendation has follow-through.

Relationship structure: Referral fee (10–15% of first-milestone payment) plus a reciprocal referral (when a prospect needs general practice consulting, Veta sends them the partner's way). More valuable than the referral fee: the partner gets to tell their clients they have a preferred technical implementation partner — it makes their advisory work more complete.

**Category 2 — Dental CPAs and financial advisors**

Who they are: CPAs and financial advisors who specialize in dental practices. They see the financials — they know which clients have high staff costs, revenue leakage, or declining production.

Why they refer: A financial advisor who sees that a client's pre-auth denial rate is costing $80K/year in uncollected revenue has a natural reason to introduce Veta. The fix improves the financial metrics they're paid to improve.

Relationship structure: No referral fee to CPAs in most states (ethics rules apply — confirm). Instead: a formal "strategic partner" relationship where the CPA can describe Veta as a recommended operational resource. The value to the CPA is that their clients get better operational results, which reflects well on the financial advisory relationship.

**Category 3 — Dental practice brokers and transition advisors**

Who they are: Advisors who help dentists buy, sell, or transition dental practices. They work with both sellers (practices that need to be "ready to sell") and buyers (new owners who need to modernize).

Why they refer: A practice being prepped for sale needs clean, efficient operations. A practice just acquired by a new owner often needs operational overhaul. Both moments are natural entry points for a Tier 1 engagement.

Relationship structure: Referral fee. The transaction context creates urgency — these clients have a reason to act now.

**Category 4 — Dental supply and equipment reps (Patterson, Schein, Benco)**

Who they are: Territory reps for major dental distributors who have relationships with dozens of practices in a geographic area.

Why they refer: They want to be the "go-to" resource for their practice relationships — referring Veta makes them more valuable. Reciprocal referral (new practices starting up often need a supply relationship) is meaningful to them.

Relationship structure: Informal referral partnership. No formal fee structure typically — they're employees, commissions from referral fees are problematic. Instead: a branded partner mention, Veta content distributed through their client newsletters, and genuine reciprocal referrals.

**Category 5 — Dental association chapters and study clubs**

Who they are: Local or regional chapters of the ADA, AGD, AAOMS, AAO, AAP. Study clubs are informal peer groups of dentists who meet monthly to discuss cases and business.

Why they're valuable: Speaking opportunity, membership in a vendor resource directory, and access to a concentrated group of decision-makers in the target vertical.

How to engage: Request to speak at a chapter meeting or study club on a relevant operational topic (not a sales pitch — a genuine educational session: "The Pre-Authorization Problem in Implant Dentistry: What's Changing and What You Can Do About It"). Offer to be listed in their vendor resources directory.

### Specialty legal

**Category 1 — Legal practice management consultants**

Who they are: Consultants who help law firms with operations, billing optimization, technology selection, and client experience. Often former law firm administrators or practice managers. ALA (Association of Legal Administrators) is the primary professional community.

Why they refer: They see the operational problems directly. A consultant who recommends "your intake process is losing you 30% of prospects" needs an implementation partner.

Relationship structure: Same as dental practice consultants — referral fee plus reciprocal referral.

**Category 2 — Legal malpractice insurance brokers**

Who they are: Insurance brokers who specialize in professional liability coverage for law firms. They directly advise on risk management — and missed deadlines, manual intake errors, and privilege leaks are exactly the risks Veta's systems reduce.

Why they refer: A firm with better docketing, cleaner intake, and more disciplined communication has a lower risk profile. Some brokers actively recommend risk management tools to clients. This is an underused channel in legal tech.

Relationship structure: Referral relationship, potentially with formal risk management certification ("Veta-built docketing systems are recommended by [broker] as a risk mitigation measure"). Valuable for both parties.

**Category 3 — Bar association technology committees**

Who they are: The technology or practice innovation committee of state or specialty bar associations (AILA for immigration, ACTEC for estate planning, INTA for IP). These committees produce technology guidance for member attorneys.

Why they're valuable: An endorsement or resource listing from a bar association technology committee has enormous credibility. It's also an opportunity to participate in CLE programming — speaking at an AILA tech panel on "Automating Immigration Document Assembly" reaches hundreds of immigration attorneys simultaneously.

How to engage: Join the committee as a vendor member (most allow it), contribute educational content to the committee's newsletter, request a speaking slot at the annual tech CLE.

**Category 4 — Law school incubators and solo/small firm programs**

Who they are: Law school programs that support new attorneys launching solo or small practices. Georgetown, NYU, Suffolk, and many others run these. The attorneys coming out of these programs are exactly the right size (1–5 people, high operational overhead relative to size) and often technically open-minded.

Why they're valuable: Early-stage client pipeline, word-of-mouth within the incubator cohort, and the prestige association of working with a law school program.

Relationship structure: Offer a discounted first engagement rate for incubator participants (not below floor, but at the low end of the Tier 1 range) in exchange for a case study and word-of-mouth within the cohort.

### Aesthetic medicine

**Category 1 — MedSpa and aesthetic medicine business consultants**

Who they are: Consultants who help MedSpas and aesthetic practices grow revenue, improve operations, and scale. Often come from a healthcare practice management or wellness industry background. Esthetic Immersion, Practice Building Champions, and independent consultants who are active in the MGMA aesthetics community.

Why they refer: Same model as dental — they identify the problem, they can't build the solution.

**Category 2 — Aesthetic medicine CME providers and training programs**

Who they are: Companies that provide injector training, business training, and continuing education to aesthetic medicine practitioners. Allergan Aesthetics (Botox, Juvederm), Galderma (Restylane, Dysport), and independent training academies. These providers have direct relationships with thousands of practitioners.

Why they're valuable: Distribution into a concentrated, highly relevant audience. A mention in an Allergan training program ("here's a resource for automating your consultation follow-up process") reaches exactly the right people.

How to engage: Approach the business development or practice development team at the pharmaceutical company's aesthetics division. Offer to contribute an educational module on practice efficiency. This takes time but has high leverage once established.

**Category 3 — MedSpa industry associations**

Who they are: American Med Spa Association (AmSpa), Medical Aesthetics Network, Spa Industry Association.

Why they're valuable: Vendor member status, resource directory listing, annual conference speaking opportunities, newsletter distribution.

How to engage: Join as a vendor/partner member, contribute educational content, apply for conference speaking.

**Category 4 — EMR/software sales reps (Nextech, Aesthetic Record)**

Who they are: Account executives and customer success managers at the major aesthetic medicine software companies. They work with the practices we target.

Why they refer: Similar to dental supply reps — they want to be a complete resource for their clients. A Nextech AE who can say "and if you want to build automation on top of this, I know who to call" is more valuable to their clients.

Relationship structure: Informal. Most of these reps can't accept referral fees. The relationship is built on reciprocal value — when a Veta client needs a new EHR, the Nextech rep is the referral.

## Outreach process

### Identifying targets

Before reaching out to anyone in a partner category, build a short list:

1. Name 5–10 specific individuals per category per vertical (not just "dental consultants" — actual named people)
2. Research each person: LinkedIn, their website or firm website, any content they've published, any mutual connections
3. Prioritize by: (a) geographic overlap with your target client market, (b) evident alignment (they talk about the problems we solve), (c) mutual connections who can make an introduction

**Warm introductions are 5x more effective than cold outreach.** Before sending a cold email, check LinkedIn for any second-degree connection who could introduce you. A message that says "I was introduced by [name]" opens doors that a cold email doesn't.

### First contact

The first message is not a pitch. It's a specific, relevant observation about their work that earns a reply.

**Cold email format (under 150 words):**

> Subject: [Their specific area of focus] + something specific
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> I've been following your work on [specific thing they've published or posted about — be specific, not generic]. 
>
> I run a vertical agency that builds agent systems for [their vertical] — specifically, the operational clusters that cost practices the most time: [name the 1–2 most relevant clusters for this person's focus].
>
> I think there might be a natural complement between what you advise practices on and what we build. Worth a 20-minute call to see if that's the case?
>
> [Name]

What makes this work: it's specific (mentions their actual work), it's honest about what we do, it proposes a low-commitment next step, and it doesn't ask for anything except a call.

What doesn't work: long descriptions of the agency, feature lists, generic "I'd love to connect" language, asking for a referral in the first message.

### Follow-up cadence

| Step | Timing | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Initial outreach | Day 0 | Send first message |
| Follow-up 1 | Day 7 | Short follow-up — one sentence adding a new observation or sharing a relevant piece of content |
| Follow-up 2 | Day 21 | Final follow-up — "Last note on this — if the timing isn't right, no problem. Here's a piece we published that might be useful regardless: [link]." |
| Archive | Day 22+ | Move to dormant, revisit in 6 months |

Two follow-ups maximum. No exceptions. Persistence beyond two follow-ups is spam.

### The partner call

When someone agrees to a 20-minute call:

1. Spend 10 minutes learning about their practice (not pitching): who they work with, what the most common operational problem they see is, what they're currently recommending for the automation/efficiency gap
2. Spend 5 minutes describing Veta specifically — not generically. Reference what they just told you: "Based on what you described, the cluster we'd most likely address for your practices is [X]."
3. Spend 5 minutes on the referral model: "The way we work with advisors: when one of your clients needs the implementation layer for what you're recommending, we scope it and build it. You get a referral fee of [X%] on the first milestone. We'll also refer clients who need [your specific advisory service] to you. Does that model make sense?"

Don't overcomplicate the referral structure on the call. Agree on the principle; document the details in a short partner agreement afterward.

### Partner agreement

A one-page document (not a legal contract — a working agreement). Covers:

- Referral fee: % of first-milestone payment, paid within 30 days of milestone receipt
- Reciprocal referral: yes/no and which specific services
- Disclosure: partner agrees to disclose the referral relationship to referred clients (required in some professional contexts — lawyers and CPAs especially)
- Duration: 12 months, auto-renewing, terminable by either party with 30 days notice
- No exclusivity: partner is not required to refer only to Veta; Veta is not required to refer only to the partner

## Referral tracking and payment

When a referred client signs an SOW:
1. Note the referring partner in the engagement folder
2. Issue the referral fee payment within 30 days of the first-milestone payment being received
3. Notify the partner that the referral converted and the fee is being processed — they should hear from Veta before they hear from the client
4. After the engagement completes, send the partner a brief (2-sentence) update: the client is live, it went well. Partners who refer clients want to know the referral was handled well.

Do not pay referral fees in exchange for endorsements, reviews, or testimonials. The fee is for the referral, not for what the partner says about us.
