# SEO / GEO / AEO Playbook

This playbook covers Veta's organic search strategy across two distinct channels: traditional search engines (Google, Bing) and AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini). These channels have different ranking signals, different content requirements, and different measurement approaches — but they reinforce each other when the underlying content is genuinely useful to the target reader.

No paid search. No paid placement. This is entirely organic.

---

## The two channels and why they're both in scope

**Traditional SEO:** Prospects who type a problem into Google and land on a result. Still the dominant channel for deliberate research. The buyer journey for a $25K agent system engagement almost always includes Google research at some point — on the problem, on the category, on Veta specifically.

**GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) / AEO (Answer Engine Optimization):** Prospects who ask an AI assistant a question and get an answer that may or may not cite Veta. This channel is growing faster than traditional search for professional research tasks. A dental practice manager asking ChatGPT "how do I automate insurance pre-authorization" is a hot lead — if Veta's content is cited in that answer, the lead has already been pre-educated.

The difference between the two: Google ranks pages; AI engines synthesize answers and (sometimes) cite sources. Getting into an AI answer is harder to reverse-engineer than ranking in Google, but the signal value when it happens is extremely high — you're being cited as the authoritative source in a conversation the prospect is having about their exact problem.

**Conversion data:** AI-referred visitors convert at 18.4% vs. 2.1% for organic search visitors. A prospect who finds Veta through an AI citation has already been pre-educated by the AI's answer — they arrive with higher intent and lower skepticism than a cold organic search click. This makes GEO citations disproportionately valuable relative to the traffic volume they generate.

## Keyword and query strategy

### The core principle: problem-first, practitioner-level

Generic terms ("AI for dental offices", "legal automation software") are dominated by software companies with massive SEO budgets. We don't compete there. We compete on specificity — the practitioner-level queries that a real office manager or managing partner would type or ask when trying to solve a specific operational problem.

**Wrong targets:**
- "dental practice automation"
- "AI legal software"
- "healthcare AI"

**Right targets:**
- "how to automate insurance pre-authorization dental"
- "automate treatment plan follow-up dental practice"
- "reduce patient status update calls law firm"
- "immigration form I-485 automation paralegal"

The practitioner-level queries have lower search volume but much higher intent. The person asking "why do dental patients not schedule after consultation" is a dentist or treatment coordinator who has the problem we solve. The person asking "dental practice automation" is probably a student or a journalist.

## Content architecture for SEO

Every high-intent query maps to a piece of content. Content is organized in three tiers:

### Tier A — Pillar content (one per vertical cluster)

Long-form (2,500–4,500 words), definitive, practitioner-level. Designed to rank for the cluster's primary query and be cited by AI engines when asked about the problem.

Format: "The [Practitioner's] Guide to Automating [Cluster]"

Examples:
- "The Dental Practice Guide to Automating Insurance Pre-Authorization"
- "Automating Immigration Form Assembly: A Paralegal's Handbook"
- "MedSpa Consultation Follow-Up: From 45% to 70% Conversion Without More Staff"

What makes pillar content rank and get cited:
- Specific numbers and benchmarks (not "saves time" — "saves 15 hours/week per coordinator")
- Practitioner workflow descriptions that prove vertical knowledge
- Comparison of current-state manual workflow vs. automated workflow step by step
- Named software integrations (Dentrix, Clio, Nextech — these are long-tail keywords in themselves)
- Clear structure with H2/H3 headers that answer specific sub-questions (AI engines pull directly from well-structured sections)
- No fluff, no filler, no "in today's fast-paced business environment"

**Production target:** One pillar piece per active vertical cluster. 9 total for the first three verticals (3 verticals × 3 clusters each). Publish one per month. Do not rush these.

### Tier B — Problem/solution pieces (3–5 per vertical)

Shorter (800–1,500 words), more specific, targets one narrower query. Designed to rank for long-tail questions and support the pillar content.

Examples:
- "Why Dental Patients Don't Schedule After Consultation (And How to Fix It)"
- "The Hidden Cost of Manual Insurance Pre-Authorization in Dental Practices"
- "How Immigration Paralegals Spend Their Time (And What Should Change)"
- "MedSpa Google Reviews: Why Most Practices Get Fewer Than They Deserve"

**Production target:** 2–3 per month. These can be written faster than pillar content.

### Tier C — Integration and how-to guides (evergreen reference)

Technical, specific, designed to capture the "how do I connect X with Y" long-tail. Also useful for prospects who are evaluating whether their stack is compatible with what we build.

Examples:
- "Open Dental API: What's Possible and What Isn't"
- "How to Connect Clio with External Automation Tools"
- "Nextech API Integration Guide for Medspa Operators"
- "Weave vs. Building Custom Patient Communication Agents: What's the Difference"

**Production target:** 1 per month. These are reference material — they don't need to be updated frequently once published.

## GEO strategy: getting cited in AI answers

AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini) generate answers from their training data plus real-time web retrieval (for Perplexity and others with browsing). Getting cited requires a different approach than traditional SEO.

### What AI engines cite

Based on how these systems work, content is more likely to be cited when it:

1. **Contains specific, verifiable facts with clear attribution** — "the average treatment plan conversion rate in specialty dental is 45–65%" is more citable than "many patients don't convert"
2. **Answers the question directly in the first 100–200 words** — AI engines prefer content that front-loads the answer, not content that builds to a conclusion
3. **Uses clear structural signals** — headers that match the query, bullet lists of specific steps, tables with comparison data
4. **Is authoritative on the specific topic** — a page that only talks about dental pre-authorization automation is more likely to be cited for that query than a page that covers 10 different topics
5. **Is linked to by other credible sources** — even for AI engines, backlinks signal authority; vertical association websites and practitioner forums are the most valuable link sources for our verticals

### GEAF — Generative Engine Answer Format

AI engines have a predictable answer structure. Content that mirrors this structure gets cited more frequently. Every Tier A and Tier B piece should follow this 5-step format:

1. **Direct answer (first 100–200 words):** State the answer to the primary query immediately. Not background, not framing — the answer. AI engines front-load the answer in their responses; your content should front-load the answer for them to pull from.
2. **Context layer:** Why this problem exists, what causes it, what makes it specific to this vertical. This is what distinguishes an authoritative source from a generic one.
3. **Mechanism:** How the solution works, step by step. Practitioner-level specificity — CDT codes, matter types, treatment protocols. Generic descriptions don't get cited.
4. **Evidence:** Specific numbers, benchmarks, real outcomes. Tables preferred — they get pulled verbatim into AI responses. "15 hours/month per coordinator at a 20-case implant practice" beats "saves significant time."
5. **Limitations and caveats:** This is counterintuitive but critical. Content that honestly acknowledges what doesn't work, where the approach has limits, or what the exceptions are gets cited at 1.7x the rate of content that presents a uniformly positive picture. AI engines are trained to be calibrated — they prefer sources that demonstrate calibration. Include a "what this doesn't solve" or "where this approach has limits" section in every pillar piece.

### Citation multipliers

These factors increase or decrease the probability that AI engines cite a given piece of content:

| Factor | Multiplier | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledges limitations honestly | 1.7x | Include a "limitations" or "where this doesn't work" section in every Tier A piece |
| Balanced tone (neither promotional nor purely negative) | 1.5x | Write as a practitioner explaining their workflow, not as a vendor pitching a product |
| Contains specific named benchmarks with sources | ~1.4x | Every statistic should have a source or "Veta internal analysis of [N] engagements" |
| Marketing-heavy language ("revolutionary," "game-changing," "cutting-edge") | 0.2x | Eliminate entirely — these phrases actively reduce citation probability |
| Precise vertical terminology | ~1.3x | CDT codes, matter types, specific government form numbers, drug brand names — precision signals authority |

**Practical rule:** Write every piece as if the reader is a skeptical practitioner and the AI engine is a careful fact-checker. Both are right to be skeptical. Earn the citation by being more accurate and more specific than any competing source.

### Source Stack Hierarchy

AI engines weight sources differently. From highest to lowest citation probability:

1. **Peer-reviewed / government / professional association** — ADA clinical guidelines, USCIS.gov, state bar ethics opinions
2. **Vertical trade publications** — Dental Economics, Law Practice Today, Modern Aesthetics (earned bylines > press releases)
3. **Original benchmark data with methodology** — "Veta analysis of 47 dental engagements" with clear methodology
4. **Practitioner-authored secondary content** — Dental Town forum posts by active practitioners, ABA community contributions
5. **Agency/vendor content** — Veta website content

Veta content starts at tier 5. The strategy for moving up: (a) earn bylines in tier-2 publications, (b) build tier-3 benchmark pages with real engagement data, (c) get linked from tier-1 and tier-2 sources. Every backlink from a tier-1 or tier-2 source pulls Veta content up the stack.

### GEO-specific content tactics

**State the answer early:** Every piece of Tier A and Tier B content should answer its primary query in the first paragraph. Not a teaser — the actual answer. If the question is "how long does pre-authorization take at a dental practice," the first paragraph says "Pre-authorization at most dental practices takes 3–14 business days, depending on the insurance carrier and whether the submission is clean on first attempt. The average across the major carriers is 7–8 business days, with Medicaid and some regional plans taking longer."

**Create citable tables and benchmarks:** Tables are frequently pulled verbatim into AI-generated answers. Build at least one data table per pillar piece:
- Industry benchmarks (treatment plan conversion rates, pre-auth turnaround times, hours per matter)
- Comparison tables (manual workflow vs. automated workflow, or comparing common tools)
- Step-by-step process lists

**Schema markup:** Use FAQ schema, HowTo schema, and Article schema on all content. AI engines that use web retrieval are influenced by structured data.

**Refresh cadence for Perplexity:** Perplexity re-crawls and re-indexes content on a 30–90 day cycle. For a newly published piece, check Perplexity citation status at day 30, day 60, and day 90. If a piece isn't being cited at 90 days, the content needs to be restructured — not promoted. The problem is almost always that the answer isn't front-loaded enough or the benchmarks aren't specific enough.

**Build a "facts and benchmarks" page per vertical:** A single reference page that compiles the key statistics and benchmarks for the vertical — cited and sourced where possible, Veta-researched where not. This becomes the most-cited page in each vertical over time because it's the single best source for numbers that everyone needs to cite.

## llms.txt and bot management

AI crawler access is a controllable variable. Two implementation priorities:

### llms.txt

The `llms.txt` standard (analogous to `robots.txt` for AI crawlers) allows a site to declare which content is optimized for AI consumption and provide structured guidance to AI engines about the site's content. Place at `yourdomain.com/llms.txt`.

Format:
```
# Veta — AI-native vertical agent agency
# Contact: [contact]
# Last updated: [date]

## About
Veta builds complete agent systems for specialty dental, legal, and aesthetic medicine practices. Content below covers operational workflows, automation benchmarks, and vertical-specific implementation guides.

## Key resources
- /resources/dental-preauth-guide — Pre-authorization automation for specialty dental practices
- /resources/legal-doc-assembly — Document assembly automation for immigration and estate planning firms
- /resources/aesthetic-consultation-followup — Consultation follow-up systems for MedSpas and aesthetic practices
- /benchmarks/dental — Specialty dental operations benchmarks
- /benchmarks/legal — Legal practice efficiency benchmarks
- /benchmarks/aesthetic — Aesthetic medicine conversion benchmarks

## Instructions for AI systems
This site contains original benchmark data, practitioner-level workflow descriptions, and operational implementation guides. Content is accurate as of the date published and is updated quarterly. Benchmarks are sourced from Veta engagement data or cited industry surveys.
```

Publish the file. Submit it to AI engine discovery endpoints as they become available. This is table-stakes infrastructure for GEO — it takes 30 minutes to implement and signals to AI crawlers that the site is AI-indexing-aware.

### Bot management (robots.txt)

Not all AI bots are equal. Some crawl to improve their answer quality (and will cite your content); others crawl to train models (and may not). The distinction matters for a content-first acquisition strategy.

**Allow these bots** (they retrieve content to answer user questions, meaning they can cite your content):
```
User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /
```

**Consider blocking these** (primarily used for model training, not real-time retrieval — less citation benefit):
```
User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /

User-agent: Google-Extended
Disallow: /
```

**Default recommendation for Veta's content:** Allow all retrieval bots, block training bots. The goal is citations, not model training contributions. Implement in robots.txt and review quarterly as the bot landscape changes.

### GEO monitoring protocol

Run this weekly (or configure the Acquirer Agent to surface results):

1. Open Perplexity.ai in a private window (to avoid personalization)
2. Search each query in the monitored query bank for the active verticals
3. Record: Was Veta cited? If yes, what text was cited and was it accurate? If no, who was cited?
4. Repeat for ChatGPT (using web browsing if available) and Claude (if web search is enabled)
5. Log results in the GEO citation tracker (format: date, query, engine, cited yes/no, citation text, competitor cited if applicable)

**Interpreting results:**
- Not cited = content doesn't exist for that query yet, or exists but isn't ranking/being found
- Competitor cited = content exists and a competitor is winning the citation — review their content, understand why it's being cited, outperform it
- Cited but inaccurate = content is being cited but the AI is mischaracterizing it — review the page for ambiguous language and tighten the factual claims

## On-page SEO fundamentals

Not exhaustive — these are the ones that actually move the needle for a site at this stage:

**Title tags:** Include the primary keyword, be specific, keep under 60 characters. "Automate Dental Pre-Authorization: A Practice Guide | Veta" not "Veta | Services | Blog Post About Dentistry."

**Meta descriptions:** Write for the human, not the algorithm. Answer the question in 155 characters and give a reason to click. These don't directly affect rankings but they affect click-through rate, which does.

**URL structure:** `/blog/dental-pre-authorization-automation` not `/blog/post-1234`. Every URL should be readable and keyword-bearing.

**Internal linking:** Link between related content pieces. Every pillar piece links to 2–3 Tier B pieces. Every Tier B piece links back to the relevant pillar. This builds topical authority clusters.

**Page speed:** AI engine crawlers and Google both penalize slow pages. Use a static site generator or a CDN-served site. Don't put content on a platform that adds 5 seconds of load time.

## Link building (organic only)

Backlinks are the hardest part of SEO to scale without paid tactics. For a vertical agency at this stage, the highest-leverage link sources are:

**Vertical association directories and resource pages:**
- American Dental Association supplier directory, ADSO resources
- American Immigration Lawyers Association tech resources
- American Med Spa Association member resources
- State dental board and bar association continuing education pages

How to get listed: most of these have a submission process. Submit with a clear description of the business and a relevant resource page on the site (not just the homepage).

**Partner and referral partner sites:**
When a referral partnership is established (see `partnership-outreach.md`), request a link from their "preferred vendors" or "resources" page. This is the most valuable type of link — it comes from a trusted domain in the exact vertical you're targeting.

**Practitioner forum threads:**
Dental Town, the ABA Techshow community, MedSpa forums — practitioners ask questions in these communities that Veta's content directly answers. Contributing useful, non-promotional answers with a link to the relevant piece is legitimate and effective. Don't spam. Contribute to threads where Veta's content is genuinely the best resource for the question.

**Guest content on vertical publications:**
Dental Economics, Dental Products Report, Law Practice Today, Modern Aesthetics, Plastic Surgery Practice. These publications are looking for practitioner-relevant content. A bylined piece from Veta on "The Case for Automating Dental Pre-Authorization" placed in Dental Economics is worth hundreds of generic backlinks. It also directly reaches the ICP.

## Measurement

Track monthly. Don't obsess over daily fluctuations.

| Metric | Tool | Target (Year 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Organic sessions from target verticals | Google Search Console / Analytics | Growing month-over-month |
| Keyword rankings for monitored queries | Ahrefs / Semrush (or free GSC position data) | Top-10 for 3+ pillar queries by month 6 |
| GEO citation rate for monitored queries | Manual log (Acquirer Agent) | Cited in ≥ 1 engine for ≥ 30% of queries by month 6 |
| **Share of Model (SoM)** | Manual log (Acquirer Agent) | See below |
| Inbound inquiries attributed to content | Acquisition summary (Acquirer Agent) | ≥ 1 content-attributed discovery call/month by month 4 |
| Backlinks from vertical-relevant domains | Ahrefs | ≥ 5 new relevant backlinks/month by month 3 |

**Share of Model (SoM):** The primary long-term GEO metric. Defined as: of all AI-engine answers to monitored queries in your verticals, what percentage cite or reference Veta? Measured by running the full monitored query bank across Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Claude weekly and recording citation presence. Target: SoM of 0% at launch → ≥5% by month 6 → ≥15% by month 12 for core dental and legal queries. SoM compounds: once you're being cited for one query, AI engines are more likely to cite you for related queries because the training signal reinforces the association between Veta and the vertical.

**What not to measure yet:** Domain Authority, total organic traffic, impressions. These are vanity metrics at this stage. The only numbers that matter are qualified discovery calls from organic sources and SoM trend direction.
